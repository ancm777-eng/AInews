Google Launches Gemini 4 Neon and Orchestrator (Neon) for High-Frequency Enterprise Coding

1. Executive Summary

Enterprise coding and analytical agent pipelines face an acute "reasoning tax," where frontier accuracy arrives only with slow turnarounds and high token bills. On November 18, 2026, Google DeepMind released Gemini 4 Neon (gemini-4-neon)—its fourth Gemini 4 model in seven weeks—alongside Orchestrator (Neon) inside its Antigravity IDE, pairing Neon as planner with lightweight Gemini 4 Hydrogen as parallel code executor.

Priced at $3.00 per million input tokens ($0.15 cached, a 95% discount) and $15.00 per million output tokens with a 1M-token context window, Neon scores 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (#3 overall) and 58.6–59 (#1) on the Coding Agent Index at $6.86 per task—undercutting Claude Opus 5.5 ($12.61) and GPT-6.2 Astra ($8/$40).

2. Architecture & Performance

Rather than routing every agentic turn through a single heavyweight model, Orchestrator (Neon) embeds hierarchical multi-model scheduling directly into developer workflows. Neon handles repository-wide planning, architectural review, and context compaction, while delegating rapid file edits and test loops to Gemini 4 Hydrogen ($1.50/$7.50 per 1M tokens) to curb redundant file reads across extended tool-use sessions.

Context, Modalities & Throughput: Built with a 1,048,576-token shared input window (text, image, video, audio, PDF) and a 65,536-token default output cap extendable via long-decode mode, Neon offers four reasoning levels (low, medium, high default, xhigh) plus 50% Batch/Flex discounts. At xhigh, it streams at ~110 tok/s—nearly 1.9× faster than Gemini 4 Carbon at high (~59 tok/s)—and encourages deep prefix KV-cache reuse at $0.15/M cached input.

Pika Unveils Pika Enact for Sub-Second Causal Streaming Video Direction

1. Executive Summary

Offline generative video traps filmmakers and VFX artists in a slow "slot-machine" loop of writing prompts, waiting for fixed clips to render, and regenerating flawed blocking. On November 18, 2026, Pika unveiled Pika Enact, an experimental research preview built atop open-weight Pika H3 Flex (distilled to 6 steps from MiniMax H3) that converts video synthesis into a live, continuous directing environment.

Streaming synchronized video and audio in 0.708 s causal chunks (0.17 s in low-latency mode) at 1.5× to 1.8× real-time speed, Enact supports live dragging (Live Staging in <0.4 s on 1× H100), voice/text notes (Live Actor Direction), and untextured Blender viewport steering (Live Camera Control at 640×480, 24 fps on 8× NVIDIA B300).

2. Architecture & Performance

Architecturally, Pika Enact transforms a bidirectional offline diffusion transformer into a stateful causal stream. Built on Pika H3 Flex—which uses self-forcing distribution matching distillation to compress ~50-step MiniMax H3 sampling down to 6 denoising steps (~8.3× fewer steps)—Enact incorporates a sliding memory window, static appearance caching, and a streaming latent decoder trained on synthetic physics and automated Blender camera/drag trajectories.

Creators intervene mid-stream across three specialized control modes: Live Staging conditions on 24 fps mouse coordinates and grab heatmaps to redraw lighting and occluded backgrounds dynamically; Live Actor Direction accepts typed or spoken cues with persistent character voices; and Live Camera Control ingests untextured 3D Blender viewports directly for virtual previsualization.

Q.ANT Demonstrates Hybrid Optical-GPU LLM Inference on NPU Gen 2+ Over PCIe

1. Executive Summary

Quadratic self-attention scaling constrains long-context LLM inference behind HBM capacity and chip power walls. At SC26 in Chicago, German photonic computing firm Q.ANT unveiled its NPU Gen 2+ Native Processing Unit and demonstrated live hybrid LLM inference pairing the optical co-processor with an AMD Instinct MI210 GPU over a standard PCIe server bus.

Fabricated on a Thin-Film Lithium Niobate Photonic Integrated Circuit (TFLN PIC) via Q.ANT's Stuttgart pilot line with IMS CHIPS, NPU Gen 2+ adds native single-pass optical Discrete Fourier Transforms (DFTs) to Gen 2 primitives—replacing an attention layer in an unnamed ~1B-parameter model via light propagation—with commercial hybrid racks targeted for H2 2027.

Key Hardware & Packaging Concepts

  • TFLN PIC (Thin-Film Lithium Niobate Photonic Integrated Circuit): An electro-optic chip platform whose waveguides modulate laser light at high speeds to execute analog linear math with minimal waveguide resistive loss.
  • Optical DFT (Discrete Fourier Transform): A passive light-interference operation that mixes sequence tokens globally in one pass—acting as a structural replacement rather than an exact accelerator for quadratic transformer self-attention.
  • DAC / ADC & Modulator Overhead: The electrical-to-optical and optical-to-electrical conversion stages bridging digital GPU HBM across PCIe to analog optical waveguides, often dominating rack-level power draw.

2. Architecture & Performance

Rather than pitching photonics as a standalone GPU replacement, Q.ANT embeds its TFLN PIC co-processor into standard air-cooled PCIe server chassis, building on earlier Gen 2 deployments at Germany's Leibniz Supercomputing Centre (LRZ) and Jülich (JSC). In the SC26 demo, an AMD Instinct MI210 handles standard transformer layers while NPU Gen 2+ executes a single-pass optical DFT token-mixing layer via light interference.

Tracing the physical chain exposes both promise and friction: Algorithm (Fourier substitution) flows to the Optical Die (passive TFLN waveguides), across the Board/Bus (PCIe Gen4/Gen5 linking GPU HBM through DAC/ADC converters and modulators), into an Air-Cooled Rack (avoiding bespoke free-space optical chassis like Lumai). Q.ANT also updated its open-source C/C++ and Python SDK with DFT support and a CPU simulator.

3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

While co-packaged optics (CPO) address near-term networking bottlenecks, photonic compute remains pre-commercial and requires system-level proof before challenging digital accelerators.

  • Zero Production Telemetry Published: Despite demonstrating live PCIe co-execution, Q.ANT withheld tokens-per-second throughput, end-to-end latency, total wall-plug wattage, and perplexity scores for the ~1B-parameter SC26 demo.
  • Architectural & Causal Masking Risk: Replacing self-attention with global Fourier transforms alters model mechanics—requiring proof that causal masking holds and accuracy scales to 70B+ models without costly retraining.
  • Electro-Optical Conversion Tax: Low waveguide loss does not ensure rack-level savings once laser sources, high-speed modulators, DAC/ADC converters, and PCIe host transfers are totaled; using an off-the-shelf MI210 does not signal formal AMD adoption.

Photonic Co-Processing Needs Proof

Q.ANT's NPU Gen 2+ proves PCIe optical-GPU DFT offloading alongside an AMD MI210, but H2 2027 commercial viability depends on 70B+ perplexity, causal masking, and full DAC/ADC wall-plug power.
