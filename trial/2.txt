### 2. Sanders and Casar Introduce "Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act" Imposing Moratorium and 20-Year Criminal Penalties (Category 5)

**Source:** U.S. Senate Press / Associated Press / Common Dreams / Gizmodo  
**Event Date:** 2026-09-23, 12:56 PM EDT / 2026-09-24, 01:56 KST  
**Source Date:** 2026-09-23

**Primary URL:** https://www.sanders.senate.gov/press-releases/sanders-casar-introduce-legislation-to-create-new-federal-agency-to-ban-artificial-superintelligence-pause-advanced-ai-development/  

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? For several years, national artificial intelligence governance focused on voluntary safety commitments, civil liability guidance, and state-level disclosure rules. However, as frontier models demonstrate early capabilities in automated vulnerability discovery, multi-agent planning, and algorithmic self-modification, the regulatory debate has shifted toward existential risk and non-proliferation legal mechanisms. Applying statutory doctrines historically reserved for nuclear weapons proliferation to frontier machine learning research creates severe operational and legal boundaries for hyperscale compute facilities and foundation model developers.

On September 23, 2026, at 12:56 PM EDT (September 24, 2026, 01:56 KST), Senator Bernie Sanders (I-VT) in the U.S. Senate and Representative Greg Casar (D-TX) in the U.S. House of Representatives officially introduced companion legislation titled the **Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act**. The legislation establishes a permanent statutory prohibition on the development and deployment of artificial superintelligence (ASI), defined as any system that exceeds human cognitive performance across most domains or possesses capabilities that could disempower humanity or overthrow the federal government. To enforce this ban, the bill imposes an immediate mandatory pause on advanced frontier model training runs exceeding compute or data thresholds until comprehensive federal safety standards are codified. The bill creates a cabinet-level **Department of Artificial Intelligence**, advised by an independent scientific advisory board, granting it statutory oversight to monitor frontier training runs, order the removal of dangerous capabilities (such as automated cyber offensive execution and biochemical synthesis), and **supervise the physical destruction of illicit superintelligence weights**. Penalties for non-compliance are explicitly aligned with federal statutes governing the unlawful development of nuclear weapons: individuals face **up to 20 years in federal prison**, while corporate entities face the **"corporate death penalty" (statutory revocation of corporate charters and forced dissolution)**. In accompanying statements, Representative Casar emphasized that the bill specifically targets unconstrained **recursive self-improvement (RSI)**.

### 4. Synopsys and TSMC Deliver A14 Certified EDA, CoWoS IVR Power Integrity, and TSMC-COUPE Silicon Photonics (Category 6)

**Source:** Synopsys / TSMC / PR Newswire / Unite.AI  
**Event Date:** 2026-09-23, 12:01 PM PDT / 2026-09-24, 04:01 KST  
**Source Date:** 2026-09-23  
**Status:** Official Semiconductor Design Enablement & Silicon IP Milestone  
**Primary URL:** https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/synopsys-and-tsmc-partner-to-accelerate-ai-systems-innovation-with-agentic-ai-and-advanced-design-302256789.html  

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? AI accelerators are encountering physical design limits across multiple engineering domains: transistor scaling, chip-to-chip interconnect bandwidth, and Power Delivery Networks (PDN). At advanced 1.4nm-class nodes, processor supply voltages drop below 0.75V while total accelerator package power exceeds 1,000 Watts, driving core rail currents beyond 1,500 Amperes. When an AI compute die transitions rapidly from idle to full tensor execution, the high rate of current change (di/dt) induces severe transient voltage droop across parasitic loop inductances, risking timing violations or logic faults. Resolving this power wall requires shifting voltage regulation from distant motherboard voltage regulator modules (VRMs) directly onto the multi-die packaging substrate via Integrated Voltage Regulators (IVR). Simultaneously, scaling I/O bandwidth demands co-packaged silicon photonics engines, supported by unified multiphysics EDA flows spanning advanced front-end silicon through 2.5D/3D packaging.

On September 23, 2026, at 12:01 PM PDT (September 24, 2026, 04:01 KST) at the TSMC North America Open Innovation Platform (OIP) Ecosystem Forum, **Synopsys, Inc. (NASDAQ: SNPS)** and **TSMC (NYSE: TSM)** announced an extensive design enablement collaboration targeting AI and high-performance computing (HPC) systems. The announcements establish key physical design milestones:
1. **TSMC A14 Process Certification:** Synopsys achieved full certification for its digital and analog EDA design flows on TSMC's A14 (1.4nm-class Gate-All-Around nanosheet) process, including automated device routing for analog layouts to accelerate advanced SerDes design.
2. **Agentic AI Engineering Workflows:** Deployment of collaborative agentic AI workflows within the EDA environment to automate floorplanning, placement, and timing closure across multi-die systems.
3. **Integrated Voltage Regulator (IVR) Multi-Die CoWoS Enablement:** To address severe power delivery constraints, Synopsys 3DIC Compiler established certified design and multiphysics signoff flows for **Integrated Voltage Regulators (IVR)** integrated directly into **TSMC CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate)** packages. Supplying intermediate intermediate bus voltages to on-package IVRs reduces board-level resistive losses (I squared times R) and positions regulators adjacent to compute dies to minimize parasitic loop inductance.
4. **TSMC-COUPE Co-Packaged Optics Co-Design:** Joint enablement for **TSMC-COUPE (Compact Universal Photonic Engine)**, supporting the heterogeneous 3D integration of Electronic ICs (EIC) and Photonic ICs (PIC) for high-density co-packaged optics (CPO).
5. **Silicon-Proven Interface IP Portfolio:** Demonstrated silicon-proven interface IP and tape-out milestones for **PCIe 7.0 operating at 128 GT/s PAM4** on 2nm nodes, alongside **HBM4 multi-die IP supporting 2048-bit wide base-die interfaces**.

System-level electrical, thermal, and packaging co-design implications:
1. **IVR Power Delivery Network (PDN) and di/dt Transient Mitigation:** In conventional motherboard power delivery, VRMs sit inches away from the processor package. The parasitic loop inductance (L) of PCB planes, socket BGA balls, and package substrate traces induces severe inductive voltage drop during rapid current steps, governed by V = L * (di/dt). For example, at a transient current step of 1,500A occurring within 10 nanoseconds (di/dt = 1.5 * 10^11 A/s), a parasitic loop inductance of 50 picohenries would theoretically induce an unmanageable voltage spike or droop of V = (50 * 10^-12 H) * (1.5 * 10^11 A/s) = 7.5 Volts across a 0.75V rail, which board decoupling capacitors cannot fully suppress. By enabling IVR integration directly on the CoWoS interposer assembly, the physical distance between the regulator and the compute load is reduced to millimeters, cutting loop inductance and providing nanosecond-scale transient response.
2. **Multiphysics Thermal and Mechanical Signoff:** Heterogeneous packages combining 1.4nm-class logic chiplets, multiple HBM4 stacks, and IVRs experience non-uniform thermal dissipation and mechanical stress. Mismatches in coefficients of thermal expansion (CTE) between silicon chiplets (~2.6 ppm/°C) and organic package substrates (~14 ppm/°C) induce package warpage and micro-bump strain. Synopsys’ certified multiphysics flow performs coupled electro-thermal and mechanical co-simulation to ensure structural and electrical integrity.
3. **TSMC-COUPE 3D Electro-Photonic Integration:** COUPE integrates the electrical driver/TIA die directly onto the silicon photonics die using 3D wafer-to-wafer hybrid bonding. This eliminates parasitic wire-bond and micro-bump inductances, reducing optical interconnect energy per bit and enabling optical scale-out links at 200G to 400G per lane.

#### [Critical View]
While integrating voltage regulators (IVR) onto the CoWoS interposer reduces power delivery loop inductance, it introduces concentrated thermal dissipation challenges. Switching regulators exhibit conversion losses, dissipating heat directly adjacent to compute dies that already operate at high thermal flux densities. Without advanced direct-to-die liquid cooling cold plates, the localized thermal dissipation from IVR components can create severe hot spots and accelerate thermal runaway. Furthermore, the adoption timeline for High-NA EUV (0.55 NA) lithography remains dependent on foundry process economics: foundries may extend standard 0.33 NA EUV using multi-patterning techniques for initial A14 layers rather than transitioning immediately to high-cost High-NA tools.
