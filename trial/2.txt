### 3. Power GaN Enters Multi-Market Transition as AI Datacenter Power Demands Drive 45% CAGR

**Source:** Yole Group / ET Datacenters / Power Integrations / Business Wire  
**Event Date:** 2026-08-15 | **Publication Date:** 2026-08-15  
**Status:** Industry Market Report & Supply Chain Transition

#### [Overview]
On August 15, 2026, semiconductor market disclosures and analysis of Yole Group's *Power GaN 2026* report confirmed that the gallium nitride (GaN) power semiconductor industry has completed its initial proving cycle in consumer fast chargers and entered a multi-market industrial expansion driven primarily by AI datacenter infrastructure. As hyperscale AI accelerator racks scale past 100 kW to 200 kW per rack, legacy silicon-based power distribution stages face extreme thermal dissipation and resistive conversion losses. GaN wide-bandgap devices offer significantly lower switching losses, higher electron mobility, and superior power density, allowing power supply units (PSUs) and board-level voltage regulator modules (VRMs) to reduce datacenter electrical conversion losses by up to 30%. According to industry data, the telecom and datacenter infrastructure GaN segment is projected to expand at a 45% compound annual growth rate (CAGR), reaching $750 million by 2031, out of an aggregate $3.5 billion power GaN device market. Concurrently, the manufacturing ecosystem is undergoing structural decentralization: TSMC's planned 2027 transition away from commercial GaN foundry operations has catalyzed major capacity expansions and multi-source foundry alignments across GlobalFoundries, Vanguard International Semiconductor (VIS), Powerchip (PSMC), and Samsung Foundry.

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem Perspective:** Elevates wide-bandgap compound semiconductors (GaN and SiC) from niche power components to critical enablers of next-generation 48V-to-1V and 800V AI server power delivery networks.
- **Business & Market Dynamics:** Broadens the competitive landscape for datacenter power management ICs, positioning specialized device makers (Innoscience, Infineon, Onsemi, Power Integrations) to capture market share from traditional silicon MOSFET suppliers.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware Implications:** Allows server designers to double power density within standard rack form factors, mitigating facility-level megawatt constraints and reducing cooling water/airflow requirements.
- **Investment Strategy:** Overweight power GaN epiwafer manufacturers, compound semiconductor foundries with 8-inch GaN-on-silicon lines, and server PSU module innovators.

### 4. Supply-Chain Reports Link NVIDIA 2028 Feynman GPU Platform to TSMC A16 Backside Power and Optical Packaging (Category 6)

**Source:** DIGITIMES / TechPowerUp / Benzinga / Semiconductor Engineering  
**Event Date:** 2026-08-14 | **Publication Date:** 2026-08-14  
**Status:** Industry Supply-Chain Report & Architecture Disclosures  
**Primary URL:** https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20260814PD202.html  

#### [Overview]
*Why this technology is important:* In next-generation AI supercomputing clusters, power distribution networks (PDN: the metallic interconnect grid that delivers electrical current across the chip) and inter-processor communication bandwidth have encountered severe physical limits. In conventional frontside power delivery architectures, power and ground lines share the same microscopic metal layers as high-speed signal wires, resulting in severe IR voltage drops (resistive loss across ultra-thin power lines) and routing congestion. Simultaneously, electrical copper interconnects (such as traditional SerDes: serializer/deserializer high-speed transceivers) incur steep insertion loss and thermal penalties as per-lane data rates scale toward 224 Gbps and 448 Gbps.

On August 14, 2026, semiconductor supply-chain reports indicated that NVIDIA is aligning medium-term fabrication and packaging capacity for its **Feynman GPU platform**, slated for commercial introduction in the second half of 2028. According to industry disclosures, NVIDIA is evaluating direct adoption of **TSMC's A16-class process platform**. The A16 platform integrates Backside Power Delivery Networks (BSPDN / Super Power Rail: a structural fabrication method where power rails are moved entirely to the backside of the silicon wafer, freeing the frontside metallization stack exclusively for signal routing). On the packaging front, Feynman is reported to mark NVIDIA's architectural transition toward true 3D heterogeneous integration via TSMC's System-on-Integrated-Chips (SoIC: direct die-to-die vertical stacking without microbumps), paired with customized HBM4E memory subsystems incorporating logic base dies. To address the copper scaling barrier, industry sources indicate prospective integration of **Co-Packaged Optics (CPO)** utilizing TSMC's COUPE (Compact Universal Photonic Engine) photonic integration platform to transition rack-level switch and compute interconnects toward optical links, enabling projected rack bisection bandwidths exceeding **1 Petabyte per second (> 1,000 TB/s)**. In tandem, TSMC is reportedly expanding SoIC advanced packaging capacity targets toward 50,000 wafers monthly by end-2027 across its AP7 and AP8 facilities.

*Impact on board and package layout designers:* For power integrity (PI) and signal integrity (SI) layout engineers, backside power delivery removes power vias from the frontside metal layers, significantly lowering PDN impedance and allowing higher transient current delivery to sub-1V compute logic. For interconnect designers, co-packaged optics transitions lossy PCB copper traces to optical fiber interfaces, simplifying board-level routing while introducing new thermal dissipation and optical alignment verification requirements for integrated Photonic Integrated Circuits (PICs).

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem Perspective:** Signals the semiconductor industry's transition from 2.5D multi-chiplet packaging to monolithic 3D SoIC die stacking and in-package photonics as necessary architectures for post-2nm AI supercomputing.
- **Business & Market Dynamics:** Deepens NVIDIA's technical moat against custom hyperscaler ASICs by reserving early A16 and SoIC foundry capacity, sustaining its premium computing platform margins.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware Implications:** Prepares datacenter facilities for multi-megawatt AI pods requiring direct-to-chip liquid cooling and optical switch fabrics to sustain 1 PB/s cluster bisection bandwidth.
- **Investment Strategy:** Overweight advanced packaging foundries, hybrid bonding capital equipment makers, backside power EDA modeling tools, and silicon photonics packaging specialists.

#### [Technical Deep Dive]

- **Core Technical Specifications (SW/HW):** [Architecture Platform]: NVIDIA Feynman GPU Generation (Supply-Chain Target: 2H 2028) | [Target Manufacturing Process]: TSMC A16-Class Node (Integrating Super Power Rail Backside Power Delivery) | [Packaging Topologies]: 3D SoIC Die Stacking + CoWoS / Panel-Level Advanced Packaging | [Optical Interconnect Integration]: Co-Packaged Optics (CPO) via TSMC COUPE Photonic Engine | [Projected Rack Bisection Bandwidth]: > 1,000 TB/s (1 PB/s) aggregate interconnect throughput | [Memory Integration]: Customized HBM4E with Integrated Logic Base Dies | [Advanced Packaging Capacity Target]: TSMC SoIC monthly capacity expanding toward ~50K wafers/month by 2027 | **Source:** DIGITIMES / Semiconductor Engineering / Supply-Chain Disclosures (2026-08-14)
- **Comparative Analysis:** Whereas the 2024–2026 Blackwell and Rubin generations rely on 2.5D planar chiplet interposers with electrical NVLink copper interconnects, the Feynman architecture roadmap transitions to 3D vertical logic stacking (SoIC) and heterogeneous optical engine integration, doubling interconnect throughput over Rubin Ultra while decoupling power delivery from signal routing.

While co-packaged optics resolves electrical insertion loss at 224G/448G per lane, embedding optical engines within compute packages presents severe manufacturing yield and thermal risks. External laser sources (ELS) or detachable optical connectors are required to prevent a single optical failure from destroying an entire multi-die compute assembly. Furthermore, TSMC's A16 backside power delivery requires wafer thinning and complex backside lithography that could face early yield volatility.
