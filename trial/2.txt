### 1. Marvell Grants Google Performance-Linked Warrants Under Expanded Custom Silicon Agreement Attached to TPU Ecosystem

#### [Overview]
On August 18–19, 2026, Marvell Technology formalized and disclosed in an SEC Form 8-K filing an expanded commercial agreement and warrant transaction with Google LLC, with institutional market reactions and technical supply-chain confirmations crystallizing on August 20–21. Under the definitive agreement, Marvell granted Google a warrant to acquire up to 58,970,907 shares of Marvell common stock at an exercise price of $206.58 per share, representing an aggregate exercise price of approximately $12.18 billion if all tranches fully vest and are exercised. Rather than replacing Google's core compute ASIC partnership with Broadcom, the co-development agreement focuses on custom silicon products designed to attach directly across Google's Tensor Processing Unit (TPU) ecosystem. Specifically, the scope covers specialized AI inference accelerators, storage controllers, custom network interface controllers (NICs), memory interface controllers, and near-memory compute silicon. The vesting mechanism is structured to align long-term commercial delivery: after an initial time-based tranche of 1,360,867 shares vesting over the first twelve months, the remaining warrant shares vest across 240 equal tranches. Each tranche is unlocked for every $500 million in recognized Custom Products revenue from the third quarter of fiscal 2027 through fiscal 2033. Full vesting would correspond to $120 billion in cumulative hardware procurement, potentially making Google one of Marvell's largest single equity holders.

### 4. d-Matrix Details 3D DRAM-Based Inference Accelerator Architecture 'Raptor' Ahead of Hot Chips 2026 (Category 6)

**Source:** Hot Chips 2026 Advance Program / Chip Log / Semiconductor Engineering / d-Matrix Technical Disclosures  
**Event Date:** 2026-08-20 | **Publication Date:** 2026-08-21  
**Status:** Academic Symposium Proceeding & Architecture Roadmap Preview  
**Primary URL:** https://hotchips.org/advance-program/  

#### [Overview]
*Why this research is important:* In transformer-based large language models operating over extensive context windows, memory bandwidth and Key-Value (KV) cache retrieval represent the primary physical bottlenecks constraining token generation throughput. While conventional High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) architectures deliver high aggregate bandwidth, they do so using wide, relatively low-frequency parallel interfaces across 2.5D silicon interposers (thin silicon substrates that connect side-by-side chips using microscopic metal traces). While effective, this lateral routing incurs physical package area overhead, routing complexity, and interface energy consumption (typically 4 to 7 picojoules per bit, pJ/bit, for the overall memory subsystem).

Ahead of the Hot Chips 2026 Symposium (scheduled for August 23–25 at Stanford University), advance technical disclosures released on August 20–21, 2026, highlighted architectural specifications for **d-Matrix's next-generation AI inference accelerator, codenamed "Raptor,"** alongside industry tutorial sessions on 3D DRAM memory technologies (co-presented by engineers from d-Matrix and Meta). Rather than arranging memory stacks laterally on an interposer, Raptor implements **3D DRAM integration**, vertically stacking custom DRAM dies directly on top of digital tensor compute logic using high-density Through-Silicon Vias (TSVs: microscopic vertical copper conduits etched through silicon dies to route power and signals directly between stacked layers). This vertical arrangement shortens physical interconnect wire lengths from millimeters to microns, reducing memory access energy to sub-1 pJ/bit and multiplying aggregate vertical interface bandwidth for real-time generative inference.

*Impact on physical board, package, and system designers:* For package layout, signal integrity (SI), and power integrity (PI) engineers, stacking DRAM directly atop active compute dies introduces major thermal-mechanical co-design challenges. Designers must manage vertical thermal resistance to ensure that heat generated by high-density digital tensor engines does not exceed the critical retention temperature of the overlying DRAM cells (typically 85°C to 95°C). Addressing this requires advanced wafer thinning, micro-bump pitch scaling or direct hybrid bonding (ultra-fine-pitch copper-to-copper direct bonding), and thermal-aware power delivery networks (PDNs: the physical network of metal planes and decoupling capacitors that stabilizes supply voltage during rapid current transients).

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem Perspective:** Demonstrates that vertical 3D DRAM logic stacking is a viable architectural pathway to bypass 2.5D interposer routing constraints for memory-bandwidth-bound generative inference.
- **Business & Market Dynamics:** Positions dedicated near-memory and in-memory compute architectures to compete against general-purpose GPUs in latency-critical enterprise inference workloads where token-generation energy efficiency dictates operational margins.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware Implications:** Enables higher compute and memory density per socket, reducing server footprint and cooling complexity for high-throughput reasoning workloads.
- **Investment Strategy:** Overweight advanced packaging toolmakers (hybrid bonding, wafer thinning), thermal interface material (TIM) specialists, and 3D signal/thermal co-simulation EDA software providers.

#### [Technical Deep Dive]

- **Core Technical Specifications (SW/HW):** [Symposium Context]: Hot Chips 2026 Advance Program & Memory Tutorial (Presented by Sudeep Bhoja, d-Matrix & Aayush Ankit, Meta) | [Architecture Codename]: d-Matrix Raptor | [Architecture Class]: 3D DRAM-Stacked Digital Near-Memory Inference Accelerator | [Vertical Interconnect]: High-density vertical TSVs and fine-pitch die stacking directly over tensor logic | [Memory Subsystem Energy]: Sub-1 pJ/bit per memory access (compared to 4–7 pJ/bit for standard 2.5D HBM subsystems) | [Target Workload Focus]: Low-latency Generative LLM Inference & Dynamic KV-Cache Offload | [Package Design Requirements]: Thermal-aware floorplanning, low-profile PDN decoupling, and specialized thermal interface materials | **Source:** Hot Chips 2026 Program / Semiconductor Engineering (2026-08-20/21)
- **Comparative Analysis:** HBM achieves bandwidth by placing DRAM stacks alongside the processor over a 2.5D silicon interposer using thousands of parallel interconnect wires. Vertical 3D DRAM eliminates the lateral interposer bridge entirely by routing data vertically through TSVs into the underlying logic, reducing memory access energy by several-fold while significantly lowering package routing area.
- **Release / Deployment Stage:** Technical presentation and architectural disclosures at Hot Chips 2026; architecture validation and prototyping underway.

#### [Critical View]
While 3D DRAM vertical stacking delivers notable energy and bandwidth improvements, thermal cross-talk remains a critical engineering constraint. High power dissipation in the underlying tensor logic die can create localized thermal hotspots that conduct directly into the DRAM stack, risking data retention degradation or necessitating elevated refresh cycles that partially offset performance gains. Additionally, multi-layer die stacking introduces compound yield risks during fabrication and testing.
