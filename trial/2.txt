#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? Enterprise adoption of generative visual pipelines has long run into a steep cost-quality cliff: frontier image models capable of surgical product retouching, reliable typography, and multi-reference consistency have often cost over $200 per 1,000 renders, pricing out high-volume creative iteration. At Microsoft Ignite on November 17, 2026, Microsoft AI introduced `MAI Image 2.7` and its speed-oriented sibling `MAI Image 2.7 Flash`, alongside `Canvas`, a new interactive visual workspace powered by the Flash tier. Rebuilt between April and November 2026 as a 20-billion-parameter (non-embedding) flow-matching diffusion architecture—a training approach that learns straighter, more continuous paths from random noise to finished pixels to support faster, higher-fidelity synthesis—`MAI Image 2.7` supports a 32K-token prompt context, native transparent background exports, up to five reference images, and outputs up to 4.19 million pixels (2048×2048 or equivalent aspect ratios). On Artificial Analysis's blind `AA-Image-Editing v2.1` leaderboard (as captured on November 17, 2026), `MAI Image 2.7` took the #1 point-estimate ranking at 1,215 Elo across 5,478 human preference votes, edging ahead of OpenAI's `GPT Image 2.6 Sunburst (max)` (1,211 Elo) at roughly one-sixth the reported API price ($34.82 vs. $210.72 per 1,000 images). Meanwhile, `MAI Image 2.7 Flash` ($10.24 per 1,000 images, 1,179 Elo) ranks seventh overall, ahead of `Seedream 5.5` (1,160 Elo) and Google's `Nano Banana 2.1` (1,152 Elo). By pairing low-cost Flash iteration in `Canvas` with top-ranked final rendering in a two-tier API workflow, Microsoft is applying direct pricing pressure to both Adobe's Firefly/Photoshop commercial editing suite and OpenAI's premium image endpoints.

### 3. [CATEGORY 6: HARDWARE / PACKAGE & THERMAL CO-DESIGN] Marvell Quantifies "Custom HBM as a System": Base-Die Offloading Reclaims 25% Compute Die Area While Addressing Severe Stack Thermal Resistance

**Source:** Marvell Technology Engineering Blog (expanding on 2026 OCP Global Summit Technical Disclosure)  
**Event Date:** 2026-11-17 *(within 7-day Category 6 window; confirm whether blog represents a new Nov 17 technical post vs. OCP Summit material repost)*  
**Source Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Status:** Engineering blog / Architecture blueprint  
**Primary URL:** https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQE-x1qmmpR6iIiKdYO37QGFAA7YnLvw0OV2zA2D87zp3hzCUibf7Jo5RD9xY9ZvHaWSh7ezcNYCQHrlCtIEP_6iuiiDurgM0iP8gsdwWHh1nKEwVEZgE8Y81pgcVY66G8FRnumFgvaW-4JtbOGKsRQWHlvBRRZt9cdQ_FWLUiOmoHYZ-6Yis-EDOgi-PlgQvuHbeA==

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM)—vertically stacked DRAM dies bonded atop a base logic die—now accounts for more than half of total XPU bill-of-materials cost at 2026 memory prices and occupies roughly 30% of the overall 2.5D package/interposer footprint, while its wide Physical Interface (PHY) and controller blocks crowd the outer perimeter ("beachfront") of the separate compute die. With HBM4 doubling the memory interface to 2,048 bits on the path toward 4.1 TB/s per stack in HBM4E, those wide HBM PHYs squeeze tensor cores out of expensive leading-edge compute silicon. In a November 17 technical disclosure building on its 2026 OCP Global Summit presentation, Marvell analyzed why moving the memory controller off the compute die onto a custom logic base die beneath each DRAM stack creates a tightly coupled area-power-thermal co-design challenge. Replacing wide HBM PHYs on the XPU with a narrow Die-to-Die (D2D) PHY frees up to 25% of existing compute die area for logic (reclaiming silicon rather than physically expanding reticle size), but relocates active switching circuits directly underneath a DRAM stack that is difficult to cool. Citing Samsung IEEE ECTC 2026 thermal validation data, Marvell notes that along each component's own vertical thermal path, internal HBM stack resistance accounts for 57% of its path resistance compared with 13% for an adjacent ASIC along its path. Placing active D2D PHY logic under that high-resistance stack elevates localized heat-flux risk—turning the D2D PHY into a potential thermal hotspot depending on power density and cooling boundary conditions—prompting Marvell to outline a co-designed stack using copper-to-copper hybrid bonding, silicon heat paths, and adaptive D2D power gating.

#### [Strategic Impact]
**Technology & Ecosystem:**  
Standard JEDEC HBM is evolving toward application-specific Custom HBM4/4E architectures, requiring XPU architects and partners like Marvell to co-design the HBM base die, interposer D2D PHY, Thermal Interface Material (TIM), and cold-plate lid as a unified system rather than treating DRAM cubes as drop-in commodity parts.

**Business & Market:**  
With HBM exceeding 50% of accelerator silicon BOM cost, reclaiming up to 25% of leading-edge (3nm/2nm) compute die area—while targeting up to 75% D2D PHY power savings under normal workloads—offers custom ASIC teams (Broadcom, Marvell, and hyperscaler silicon groups) a structural cost and efficiency lever.

**Infrastructure & Hardware:**  
This blueprint traces the physical hardware chain: wider 2,048-bit HBM4 beachfront → custom base-die controller offload → elevated base-die heat flux beneath a high-resistance DRAM stack → replacing ~30 um microbump/underfill gaps with Cu-Cu hybrid bonding → embedded silicon heat paths and microfluidic cooling targets (~2–4 kW per ~800 sq mm reticle-class die) → direct-to-chip liquid cooling at the rack level.

**Investment Implication:**  
Package-level thermal constraints are shifting strategic value toward Cu-Cu hybrid bonding equipment, high-conductivity TIMs/underfills, and low-energy D2D PHY IP—making thermal-mechanical co-design as decisive as transistor node shrinks.

### 4. [CATEGORY 6: HARDWARE / POWER INTEGRITY & FIRMWARE] AMD & UW-Madison Unveil `PACER`: Characterizing and Mitigating LLM Power Excursions (`EDPp`) on 750W GPUs

**Source:** arXiv (`arXiv:2611.17206`, AMD & University of Wisconsin–Madison; verify full paper title, authors, and submission timestamp at https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17206)  
**Event Date:** 2026-11-12 *(within 7-day Category 6 window: Nov 11 08:56 – Nov 18 08:56 KST)*  
**Source Date:** 2026-11-12  
**Status:** Academic / Industry engineering paper  
**Primary URL:** https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQHFdEkKOsb_PXSSWHbI5tpLOTUeGYRGW4_9SZJRz1E7ppatS21-Fkf2upBaYWDirbsKL-h7-FWASCACV6cNx5RD0ZSOmqWxXyAEuJlTJhFBHK6rbtUkk45mFQ== (Canonical arXiv reference: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17206)

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? As AI accelerator Thermal Design Power (TDP)—the sustained cooling ceiling a board and heatsink are rated to dissipate—reaches 750W to 1,400W+, LLM inference workloads fire thousands of short compute kernels with rapidly shifting arithmetic intensity. These bursty load steps trigger Electrical Design Power peaks (`EDPp`), transient board-power spikes that exceed rated TDP and trip coarse firmware throttling. Published on November 12, 2026 (within the 7-day Category 6 window), researchers from AMD and the University of Wisconsin–Madison introduced `PACER`, an empirical 1-millisecond hardware-counter characterization of `EDPp` across 750W AMD Instinct `MI300X` GPUs running `LLaMA3-70B`, `Qwen3-14B`, `DeepSeek-V3`, and `Stable Diffusion`. Using 1 ms Total Board Power telemetry, they observed that ~80% of power peaks hit 1.0x to 1.2x TDP, while a long tail (<1% of events) spikes up to 1.7x TDP (over 1,275W on a 750W GPU). Because production Dynamic Power Management (DPM) firmware polls on a coarse 1–2 ms loop—slower than many short LLM kernels—and operates unsynchronized across GPUs in a node, accelerators throttle asymmetrically, creating inter-GPU frequency skew and synchronization delays. While 1 ms board-power counters cannot directly instrument microsecond-level current spikes (`di/dt`), decoupling capacitor depletion, or internal Voltage Regulator Module (VRM) stress, those sub-millisecond electrical risks motivate tighter excursion bounds. `PACER` replaces blind throttling with coordinated hardware-firmware excursion control (using moderate `1.2x–1.3x TDP` clamping to preserve brief "computational sprinting" while clipping extreme tail overshoots), delivering 3–12% workload performance improvements across evaluated Time-To-First-Token (TTFT) and throughput metrics (within 3% of an idealized oracle) and improving Service Level Objective (SLO) attainment by up to 6%.

#### [Strategic Impact]
**Technology & Ecosystem:**  
`PACER` demonstrates that LLM inference efficiency is shaped not only by kernel compilers and HBM bandwidth, but also by millisecond-scale mismatches between bursty GPU execution and board-level DPM firmware, where unsynchronized throttling traps 3–12% in recoverable workload performance inside coarse guardbands.

**Business & Market:**  
Capturing 3–12% TTFT and throughput gains alongside up to 6% higher SLO attainment through firmware-hardware excursion tuning improves effective tokens-per-dollar and cluster capacity on existing silicon, while informing safer rack-level power oversubscription strategies.

**Infrastructure & Hardware:**  
Bridges software burstiness to board/rack power architecture: short-kernel prefill spikes (measured up to 1.7x TDP at 1 ms resolution) → inferred microsecond decoupling-capacitor and VRM transient stress → 1–2 ms DPM firmware lag → inter-GPU frequency skew → tighter rack-level power-budgeting constraints.

**Investment Implication:**  
High-frequency Power Delivery Network (PDN) resilience—smart multiphase VRMs, on-package/board decoupling capacitors, and sub-millisecond power telemetry—is becoming an essential performance enabler for 1 kW+ AI accelerators.
