### 4. SK hynix Breaks Ground on $4B+ Indiana Fab for HBM4E Packaging and Warns Memory Deficit to 2030 (Category 6)

**Source:** SK hynix Official Newsroom / Bloomberg News / Reuters / Evertiq  
**Event Date:** 2026-08-27 (Groundbreaking at Purdue University) / 2026-08-28 (Seoul Disclosures Finalized)  
**Source Date:** 2026-08-28 (Formal Industry Proceedings Published)  
**Status:** Official Groundbreaking & Semiconductor Manufacturing Disclosure  
**Primary URL:** https://news.skhynix.com/sk-hynix-holds-groundbreaking-ceremony-for-hbm-production-base-in-indiana/  

#### [Overview]
Why this development is important: In high-concurrency artificial intelligence systems, High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) is the critical physical component determining accelerator throughput and memory wall mitigation. However, manufacturing 16-high HBM stacks within JEDEC's strict 775-micrometer (µm) package height standard pushes silicon thinning to sub-30 µm dimensions, creating severe wafer warpage and structural yield degradation. Furthermore, because advanced packaging has historically been concentrated in East Asia, shipping fragile front-end DRAM wafers across global supply chains creates geopolitical vulnerability and extended delivery cycles for US accelerator integrators.

On August 27–28, 2026, South Korean semiconductor leader SK hynix officially broke ground on its **$4 billion+ advanced packaging fabrication and R&D base** at Purdue Research Park in West Lafayette, Indiana. The 54-hectare facility represents the first high-volume advanced HBM packaging plant in the United States, backed by US CHIPS and Science Act funding. The plant is scheduled to open cleanrooms by **October 2028 and initiate mass production of next-generation HBM (HBM4E) in the second half of 2029**. Under this manufacturing architecture, front-end DRAM wafer processing remains at SK hynix's mega-fabs in South Korea, while processed wafers are shipped to Indiana for Through-Silicon Via (TSV) micro-bumping, Advanced Mass Reflow Molded Underfill (MR-MUF), wafer thinning, high-density 16-Hi stacking, and final integration testing. Concurrently, SK hynix signed an R&D partnership with Purdue University to construct an **Advanced Packaging Testbed** focused on copper-to-copper hybrid bonding technologies for future HBM5 architectures. During the ceremony, SK hynix CEO Kwak Noh-jung issued a strategic market warning: the structural shortage of high-bandwidth memory will persist **through 2030**, as multi-die packaging capacity growth struggles to keep pace with accelerator deployment velocity.

Impact on physical board, package, and system designers: For substrate layout, power integrity (PI: maintaining clean supply rails under heavy dynamic current steps), and signal integrity (SI: preserving eye-diagram margins across dense parallel channels) engineers, the Indiana fab alters supply-chain dynamics and package co-design. Transitioning to 2,048-bit HBM4/HBM4E interfaces doubles trace routing density across 2.5D interposers, requiring over 16,000 micro-bumps and 20,000 TSVs per stack. Board designers interfacing with US-packaged HBM modules will benefit from shorter validation feedback loops with Purdue’s R&D testbed, accelerating thermal-mechanical co-simulation to resolve die warpage, micro-bump shear stress, and localized transient voltage droop (di/dt) in liquid-cooled accelerator trays.

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem Perspective:** Establishes an advanced packaging back-end cluster in North America, integrating domestic memory stacking with US foundry accelerators (TSMC Arizona / Intel EMIB).
- **Business & Market Dynamics:** Reinforces SK hynix's market position in high-density HBM while confirming structural supply tightness and firm memory pricing through 2030.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware Implications:** Streamlines the delivery pipeline for HBM4E memory modules directly to US rack integrators, reducing cross-Pacific logistics lead times for hyperscaler buildouts.
- **Investment Strategy:** Overweight advanced packaging toolmakers (wafer thinning, hybrid bonding equipment, automated optical inspection), high-density substrate manufacturers, and thermal interface materials.
