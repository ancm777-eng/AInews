While next-generation AI accelerators operate at thermal design power (TDP) thresholds exceeding 1,000W to 1,500W per socket, production clusters rarely execute continuous peak-power workloads across every compute node simultaneously. In conventional datacenter designs, infrastructure engineers must provision power supply networks according to worst-case nameplate peak power ratings, leaving massive margins of electrical substation capacity stranded and idle during dynamic workload phase shifts. If datacenter power distribution cannot dynamically throttle and shift current across compute nodes in real time, cluster operators are physically barred from installing additional accelerator hardware within existing grid allocations.

Operating in close synchronization with server-level power management integrated circuits (PMICs) and facility-level power distribution units (PDUs), DSX MaxLPS dynamically monitors instantaneous electrical draw and redistributes power headroom across interconnected liquid-cooled server racks. Delivering this power requires transitioning from legacy 12V/48V busbars to high-efficiency 54V and stepping towards 800V DC facility distribution. By stepping down voltage vertically adjacent to the compute trays using high-frequency resonant converter modules, the system slashes motherboard resistive losses (I squared R losses) and copper trace congestion, allowing cleaner routing paths for high-speed differential signal traces.

### 2. NVIDIA Surges Mixture-of-Experts Training 10.4x on GB200 via JAX and Transformer Engine Ragged-Tensor Kernels

**Source:** NVIDIA Developer Blog / Quantum Zeitgeist / The Neuron / Brocker  
**Event Date:** 2026-09-15  
**Source Date:** 2026-09-15, 08:00 PDT (2026-09-15, 20:00 KST)  
**Status:** Official Technical Architecture Publication & Open-Source Kernel Release  
**Primary URL:** https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/accelerating-dropless-moe-training-jax-transformer-engine/

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures—exemplified by frontier models such as DeepSeek-V3, Qwen, and Mixtral—have established dominance in state-of-the-art foundation models by routing tokens dynamically to a subset of specialized "expert" neural networks. While MoE drastically reduces active parameters and inference floating-point operations (FLOPs) compared to dense models, scaling MoE training across massive GPU clusters has encountered an acute systems bottleneck: expert load imbalance. Because learned gating routers dispatch tokens dynamically, individual experts receive highly uneven token distributions, creating irregular, non-rectangular data structures known as "ragged tensors". In unoptimized training pipelines, processors must either pad tokens with dummy values (wasting memory and compute) or drop tokens entirely (degrading model reasoning quality). Furthermore, moving ragged tokens across distributed GPUs during all-to-all communications causes severe interconnect congestion, leaving expensive tensor cores stalled while waiting for memory transfers.

On September 15, 2026, NVIDIA researchers and systems engineers published technical results detailing how combining the JAX Python library with targeted NVIDIA Transformer Engine kernel optimizations achieves a **10.4x throughput speedup** during dropless MoE training on NVIDIA GB200 NVL72 systems. Benchmarking the architecture on the 671-billion-parameter **DeepSeek-V3** model, NVIDIA revealed that an unoptimized baseline achieved only **103 TFLOPS/GPU**, with inter-GPU all-to-all communication consuming **84% of accumulated kernel execution time**. Through targeted algorithmic innovations, NVIDIA drove compute throughput to **1,068 TFLOPS/GPU** while maintaining a **97% multi-rack scaling efficiency across 1,024 GPUs**. The optimization stack eliminates the need for token dropping or padding by deploying: (1) specialized grouped General Matrix Multiply (GEMM) kernels via cuBLAS and cuBLASLt that process variable expert token counts within a single kernel launch; (2) Microscaling FP8 (MXFP8) group-aware quantization that compresses activations without precision loss; (3) fused NCCL Expert Parallelism (NCCL EP) primitives that merge dispatch and combine stages while deduplicating tokens; and (4) XLA multistreaming collectives that overlap inter-node communication behind active matrix math.

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem:** Establishes JAX and Transformer Engine as a premier production stack for dropless MoE training, proving that software kernel co-design can resolve communication-bound scaling bottlenecks without architectural compromises.
- **Business & Market:** Enhances the commercial return on investment for hyperscalers training multi-hundred-billion-parameter MoE models on GB200 clusters, lowering effective pre-training compute costs by an order of magnitude.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware:** Relieves inter-GPU NVLink and InfiniBand bandwidth saturation during expert all-to-all exchanges, allowing clusters to sustain higher compute arithmetic intensity without hitting interconnect saturation walls.
- **Investment Implication:** Overweight advanced AI compiler frameworks, communication-hiding runtime platforms, and low-precision quantization tooling; reinforces the structural moat of NVIDIA's full-stack software optimization layer.

### 4. OpenAI "Project Lily" Investigation Exposes Hundreds of Human Contractors Reviewing Private Prompts and Memory Summaries

**Source:** 404 Media / TechRepublic / AI Weekly / The Decoder / GattyWorks  
**Event Date:** 2026-09-15  
**Source Date:** 2026-09-15, 05:00 EDT (2026-09-15, 18:00 KST)  
**Status:** Investigative Journalistic Exposure & Corporate Confirmation  
**Primary URL:** https://www.techrepublic.com/article/openai-project-lily-human-reviewers-privacy/

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? Foundation model developers rely extensively on Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and fine-tuning pipelines to align raw base models with human preferences, eliminating undesirable behaviors such as sycophancy, excessive verbosity, and hallucinations. However, enterprise and consumer trust rests on the assumption that personal conversations remain confidential and protected by automated redaction filters. When an AI laboratory routes real-world user interactions—including cross-session personal memory summaries—to distributed external contractor networks, data privacy ceases to be an abstract terms-of-service clause and becomes an active enterprise data-leakage vulnerability.

On September 15, 2026, investigative reporting by 404 Media, corroborated by leaked internal documentation and contractor testimonies, revealed that OpenAI employs hundreds of human contract workers under an internal initiative codenamed **"Project Lily"** to inspect, summarize, and rate real ChatGPT user conversations. Recruited via staffing firm Crossing Hurdles and paid through AI-training platform Mercor at rates exceeding $50 per hour, reviewers are tasked with evaluating model responses on a 1-to-7 scale. Internal guidelines instruct reviewers to steer models away from "AI-speak," weed out sycophantic agreement, and eliminate unnatural anthropomorphism. However, leaked materials disclosed that contractors review full conversation histories and can view a **"user memories summary"** placed above the prompt, which aggregates historical user topics and reveals approximate geographic locations. Crucially, internal OpenAI documentation obtained in the investigation acknowledged that while conversations are processed through an automated "Privacy Filter" to redact personally identifiable information (PII), the filter routinely fails on uncommon identifiers, rare names, and ambiguous contextual references, leaving sensitive personal, legal, and medical details visible to contractors. The setting permitting conversation harvesting ("Improve the model for everyone") is enabled by default across consumer tiers (Free, Plus, and Pro), and opting out does not delete past reviews retroactively. Anthropic confirmed to reporters that it utilizes analogous human review workflows for users with active data-sharing settings.

- **Technology & Ecosystem:** Exposes the technical limitations of automated NLP-based PII scrubbing filters in high-throughput alignment pipelines, accelerating enterprise demand for zero-retention API endpoints and client-side differential privacy.
- **Business & Market:** Deepens enterprise reluctance to deploy consumer-tier AI accounts for business workflows; organizations in legal, financial, and healthcare sectors will likely mandate on-premises or private sovereign cloud instances with hard contractual guarantees against human review.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware:** Accelerates enterprise adoption of localized edge AI models and on-premises inference appliances (e.g., local agent workstations) where sensitive proprietary context never traverses external cloud networks.
- **Investment Implication:** Overweight enterprise data loss prevention (DLP) vendors, client-side PII sanitization software, and privacy-preserving synthetic data startups; monitor potential regulatory inquiries from EU and California data protection authorities.
