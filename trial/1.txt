#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? At Microsoft Ignite on November 17, 2026, Microsoft AI introduced `MAI Image 2.7` and its speed-optimized sibling `MAI Image 2.7 Flash`, alongside `Canvas`, a new interactive visual workspace powered by the Flash tier. Rebuilt from the ground up between April and November 2026 as a 20-billion-parameter (non-embedding) flow-matching diffusion architecture—a training objective that learns continuous, straighter trajectories from noise to pixels to enable faster, higher-fidelity generation—`MAI Image 2.7` supports a 32K-token prompt context, transparent background exports, up to five reference images, and outputs up to 4.19 million pixels (2048×2048 or equivalent aspect ratios). On Artificial Analysis's blind `AA-Image-Editing v2.1` leaderboard, `MAI Image 2.7` immediately claimed the #1 overall spot with 1,215 Elo across 5,478 human preference votes, edging out OpenAI's `GPT Image 2.6 Sunburst (max)` (1,211 Elo) at roughly one-sixth the API price ($34.82 vs. $210.72 per 1,000 images). The release directly pressures Adobe's Firefly/Photoshop commercial editing moat and OpenAI's premium image pricing by pairing near-zero-cost Flash exploration in `Canvas` with top-ranked final rendering in a single two-tier API workflow.

### 3. [CATEGORY 6: HARDWARE / PACKAGE & THERMAL CO-DESIGN] Marvell Quantifies "Custom HBM as a System": Base-Die Offloading Frees 25% Compute Area While Exposing 57% Stack Thermal Resistance

**Source:** Marvell Technology Engineering Blog (2026 OCP Global Summit Technical Disclosure)  
**Event Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Source Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Status:** Conference technical disclosure / Architecture blueprint  
**Primary URL:** https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQE-x1qmmpR6iIiKdYO37QGFAA7YnLvw0OV2zA2D87zp3hzCUibf7Jo5RD9xY9ZvHaWSh7ezcNYCQHrlCtIEP_6iuiiDurgM0iP8gsdwWHh1nKEwVEZgE8Y81pgcVY66G8FRnumFgvaW-4JtbOGKsRQWHlvBRRZt9cdQ_FWLUiOmoHYZ-6Yis-EDOgi-PlgQvuHbeA==

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM)—vertically stacked DRAM dies bonded atop a base logic die—now consumes roughly 30% of an AI accelerator's die area and, at 2026 memory prices, more than half of total XPU bill-of-materials cost. With HBM4 doubling the memory interface to 2,048 bits on the path toward 4.1 TB/s per stack in HBM4E, wide HBM PHYs (physical interface circuits) and controllers are crowding the compute die's "beachfront"—the outer die perimeter where signals enter and exit—starving tensor cores of silicon area. In a November 17 technical engineering disclosure expanding on its 2026 OCP Global Summit presentation, Marvell quantified why moving the memory controller off the XPU onto a custom logic base die beneath each DRAM stack creates an interlocking area-power-thermal co-design problem. Replacing wide HBM PHYs on the compute die with a narrow Die-to-Die (D2D) PHY frees up to 25% of compute die area, but shifts active switching logic directly underneath a DRAM stack that is notoriously difficult to cool. Citing Samsung IEEE ECTC 2026 thermal validation data, Marvell notes that in 2.5D packages, the HBM stack's internal thermal resistance already accounts for 57% of total thermal resistance (versus just 13% for the adjacent ASIC), making the D2D PHY on the custom base die the package's primary thermal hotspot. Marvell therefore outlines a unified package-level solution combining copper-to-copper hybrid bonding, silicon heat paths, and adaptive D2D power gating.

#### [Strategic Impact]
**Technology & Ecosystem:**  
Standard JEDEC HBM is transitioning into application-specific Custom HBM4/4E architectures where hyperscaler XPU architects (via merchant partners like Marvell) co-design the HBM base die, interposer D2D PHY, Thermal Interface Material (TIM), and cold-plate lid simultaneously rather than treating DRAM stacks as drop-in commodity cubes.

**Business & Market:**  
With HBM exceeding 50% of accelerator silicon cost, reclaiming up to 25% of expensive leading-edge (3nm/2nm) compute die area—while cutting D2D PHY power by up to 75%—gives custom ASIC vendors (Broadcom, Marvell, and hyperscaler internal silicon teams) a direct BOM and perf-per-watt lever against merchant GPUs.

**Infrastructure & Hardware:**  
This disclosure directly traces the physical chain: wider 2,048-bit HBM4 beachfront → custom base-die controller offload → D2D PHY base-die hotspot → elimination of ~30 um microbump/underfill gaps via Cu-Cu hybrid bonding → embedded silicon heat paths / microfluidic cooling scaling toward 2–4 kW per reticle-class (~800 sq mm) die → mandatory direct-to-chip liquid cooling at the rack level.

**Investment Implication:**  
Advanced packaging thermal bottlenecks are creating outsized structural value in Cu-Cu hybrid bonding, high-thermal-conductivity underfills/TIMs, and low-energy D2D PHY IP—making thermal-mechanical co-design as decisive as transistor node shrinks.

### 4. [CATEGORY 6: HARDWARE / POWER INTEGRITY & FIRMWARE] AMD & UW-Madison Unveil `PACER`: Characterizing and Mitigating LLM Power Excursions (`EDPp`) on 750W GPUs for 3–12% Performance Gains

**Source:** arXiv (`arXiv:2611.17206`, AMD & University of Wisconsin–Madison)  
**Event Date:** 2026-11-12  
**Source Date:** 2026-11-12  
**Status:** Academic / Industry engineering paper  
**Primary URL:** https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQHFdEkKOsb_PXSSWHbI5tpLOTUeGYRGW4_9SZJRz1E7ppatS21-Fkf2upBaYWDirbsKL-h7-FWASCACV6cNx5RD0ZSOmqWxXyAEuJlTJhFBHK6rbtUkk45mFQ==

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? As AI accelerator Thermal Design Power (TDP)—the sustained cooling ceiling a board and heatsink are rated to dissipate—hits 750W to 1,400W+, LLM inference workloads generate thousands of microsecond-scale compute kernels with violently fluctuating arithmetic intensity. These rapid load steps trigger Electrical Design Power peaks (`EDPp`), transient current and power spikes that exceed rated TDP, drain package decoupling capacitors, push Voltage Regulator Modules (VRMs) toward compliance limits, and trip coarse firmware throttling. Published on November 12, 2026, researchers from AMD and the University of Wisconsin–Madison unveiled `PACER`, the first empirical 1-millisecond hardware-counter characterization of `EDPp` across 750W AMD Instinct `MI300X` GPUs running `LLaMA3-70B`, `Qwen3-14B`, `DeepSeek-V3`, and `Stable Diffusion`. They found that across ML workloads, ~80% of power peaks hit 1.0x to 1.2x TDP, while a long tail (<1% of events) spikes up to 1.7x TDP (over 1,275W on a 750W GPU). Because production Dynamic Power Management (DPM) firmware polls at a coarse 1–2 ms cadence—slower than many LLM kernels—and operates unsynchronized across multi-GPU nodes, GPUs thrash clocks asymmetrically, causing severe intra-GPU latency jitter. `PACER` replaces blind throttling with coordinated hardware-firmware excursion management (using moderate `1.2x–1.3x TDP` clamping that preserves brief "computational sprinting" while eliminating extreme overshoots), improving Time-To-First-Token (TTFT) and overall performance by 3–12% over production power management—within 3% of an idealized oracle—and boosting Service Level Objective (SLO) attainment by up to 6%.

#### [Strategic Impact]
**Technology & Ecosystem:**  
`PACER` proves that LLM inference efficiency is no longer purely a kernel-compiler or HBM-bandwidth problem; millisecond-scale mismatches between GPU execution engines and board-level VRM/DPM firmware leave 3–12% free performance trapped inside power-delivery safety guardbands.

**Business & Market:**  
Recovering 3–12% TTFT performance and up to 6% SLO compliance via firmware/hardware excursion tuning directly increases tokens-per-dollar and cluster capacity without adding silicon, while enabling tighter rack-level power oversubscription (where hyperscalers pack more GPUs under fixed facility power feeds).

**Infrastructure & Hardware:**  
Connects LLM workload burstiness directly down the hardware stack: short-kernel prefill spikes (up to 1.7x TDP) → package decoupling capacitor depletion & VRM transient stress → 1–2 ms DPM firmware lag → asymmetric multi-GPU frequency droop → rack power oversubscription limits.

**Investment Implication:**  
High-frequency Power Delivery Network (PDN) resilience—smart multiphase VRMs, on-package/board decoupling (MLCCs), and sub-millisecond hardware telemetry—is emerging as a first-class performance multiplier for 1 kW+ AI accelerators.
