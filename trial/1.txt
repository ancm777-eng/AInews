Why does this matter? While the federal government under the Trump administration is actively deregulating the technology sector, the State of California houses the headquarters, primary compute clusters, and core engineering talent of nearly every major Western frontier AI laboratory (including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Meta). Under the U.S. federalist system, states hold broad police powers to protect public welfare, consumer safety, and critical infrastructure. If California enacts state-level operational mandates—such as requiring third-party written safety plans or hardcoded emergency shutoff mechanisms—frontier labs cannot simply ignore the rules without relocating their corporate entities and physical operations outside the world's fifth-largest economy.

### 3. Consumers File Federal Antitrust Class Action Against Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI, and Google Over Alleged AI Pacing Cartel

**Source:** U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California / Los Angeles Times / Politico / CBS News / BigGo Finance  
**Event Date:** 2026-09-18

**Primary URL:** https://www.latimes.com/business/technology/story/2026-09-19/anthropic-openai-spacexai-google-lawsuit-ai-slowdown

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? In modern antitrust jurisprudence, competitors are strictly prohibited from entering into horizontal agreements that restrict production output, cap technological development velocity, or coordinate commercial release schedules. Under Section 1 of the Sherman Act, agreements among direct rivals that dampen competitive rivalry are treated with severe skepticism and can constitute per se violations. When competing executives publicly express mutual commitments to slow down model releases or "pace the frontier," what research scientists view as responsible ethical restraint can be interpreted by antitrust litigators as an anti-competitive cartel designed to protect incumbent market share and shortchange paying consumers.

On September 18, 2026, four paid consumer subscribers filed a nationwide class-action antitrust lawsuit in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California (**Buist et al. v. Anthropic PBC, OpenAI Inc., SpaceXAI, and Google LLC, Case No. 3:26-cv-10693**). Represented by prominent trial attorney Nick Rowley, the plaintiffs—subscribers to Claude, ChatGPT, Grok, and Gemini—allege that the four leading artificial intelligence developers formed an unlawful horizontal conspiracy to pace and decelerate model development. The complaint centers on the public events of **September 12, 2026**, when Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published his essay *"We Must Pace the Frontier"*, calling for industry-wide coordination. The complaint highlights that OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, SpaceXAI (xAI) CEO Elon Musk, and Google DeepMind Chair Sir Demis Hassabis promptly published public affirmations endorsing coordinated pacing. The lawsuit alleges that the defendants collectively control more than **80% of the U.S. paid AI assistant subscription market** and that agreeing to slow development progress constitutes an illegal agreement to restrict product quality improvements and limit market output. Demanding treble damages and injunctive relief under Section 16 of the Clayton Act, the suit asserts that private technology companies cannot substitute private cartel agreements for individual market competition under the guise of safety.

### 4. MACOM Unveils 448 Gbps/Lane 3.2T Optical Engines, 224 Gbps 1.6T Active Copper, and PCIe 7.0 Silicon Ahead of ECOC 2026 (Category 6)

**Source:** MACOM Technology Solutions / GlobeNewswire / Nasdaq / Converge Digest / Lightwave  
**Event Date:** 2026-09-15  
**Source Date:** 2026-09-15, 08:15 EDT (21:15 KST)  
**Status:** Commercial Semiconductor Architecture Demonstration & Product Line Unveiling  
**Primary URL:** https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/15/3150821/0/en/MACOM-Demonstrates-Technologies-Enabling-the-Future-of-High-Speed-Connectivity-at-ECOC-2026.html

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? In massive distributed artificial intelligence training and inference clusters, physical networking interconnects represent the primary performance boundary governing overall accelerator scaling efficiency. As modern compute nodes transition from 100 Gbps to 200 Gbps SerDes—and prepare for the next inflection point at 400 Gbps SerDes per lane—the high-frequency electrical loss of copper traces across conventional printed circuit boards (PCBs) becomes prohibitive. At a Nyquist frequency of 112 GHz (required for 224 Gbps PAM4) and 224 GHz (for 448 Gbps PAM4), dielectric absorption and the skin effect cause copper trace signal attenuation to exceed -3 dB to -5 dB per inch. Passive Direct Attach Copper (DAC) cables are physically restricted to reach lengths under one meter, leaving cluster architects unable to link accelerators across adjacent racks without introducing severe latency or massive electrical power penalties.

On September 15, 2026, analog and optoelectronic semiconductor pioneer MACOM Technology Solutions Inc. (NASDAQ: MTSI) announced an extensive suite of high-speed connectivity solutions prepared for live demonstration at the European Conference on Optical Communication (ECOC 2026) in Málaga, Spain (Stand 1055). Directly addressing the physical channel-loss wall, MACOM unveiled silicon solutions spanning three critical architectural domains: (1) **3.2 Tbps aggregate Optical Transceiver engines utilizing 448 Gbps per lane optical signaling**, featuring integrated monolithic modulator drivers, high-bandwidth Transimpedance Amplifiers (TIAs), and high-responsivity photodetectors; (2) **1.6 Tbps Active Copper Cable (ACC) equalization chipsets operating at 224 Gbps per lane (112 Gbaud PAM4)**, utilizing analog redrivers and linear equalizers to extend the physical reach of copper cabling inside AI server racks to over 2.5 meters while consuming under 20% of the electrical power of full digital signal processors (DSPs); and (3) dual-path PCIe fabric silicon encompassing **Optical PCIe 6.0 transceivers and Copper PCIe 7.0 physical layer ICs (operating at 128 GT/s)** for low-latency disaggregated memory and accelerator expansion. Additionally, MACOM introduced dedicated driver chipsets engineered for Near-Packaged Optics (NPO) operating at 112 Gbps and 226 Gbps per lane, enabling direct co-location beside compute ASICs.

System-level electrical, thermal, and packaging co-design implications:
1. **Frequency Scaling and Dielectric Insertion Loss (SI):** Transmitting at 224 Gbps and 448 Gbps per lane requires managing transmission lines at millimeter-wave frequencies. Traditional FR-4 PCB materials exhibit intolerable insertion losses at these speeds. System designers must transition accelerator carrier boards to ultra-low-loss dielectric laminates (e.g., Megtron 8 or PTFE-based materials) paired with smooth copper foils (HVLP2/HVLP3) to mitigate surface roughness losses. By integrating MACOM's 224G analog equalizers directly within cable backshells (ACC), server designers can eliminate multiple inches of motherboard trace loss, maintaining high signal-to-noise ratios (SNR) without forcing expensive board-wide retimer installations.
2. **DSP-Free Linear Architecture and Power Integrity (PI):** Full optical DSP chips typically consume 8 to 12 watts inside an 800G transceiver and up to 20 to 25 watts in a 1.6T module. When thousands of optical modules are plugged into a scale-out leaf switch tray, pluggable optics alone can dissipate several kilowatts. MACOM’s linear direct-drive transimpedance amplifiers and modulator drivers interface directly with host SerDes, eliminating the localized DSP. This linear direct-drive architecture reduces transceiver module power consumption by up to 60%, drastically simplifying the rack-level Power Delivery Network (PDN) and mitigating voltage droop on switch motherboard supply rails.
3. **Thermal Density and Optical Engine Packaging:** High-power switch ASICs (drawing 800W to 1,200W) operate at high junction temperatures. Co-locating 448 Gbps/lane optical drivers or NPO engines directly adjacent to the processor package concentrates thermal dissipation. Because laser wavelength stability and photodetector dark current degrade rapidly at elevated temperatures, packaging engineers must employ isolated thermal zones: high-performance vapor chambers and dedicated cold plates isolate the optical engines from the switch ASIC, ensuring laser junction temperatures remain below 70 degrees Celsius while the compute die operates at up to 95 degrees Celsius.

[Host SerDes] ──> [MACOM 448G/Lane Drivers & TIAs] ──> [3.2T Optical Engine / NPO Socket]

  - Components: Integrated linear cable equalizers and low-noise drivers extending copper reach up to 2.5–3.0 meters
  - Power Consumption: Consumes <20% of the electrical power of equivalent retimed DSP architectures
- High-Speed PCIe Fabric Controllers:
  - Optical PCIe 6.0 ICs (64 GT/s PAM4) for multi-rack disaggregated pooling
  - Copper PCIe 7.0 physical-layer ICs (128 GT/s PAM4) for board-level low-latency accelerator fabrics
- Near-Packaged Optics (NPO) Solutions:
  - High-density driver arrays supporting 112 Gbps and 226 Gbps per lane direct-attached configurations

#### [Critical View]
Operating optical channels at 448 Gbps per lane pushes semiconductor physics and packaging tolerances to their absolute limits. At 448 Gbps PAM4, the symbol duration shrinks to approximately 4.4 picoseconds, leaving virtually zero margin for timing jitter, chromatic dispersion, or laser phase noise. Furthermore, while Active Copper Cables (ACC) extend copper reaches inside server racks, their analog-only equalization cannot completely eliminate reflection notches caused by imperfect connector-to-board via transitions. Datacenter operators must enforce extreme manufacturing tolerances across PCB backplanes and cable assemblies, which could lead to lower yields and higher initial unit costs compared to mature 100G/200G hardware.
