### 1. Thinking Machines Lab Releases `Inkling 2 Mini`: A 26.4B Open-Weight Looped Transformer Running Locally on 32GB Laptops and DGX Spark

**Source:** Thinking Machines Lab / Hugging Face / Artificial Analysis / Vals AI  
**Event Date:** 2026-11-20  
**Source Date:** 2026-11-20  
**Status:** Official announcement & open-weight model release  
**Primary URL:** https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFcQU5HSinBFmFrAfIzntZscy_GmvD7EJGP1XU09Rv2a3uLRZCUNclRzE0bWlcEnyb--04AA6Srl56DmJS_GLpB_7FWgBg93KGxKrA1edEMhpQMdanenZCLRRy7nXwEdiMJx8FrHjc=

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? Local AI inference on laptops and desktop AI workstations has hit a memory-bandwidth wall: running larger models requires massive VRAM pools, while smaller models lack the reasoning depth needed for autonomous coding and multi-step agentic loops. On November 20, 2026, Thinking Machines Lab released `Inkling 2 Mini` (alongside a variable-loop research checkpoint, `TML-d24-varloop-research`) under an Apache 2.0 license, introducing an open-weight **looped transformer** designed specifically to fit inside a ~20 GB memory footprint. Instead of expanding parameter count or generating excessively long, token-heavy chains of thought, the 24-layer Mixture-of-Experts (MoE — an architecture that activates only a small subset of specialized sub-networks per token) model runs its middle 12 layers three times per token, reusing the same weights with learned cross-pass gating. By trading extra internal compute per token for a drastically smaller weight footprint, `Inkling 2 Mini` uses just 3.1 billion unique weights per text token (5.4 billion effective active parameters across three passes) out of 26.4 billion total parameters. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, it scores 20.1, matching larger models like `Qwen3.8 27B` while consuming ~2.8x less compute, completing tasks ~4x faster, and costing ~6x less. On Vals AI's CyberBench v1.1, it ties `Claude Opus 5.5` (CVP) at 74.58% while running at less than a quarter of the API test cost ($0.93 versus $4.17). The release immediately drew day-zero support across Hugging Face Transformers, `llama.cpp`, SGLang, vLLM, Ollama, and Apple MLX, signaling a credible architectural path for frontier-grade local agents on 32 GB consumer hardware.

- **context:** Exceeds `Qwen3.6-35B-A3B` (0.430 at NVFP4), `Gemma-4-26B-A4B` (0.159 at NVFP4), and `Qwen3.5-27B` (0.036 at INT4) in early Stanford Hazy Research Lab / Scaling Intelligence Lab local energy-efficiency testing, enabling persistent offline agents on battery- or thermally-constrained devices.

#### [Strategic Impact]
**Technology & Ecosystem:** Looped transformers decouple logical depth from physical parameter storage. By looping layers 6–17 three times with cross-pass residual messaging, the model performs iterative latent reasoning without paying the DRAM capacity tax of a 100B+ parameter checkpoint or the latency penalty of verbose output-token chains.

**Business & Market:** `Inkling 2 Mini` challenges the assumption that serious agentic software engineering and cybersecurity analysis require cloud API subscriptions. Because it fits within 32 GB of unified memory on an Apple Silicon Mac or a single desktop workstation GPU, enterprise teams can run persistent, private coding and triage agents locally at zero marginal API cost (`$0.15/$0.50` per 1M input/output tokens if hosted via API).

**Infrastructure & Hardware:** The model was natively trained in mixed **NVFP4** (NVIDIA 4-bit floating-point format) for routed experts and **MXFP8** (8-bit microscaling format) for the final four layers, eliminating post-training quantization quality loss. Because looped execution reuses weights already resident in on-chip SRAM or L2 cache across passes, it shifts local inference from being purely memory-bandwidth-bound toward higher arithmetic intensity—favoring unified-memory SoCs (Apple M5 Pro/Max) and 128 GB Blackwell-based desktop units (NVIDIA DGX Spark, reaching ~75 tokens/s decode).

**Investment Implication:** Value in edge AI silicon is migrating toward native 4-bit/8-bit hardware support (FP4/FP8 tensor cores) and high-capacity unified memory (>=32 GB baseline). Meanwhile, frontier cloud labs face margin compression on mid-tier reasoning tasks as weight-reusing architectures push "good enough" agentic capability onto existing client hardware.

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? End-to-end encryption only protects messages until they are decrypted and rendered on a user's screen—making the operating system display buffer and accessibility layer the weakest link in the AI agent era. On November 20, 2026, Signal President Meredith Whittaker announced that the encrypted messaging platform is enabling screenshot blocking by default on iOS 27 (via a new "Block Siri AI" setting) and automatically triggering "Screen security" on supported Android devices whenever an AI assistant attempts to read the screen. The defensive move follows an urgent Reuters investigation (conducted with Signal and the AI Now Institute) revealing that Meta's viral `Muse` personal AI agent—downloaded more than 10 million times since September—bypassed Meta's own permissioned connector to read private Signal messages on Android phones via push-notification listeners and on macOS via accessibility and screenshot permissions. ### 3. JEDEC Previews DDR6 DRAM Standard With 74mm Compact Modules, 96-DQ Bus, and 12.8–14.4+ GT/s Speeds for AI Server Density

**Source:** JEDEC Solid State Technology Association (Business Wire) / TrendForce / TechSpot  
**Event Date:** 2026-11-18  
**Source Date:** 2026-11-18  
**Status:** Standards organization technical preview (under development)  
**Primary URL:** https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFOYc7vcp775Q7OU060ia8DuINgD1LdgYofZAjFz2nxcV8y1WBTRvZyhtFcX9JzPC6LMuOYGikP1D3KCDYFFiL9G8_zAa_r21MuApUnxSw8NDsIUjpV0CjT3aSV1I2CqJSHehnkAtNoKqIX-2FdqcDgYMx3w2RLn7WkLnauxYwbYI5D1pyez_2nK1q5NsZ7yjc0WYiXmWGRopZijdzaCqZRUaxJmx4jyvJAgMHJLBc-UPj2VwydkuzvVRvNZKs49fWRhsGT3EP64aCl1HHAXGD1tlyi3bq8xQig4Juo69eb-M_WqMRucw3Oj0-H8uclz2aRrZj8Atq2vw==

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? While High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) grabs headlines on AI accelerators, agentic AI workflows, Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) CPU-offloading, and large-context inference increasingly choke on host CPU memory capacity and bandwidth—where conventional 133 mm DDR5 DIMMs have hit physical motherboard crowding and signal-integrity limits. On November 18, 2026, JEDEC formally previewed the next-generation DDR6 DRAM standard, outlining the largest structural overhaul of main system memory in decades. Rather than merely cranking clock frequencies, JEDEC is simultaneously widening the data bus by 50% (from 64 to 96 data lines, or DQ), boosting initial Registered DIMM (RDIMM) transfer rates by 39% over DDR5's 9.2 GT/s ceiling to **12.8 GT/s** (with a roadmap to **14.4 GT/s** and beyond), and shrinking physical module length by **44% from 133 mm to 74 mm**. Internally, DDR6 introduces a **pseudo-channel architecture** (splitting each DRAM package into independently addressable sub-channels), doubles the memory bank count, adopts **VSS-terminated I/O** (referencing signals to ground rather than supply voltage to cut switching power), and supports monolithic DRAM dies up to **128 Gb (16 GB per die)** alongside package die stacking. JEDEC explicitly noted that DDR6 is "built for the data center," targeting AI inferencing and agentic AI workloads that require terabytes of high-bandwidth capacity physically close to the CPU.

#### [Strategic Impact]
**Technology & Ecosystem:** Shrinking server DIMM length from 133 mm to 74 mm—nearly matching laptop SO-DIMM length—fundamentally changes server motherboard topology. Board architects can pack significantly more memory channels and modules tightly around massive multi-chiplet server CPUs (e.g., future AMD EPYC and Intel Xeon Diamond Rapids successors), reducing trace lengths while supporting RDIMM, UDIMM, and Compression Attached Memory Module (CAMM) form factors.

**Business & Market:** As HBM prices remain elevated and supply constrained, high-capacity DDR6 systems paired with CXL memory pooling give hyperscalers a lower-cost tier for storing massive KV caches and inactive MoE expert weights near the CPU. DRAM leaders (Samsung, SK hynix, Micron) gain a high-ASP upgrade cycle centered on 128 Gb dies and die-stacked packages.

**Infrastructure & Hardware:** Moving to a 96-DQ bus at 12.8–14.4+ GT/s inside a 74 mm connector footprint imposes severe **signal integrity (SI)** and **PCB routing** challenges. Packing 50% more high-speed data lines into 44% less linear module edge demands ultra-fine-pitch connectors, higher server motherboard layer counts, low-loss dielectric PCB laminates (to curb insertion loss above 6.4 GHz Nyquist frequency), tighter impedance matching, and localized airflow/liquid cooling over densely packed 74 mm DIMM banks.

**Investment Implication:** Beyond memory makers (SK hynix, Samsung, Micron), JEDEC's 74 mm / 96-DQ pivot creates structural tailwinds for high-speed connector vendors (Amphenol, TE Connectivity, Molex), low-loss CCL/PCB substrate suppliers, DDR6 RCD/PMIC/clock-driver silicon vendors (Rambus, Montage, Renesas), and SI/PI simulation EDA tools.

### 4. Scaling AI Accelerators Past 2,000A Forces Shift From Lateral to Vertical Power Delivery (VPD), Halving PDN Loop Inductance

**Source:** arXiv (`arXiv:2611.21982`) / Semiconductor Engineering  
**Event Date:** 2026-11-16  
**Source Date:** 2026-11-19  
**Status:** Academic paper & industry engineering synthesis  
**Primary URL:** https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQG9-h34AiLKIDFjvyx85mCD1T4UiWhP2pLcqDnq4sLi8CuPVHIuDo_N2VulnFCcb1jekAV9KiPHLI7t28lhtmwgnCpWfaMVAqiO-NojlUDv5wErVx2aG3oX7A==

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? As next-generation AI GPUs and custom ASICs push power consumption beyond 600 W to 1,000–3,000 W while core operating voltages drop near **0.7 V**, processor current demand is climbing past **1,000 A to 2,000 A and beyond**. At these currents, traditional **lateral power delivery (LPD)**—placing Voltage Regulator Modules (VRMs, the DC-DC converters that step rack bus voltage down to sub-1V silicon rails) along the perimeter of the processor on the top side of the PCB—suffers unsustainable resistive losses (I-squared-R heating) and voltage droop during rapid AI workload transients. A technical study published November 16, 2026 (`arXiv:2611.21982` by Missouri University of Science and Technology), alongside a November 19 industry synthesis in *Semiconductor Engineering* (citing Intel Foundry, ASE PowerSiP, Analog Devices, and onsemi's Ferric acquisition), quantifies the unavoidable architectural transition to **Vertical Power Delivery (VPD)**. In VPD, VRMs are relocated from the PCB periphery to the **landside** directly beneath the processor die (or integrated inside the package substrate as Integrated Voltage Regulators, IVRs). Power-integrity (PI) simulations in `arXiv:2611.21982` show that moving a VRM from 50 mm laterally away to directly beneath the die **cuts PDN loop inductance by nearly 50%**, substantially lowering the low-frequency anti-resonant impedance peak. Combined with upstream 800 VDC rack buses and single-stage 48V-to-<1V conversion, VPD is transforming power integrity from a board-level afterthought into a tightly coupled die–interposer–package–PCB–thermal co-design discipline led increasingly by OSATs (Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test providers).

#### [Strategic Impact]
**Technology & Ecosystem:** Power delivery can no longer be designed in silos. When current flows vertically through the PCB and package substrate directly under the die shadow, the same vertical z-axis must simultaneously accommodate thousands of high-current power/ground vias, high-speed SerDes/PCIe escape routing, decoupling capacitors, and embedded magnetic inductors—forcing EDA tools (Synopsys, Cadence, Ansys) and OSATs (ASE, SPIL) to run unified electromagnetic, power-integrity, and thermo-mechanical co-simulations.

**Business & Market:** VPD is driving vertical M&A and ecosystem consolidation across power semiconductors and packaging—evidenced by onsemi's November 2026 acquisition of IVR pioneer Ferric, ASE's PowerSiP platform expansion, and Delta Electronics' SC26 showcase of 3,000W compute + 500W VPD double-sided cold plates. Power-module density and embedded passives are becoming primary differentiators alongside HBM bandwidth.

**Infrastructure & Hardware:** Tracing the full physical chain—**800 VDC facility/rack bus → 48V intermediate rail → single-stage landside VRM → <1V GPU core**—shows how chip-level current scaling rewrites server hardware:
1. **Board & PCB:** High-current lateral copper planes are replaced by vertical via arrays directly beneath the BGA/socket, freeing top-side PCB real estate for high-speed signaling while demanding stricter power/ground plane stack-up ordering to control mid- and high-frequency PDN impedance.
2. **Thermal:** Placing 300–500 W of active VRM switching silicon on the PCB backside directly under a 1,000–3,000 W GPU blocks passive backside cooling, necessitating **double-sided direct liquid cooling** (cold plates clamping both the top GPU IHS and bottom VPD regulators).

**Investment Implication:** Capital is rotating toward integrated power stages (GaN/SiC and high-frequency monolithic VRMs), embedded substrate capacitor/inductor suppliers, high-thermal-conductivity PCB/substrate materials, and double-sided liquid-cooling manifold vendors.

#### [CRITICAL VIEW]
**Demonstrated capability vs. production readiness:** While PDN physics clearly favors VPD, commercial mass deployment faces three unresolved mechanical and electrical bottlenecks identified in `arXiv:2611.21982` and *Semiconductor Engineering*. First, moving the VRM beneath the die only cures *low-frequency* impedance peaks; *mid- and high-frequency* voltage droop during sharp LLM prefill/decode power spikes still degrades catastrophically unless expensive embedded capacitors/inductors are integrated inside the organic substrate. Second, tight mechanical height limits beneath GPU sockets (often <2 mm clearance, or requiring PCB cutouts) constrain inductor size and thermal efficiency. Third, because OSATs and fabless chip houses must now exchange proprietary PDN and thermal models across company boundaries without leaking process secrets, multi-vendor co-design workflows remain immature.

### [SUMMARY & FORWARD-LOOKING OUTLOOK]

1. **What changed today?**
   On the software side, Thinking Machines Lab proved that **weight-reusing looped transformers** (`Inkling 2 Mini`) can pack agentic-grade reasoning into a 20 GB local checkpoint, while Signal's default screenshot blocks exposed an acute **trust-boundary collision** between OS-integrated AI agents and app-level encryption. On the hardware front, JEDEC previewed a **44%-shorter, 50%-wider DDR6 memory geometry**, and new PDN research quantified why 2,000A+ accelerators must abandon lateral PCB power delivery for **vertical landside VPD**.

2. **What structural trend is becoming stronger?**
   **Compute-vs.-Memory Decoupling at Every Tier:** In models, looped layers trade extra arithmetic reuse for smaller memory footprints (`Inkling 2 Mini`). On server boards, JEDEC is shrinking DIMMs to 74 mm while expanding DQ width 50% to feed memory-starved inference CPUs. In packages, VRMs are migrating vertically under the die to eliminate millimeter-scale copper resistance.

3. **What bottleneck is emerging?**
   - **Physical Z-Axis Crowding:** Shoving 96-DQ memory lanes onto 74 mm modules and stacking landside VRMs, decoupling passives, and double-sided liquid cold plates beneath 2,000A+ dies leaves virtually zero uncontested PCB or substrate volume.
   - **Agent Observability & OS Security:** Both internal looped reasoning (invisible to chain-of-thought monitors) and OS-level screen-reading agents (invisible to app sandboxes) bypass legacy software auditing layers.

4. **Which layer of the AI stack may become strategically more important?**
   **Package-Board-Thermal Co-Design (OSATs + Power/Interconnect Substrates)** and **Client Unified-Memory Hardware (>=32 GB)**. As OSATs mediate between die designers, embedded-passive vendors, and 800V/48V board power engineers, packaging and PDN integration are becoming as decisive as transistor node shrinks. Simultaneously, >=32 GB local unified-memory workstations (`DGX Spark`, `Apple Silicon`) are emerging as viable enterprise execution nodes.

5. **What should be monitored next?**
   - Whether Apple, Google, and Microsoft introduce standardized developer opt-out APIs for OS-level agents (`Siri AI`, `Gemini Intelligence`, `Recall`) in early 2027 OS updates.
   - Adoption of looped transformer recipes (`TML-d24-varloop-research`) across open-weight coding and robotics labs.
   - JEDEC JC-42 committee convergence on DDR6 CAMM versus 74 mm RDIMM pinouts and connector pitches.
   - Commercial tape-outs and double-sided liquid-cooled board qualifications for >1,000W accelerators using landside VPD/IVRs.
