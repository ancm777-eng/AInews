### 4. Open CPX MSA Releases Specification 1.0 for Pluggable 7.2 Tbps Co-Packaged Optics Ahead of ECOC 2026 (Category 6)

**Source:** Open CPX Multi-Source Agreement Group / Photonics Spectra / Lightwave / Business Wire / Converge Digest  
**Event Date:** 2026-09-17 (Release) / 2026-09-18 (Global Availability & ECOC Showcase)  
**Source Date:** 2026-09-18  
**Status:** Official Open Industry Technical Specification Release (Specification 1.0)  
**Primary URL:** https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260917190669/en/Open-CPX-MSA-Group-Releases-Specification-for-High-Density-Pluggable-CPONPO

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? Datacenter switching and accelerator fabrics are confronting the physical breakdown of copper interconnects. At 200 Gbps per lane signaling (PAM4), electrical channel loss across standard high-speed PCB copper traces (such as Megtron-8) exceeds 30 to 40 dB per meter, physically restricting passive copper reaches to less than one meter and rendering intra-rack copper backplanes impractical for high-density AI clusters. Co-Packaged Optics (CPO) and Near-Packaged Optics (NPO)—which relocate optical engines directly adjacent to host switch or compute ASICs—eliminate copper channel loss by reducing host electrical traces to millimeters. However, high-volume deployment has been paralyzed for a decade because CPO implementations were entirely proprietary, non-standardized, and non-serviceable: if an integrated laser or optical modulator failed, the entire expensive host ASIC and substrate had to be discarded.

On September 17–18, 2026, ahead of the European Conference on Optical Communication (ECOC 2026) in Málaga, Spain, the **Open CPX Multi-Source Agreement (MSA) Group** announced the general availability of **Specification 1.0 for Co-Packaged and Near-Package Connectors and Optics Modules**. Formed by leading optical and semiconductor players—including Ciena, Coherent, Marvell, Molex, Samtec, TeraHop, Credo, Intel, HPE, and Dell—the 78-page specification establishes the industry's first open, standardized form factor for pluggable CPO and NPO optical modules. The architecture defines an ultra-compact, mixed-media socket capable of delivering up to **7.2 Tbps of bidirectional bandwidth per module using 200 Gbps per lane optical and electrical signaling** (supporting both 6.4T and 7.2T configurations). Crucially, Open CPX solves the serviceability dilemma: modules plug directly into standardized host board sockets without requiring custom compression hardware, allowing failed optical engines to be replaced in the field while supporting both internal and external laser sources. In lockstep with the specification's release, photonic computing developer **Lightmatter** joined the Open CPX MSA and introduced the **Passage L20 CPX**, the industry's first bidirectional (BiDi) Open CPX optical engine, which transmits and receives over a single fiber to cut required cluster cabling and optical connectors by 50% across 512-GPU scale-up pods.

System-level electrical, thermal, and packaging co-design implications:
1. **Channel Insertion Loss & SerDes Power Reduction:** By mounting standardized Open CPX sockets directly adjacent to the host ASIC (within 50 to 100 mm), electrical trace lengths are minimized, reducing channel insertion loss from >30 dB (typical for front-panel pluggables) down to sub-10 dB at 56 GHz Nyquist (200G PAM4). This allows host SerDes to operate in low-power, ultra-short reach (XSR/VSR) modes, slashing SerDes power consumption by up to 60% compared to long-reach front-panel PHYs.
2. **Standardized High-Density RF Pinout & Socket Dynamics:** Open CPX defines an ultra-fine-pitch detachable socket utilizing up to 300 pins (12 rows by 25 positions) dedicated to high-speed RF differential pairs, power rails, and Common Management Interface Specification (CMIS) control lines. The connector maintains impedance matching to 100 ohms differential while withstanding repeated robotic blind-mate insertions without degrading high-frequency return loss (sdd11) or inducing micro-bump solder joint fatigue on the host substrate.
3. **Liquid-Cooling Thermal Co-Design:** In high-power switch systems (such as 102.4 Tbps and 204.8 Tbps chassis dissipating several kilowatts), optical engines sit within the thermal plume of the host ASIC. The Open CPX specification defines dedicated thermal envelope interfaces for direct liquid cold-plate integration, ensuring the optical silicon and laser diodes operate below 70°C even when adjacent compute silicon operates at 100°C junction temperatures.

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem:** Establishes the first consensus multi-vendor open standard for socketable CPO/NPO, transitioning the optical industry from proprietary prototypes to a standardized merchant component ecosystem analogous to QSFP-DD.
- **Business & Market:** Mitigates hyperscaler supply-chain concentration; system builders can source interoperable 6.4T and 7.2T optical modules from competing suppliers (Coherent, Marvell, Lightmatter, Samtec) without redesigning underlying ASIC baseboards.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware:** Enables system vendors to manufacture 102.4 Tbps and 204.8 Tbps AI switch chassis and multi-rack scale-up accelerator pods, lowering intra-cluster interconnect power while mitigating cable congestion via bidirectional optics.
- **Investment Implication:** Overweight precision high-speed connector manufacturers (Samtec, Molex, Amphenol), optical DSP and silicon photonics innovators (Marvell, Credo, Lightmatter), and liquid-cooled AI switch ODMs; monitor field-failure RMA rates for first-generation CPX sockets.

#### [Technical Deep Dive]

- Participating Ecosystem: Ciena (Chief Editor Peter Winzer), Coherent, Marvell, Molex, Samtec, TeraHop, Credo, Lightmatter, Intel, Dell, HPE, Astera Labs, Amphenol, Viavi Solutions

#### [Critical View]
While Open CPX Specification 1.0 provides a vital roadmap, detachable optical sockets introduce severe mechanical and contamination challenges on high-volume factory floors. Operating 300-pin high-frequency connectors alongside high-density multi-fiber optical ferrules requires sub-micron alignment tolerances under sustained mechanical clamping forces. In dense datacenter environments subjected to thermal cycling between 25°C and 70°C, slight coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE) mismatches between the connector plastic, silicon photonic die, and organic host board can induce micro-fretting corrosion on RF contacts and axial misalignment on optical interfaces, risking signal loss or high bit-error rates (BER) over multi-year deployments.
