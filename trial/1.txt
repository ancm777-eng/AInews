### 4. Ayar Labs Secures $150M ($650M Total in 2026) and Partners with Wiwynn to Transition Co-Packaged Optics into High-Volume Manufacturing (Category 6)

**Source:** Ayar Labs / Wiwynn Corporation / Semiconductor Today / Unite.AI  
**Event Date:** 2026-09-10  
**Source Date:** 2026-09-10, 09:00 EDT / 22:00 KST  
**Status:** Strategic Commercial Financing & Rack-Scale Optical Hardware Manufacturing Pact  
**Primary URL:** https://ayarlabs.com/press-releases/ayar-labs-expands-2026-funding-wiwynn-rack-scale-cpo/

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? As AI training clusters expand to hundreds of thousands of accelerators interconnected across multi-gigawatt facilities, system performance is hitting the physical "copper interconnect wall." At high signaling rates (224 Gbps and 448 Gbps PAM4 per electrical lane), electrical copper cabling experiences severe high-frequency attenuation, channel loss, and dispersion beyond a distance of one to two meters. To route signals across server rows, datacenters rely on active copper cables with power-hungry retimers or discrete pluggable optical transceivers. Pluggable transceivers, however, consume up to 25% to 30% of total server power and create severe airflow and thermal obstacles at the server faceplate. Co-Packaged Optics (CPO)—placing silicon photonics optical engines directly on the substrate package alongside the accelerator or switch silicon—eliminates parasitic electrical trace losses, slashes I/O power dissipation by up to 80%, and enables latency-critical scale-up fabrics to extend seamlessly beyond single racks.

On September 10, 2026, silicon photonics optical connectivity leader Ayar Labs announced that it has closed an additional **$150 million in growth funding, lifting total primary capital raised in 2026 to $650 million**. In the same disclosure, Ayar Labs announced a strategic investment and rack-scale manufacturing partnership with Wiwynn, a premier cloud IT infrastructure and server provider for global hyperscalers. Wiwynn joins Ayar Labs' strategic backing syndicate, which already includes NVIDIA, AMD, Intel, Alchip, and MediaTek. The capital infusion directly accelerates Ayar Labs' transition to high-volume manufacturing (HVM) for its proprietary **TeraPHY optical I/O chiplets and SuperNova multi-wavelength off-package laser sources**, while underwriting the opening of an advanced Bengaluru Design Center to expand foundry and packaging co-development. By co-developing standardized CPO server trays with Wiwynn, the partnership creates a direct manufacturing pathway to replace high-power copper DACs (Direct Attach Copper) and PCIe retimers with high-density optical fabrics across exascale datacenter rows.

System-level electrical, thermal, and packaging co-design implications:
1. **Direct Substrate Integration & Parasitic Removal:** In standard pluggable optical systems, high-speed SerDes signals must travel from the accelerator die through package micro-bumps, traverse long organic substrate traces, pass through a PCB socket, route across 10 to 18 inches of low-loss PCB material (e.g., Megtron 8), and pass through a mechanical cage connector into a transceiver module. This path incurs 15 to 25 dB of electrical insertion loss at 56 GHz Nyquist. Ayar Labs’ TeraPHY chiplet is co-packaged directly onto the accelerator substrate using fine-pitch 2.5D micro-bumps or UCIe interfaces. This collapses the electrical transit distance to millimeters, dropping high-frequency channel insertion loss to under 2 dB and eliminating the need for aggressive, power-hungry Digital Signal Processor (DSP) equalization.
2. **Thermal Decoupling via External SuperNova Laser Sources:** A primary failure mode of co-packaged optics has been laser diode thermal degradation: indium phosphide (InP) distributed feedback (DFB) laser diodes experience severe quantum efficiency loss and rapid Mean Time Between Failures (MTBF) degradation when exposed to temperatures above 60°C. Stacking lasers directly beside 1,000W+ AI accelerators operating at 95°C to 105°C junction temperatures is unviable. Ayar Labs resolves this by physically decoupling the optical power source: the SuperNova laser module resides outside the compute chassis in a cool-running rack zone, transmitting continuous-wave (CW) light through multi-fiber optical blind-mate connectors into the on-package TeraPHY silicon micro-ring modulators.
3. **Power Delivery Network (PDN) & Faceplate Airflow Relief:** High-density 120kW+ AI server racks (such as NVLink-style architectures) face acute faceplate thermal blockages where rows of pluggable optical modules impede cold-aisle intake airflow. Transitioning to CPO optical fibers clears physical faceplate space, optimizing air or direct liquid cooling (DLC) cold-plate plumbing. Electrically, reducing I/O energy from 15–20 picojoules per bit (pJ/bit) for pluggable optics down to sub-3 pJ/bit for TeraPHY saves dozens of kilowatts of continuous power at the rack level, freeing dynamic electrical headroom for core tensor compute.

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem:** Accelerates the commercial maturity of Co-Packaged Optics from experimental demonstrations into standardized hyperscaler server manufacturing, establishing silicon photonics chiplets as a standard architectural tier alongside compute and HBM.
- **Business & Market:** Solidifies Ayar Labs as the definitive independent optical I/O supplier backed by all major accelerator vendors (NVIDIA, AMD, Intel), giving hyperscalers an alternative to proprietary vendor lock-in.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware:** Enables exascale clusters to expand scale-up fabric domains across dozens of server racks without hitting copper distance limits, reducing latency and avoiding costly multi-tier optical transceiver layers.
- **Investment Implication:** Overweight silicon photonics foundries, optical fiber array connector manufacturers, and specialized PIC testing equipment; monitor capital expenditure transitions from discrete pluggable transceivers toward CPO modules.

#### [Critical View]
While Co-Packaged Optics provides compelling power and bandwidth advantages, field serviceability and manufacturing yields remain significant obstacles. In standard pluggable architectures, a degraded transceiver can be hot-swapped in seconds on the datacenter floor. Under CPO, an optical laser modulator defect on the packaging substrate or damaged optical fiber pigtail can scrap an entire multi-thousand-dollar accelerator board. Furthermore, cleanroom optical connector mating requires zero particle contamination: microscopic dust on a blind-mate optical connector can cause severe optical insertion loss across multi-terabit links, demanding pristine maintenance protocols in high-throughput enterprise datacenters.
