### 4. Vicor Commits to 1 Million Sq Ft Fab Expansion to Scale Vertical Power Delivery (VPD) for 1kW+ AI Accelerators (Category 6)

**Source:** Vicor Corporation / GlobeNewswire / Semiconductor Engineering / EDN / vTools Events  
**Event Date:** 2026-09-11  
**Source Date:** 2026-09-11, 09:00 EDT (2026-09-11, 22:00 KST)  
**Status:** Corporate Manufacturing Expansion & Power Architecture Roadmap  
**Primary URL:** https://www.vicorpower.com/press-room/vicor-acquires-sites-for-additional-chip-fabs

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? Leading-edge artificial intelligence accelerators (such as multi-die GPUs and custom hyperscaler ASICs) are pushing thermal design power (TDP) beyond 1,000 watts toward 1,500 watts per socket. At operating core supply voltages below 0.8 volts, these processors draw massive dynamic currents exceeding 1,200 to 2,000 amperes. In conventional server board layouts, multi-phase Voltage Regulator Modules (VRMs) are placed laterally adjacent to the processor package on the printed circuit board (PCB). However, lateral power delivery creates an acute physical bottleneck: routing 1,500A through lateral copper motherboard traces incurs severe parasitic loop inductance and resistive voltage loss (IR drop), consuming up to 15% to 20% of total delivered power as wasted heat and occupying over 50% of the surface area around the socket. This lateral congestion physically blocks high-speed trace fan-out for High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) interfaces and 224G/448G SerDes channels.

On September 11, 2026, power semiconductor leader Vicor Corporation (NASDAQ: VICR) announced a major physical manufacturing expansion to scale production of its proprietary **Vertical Power Delivery (VPD)** architectures. Vicor acquired a 334,000-square-foot facility on 66 acres in Merrimack, New Hampshire, alongside a 54-acre industrial parcel in Hooksett, New Hampshire. The two sites will support the construction of **ChiP Fab-2 and Fab-3**, expanding Vicor’s total high-density power module manufacturing cleanroom footprint by nearly **93,000 square meters (1 million square feet)**. The gigascale capacity expansion is specifically designed to eliminate supply-chain chokepoints for hyperscale cloud operators and AI processor OEMs transitioning to Vertical Power Delivery. Vicor’s VPD architecture circumvents lateral board routing by mounting ultra-thin Current Multiplier modules directly underneath the processor package on the bottom side of the PCB or directly onto the substrate interposer. Power is delivered vertically through short vertical vias directly into the processor core, collapsing electrical path resistance by up to **85%**, eliminating lateral voltage droop during steep dynamic load transients (di/dt), and clearing 100% of top-side motherboard real estate adjacent to the chip for high-density signal interconnects.

System-level electrical, thermal, and packaging co-design implications:
1. **Vertical Current Multiplication & PDN Impedance:** In standard 48V-to-0.8V power distribution, stepping down voltage near the board edge requires routing thousands of amperes laterally through complex multi-layer PCB copper planes. Vicor’s Factorized Power Architecture (FPA) routes 48V/54V directly to a Point-of-Load Pre-Regulator Module (PRM) and then delivers a factorized bus voltage vertically into Voltage Transformation Modules (VTMs) mounted beneath the BGA socket. Stepping down voltage directly beneath the die slashes the power distribution network (PDN) loop impedance to sub-milliohm levels, mitigating catastrophic voltage droop during rapid tensor core step-load transients.
2. **Top-Side Board Congestion & High-Speed Signal Integrity (SI):** Advanced packaging configurations (such as TSMC CoWoS or Intel EMIB) integrate multiple HBM3E/HBM4 stacks requiring thousands of fine-pitch escape routing traces. Moving the VRM power stages to the board underside eliminates lateral power busbars from the top PCB layers, freeing perimeter board real estate for unimpeded, short-reach differential trace routing to 224 Gbps SerDes connectors and optical transceiver cages.
3. **Thermal Co-Design & Dual-Sided Liquid Cooling:** While VPD eliminates lateral resistive power losses, mounting high-power switching modules directly underneath a 1,000W+ compute die concentrates thermal dissipation into a unified vertical column. Server architects must implement dual-sided cooling solutions: a primary cold plate on top of the processor package and a secondary cold plate or localized vapor chamber on the PCB underside to cool the vertical power stages, requiring precise mechanical clamping tolerances and specialized thermal interface materials (TIMs) to prevent board warpage under thermal cycling.

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem:** Accelerates the industry-wide transition from legacy lateral multi-phase VRMs to Vertical Power Delivery, establishing vertical integration as the mandatory power delivery standard for sub-0.8V, exascale AI computing silicon.
- **Business & Market:** Solidifies Vicor’s intellectual property and manufacturing moat in high-density 48V/54V conversion, positioning the company to capture high-margin content across next-generation hyperscale AI server motherboards and merchant accelerator modules.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware:** Enables datacenter operators to deploy 1,000W to 1,500W accelerator sockets within 120kW+ liquid-cooled racks without suffering debilitating PDN power dissipation or board-level thermal runaway.
- **Investment Implication:** Overweight vertical power semiconductor specialists, advanced multi-layer ceramic capacitor (MLCC) suppliers, and dual-sided liquid cooling manifold manufacturers; monitor hyperscaler transition roadmaps from 12V/48V lateral VRMs to integrated vertical power.

#### [Critical View]
While Vertical Power Delivery slashes electrical losses and frees board surface area, it introduces substantial manufacturing complexity and mechanical reliability risks. Mounting power stages directly underneath the BGA socket complicates automated surface-mount (SMT) assembly, inspection, and rework: a defective solder joint beneath the power module cannot be visually inspected and requires specialized high-resolution X-ray laminography. Furthermore, vertical power modules subject the underside of the PCB to localized high-frequency switching vibration and thermal stress; without balanced mechanical stiffeners and matched coefficients of thermal expansion (CTE), board warpage during thermal reflow can crack fine-pitch BGA balls.
