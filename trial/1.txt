## 4. Celestica Details DS6005: 102.4 Tbps Liquid-Cooled Co-Packaged Optics AI Switch with Broadcom Tomahawk 6-Davisson Eliminates Pluggable DSP Bottlenecks

**Source:** Igor's Lab / Converge Digest / OCP Global Summit Technical Proceedings  
**Event Date:** 2026-10-19  
**Source Date:** 2026-10-19  
**Status:** Technical specification disclosure / Engineering architecture deep-dive  
**Primary URL:** https://www.igorslab.de/en/celestica-ds6005-102-4-tb-s-with-co-packaged-optics/

### [Overview]
Why does this matter? As AI training and inference clusters scale past 100,000 accelerators, the physical network fabric faces a catastrophic power and signal-integrity brick wall. In conventional switch architectures operating at 1.6 Terabits per second (1.6T) per port, high-speed 224 Gbps PAM4 SerDes (serializer/deserializer) electrical signals must traverse lengthy copper printed circuit board (PCB) traces to reach front-panel pluggable optical transceivers (such as OSFP modules). At these extreme frequencies, electrical channel loss, jitter, and crosstalk explode, requiring power-hungry digital signal processors (DSPs) in every transceiver module that can consume up to 30% of total network power. Today, following technical panels at the OCP Global Summit, complete hardware architecture details were disclosed for the Celestica DS6005, a groundbreaking 102.4 Tbps data center switch built for Open Rack v3 (ORv3) AI fabrics. Built on Broadcom's 3nm Tomahawk 6–Davisson (BCM78919) ASIC, the DS6005 integrates silicon photonics optical engines directly onto the switch packaging substrate—a technique known as Co-Packaged Optics (CPO). By removing conventional front-panel optical transceivers and discrete retimer DSPs, the DS6005 shortens high-speed copper traces to mere millimeters, dramatically slashing bit error rates (BER), reducing link latency, and cutting interconnect thermal dissipation. Squeezing 64 x 1.6 TbE ports into a compact 1.5OU fully liquid-cooled chassis, the system sets the physical architectural blueprint for next-generation gigawatt AI data factories.

### [Strategic Impact]
- **Technology & Ecosystem:** Marks the long-awaited commercial industrialization of silicon photonics and Co-Packaged Optics in open networking. Validates the OCP ORv3 disaggregated hardware paradigm by standardizing field-replaceable external laser sources (ELSFP) and SONiC open network operating system orchestration.
- **Business & Market:** Directly threatens traditional pluggable optical transceiver vendors (e.g., legacy optical module assemblers) by shifting the high-margin optical conversion layer directly onto the semiconductor packaging supply chain led by Broadcom and TSMC.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware:** Bridges the hardware chain: **3nm Compute ASIC → Packaging Substrate CPO → Elimination of Retimer PCBs → 100% Direct Liquid Cooling → 48V–54V Busbar Power Delivery → 100,000-Accelerator Cluster Scalability**.
- **Investment Implication:** Accelerates capital expenditure migration toward advanced optical packaging foundries, high-purity laser diode manufacturers, and data-center liquid-cooling infrastructure providers.

### [Technical Deep Dive]
- **Total Switching Capacity:** 102.4 Tbps non-blocking throughput (Source: https://cls.celestica.com / OCP Global Summit)
- **Port Density:** 64 x 1.6 TbE optical ports terminated via 16 MMC-16 fiber connectors
- **Core Switching ASIC:** Broadcom Tomahawk 6–Davisson (BCM78919), fabricated on TSMC 3nm process
- **SerDes Architecture:** 512 lanes of 200 Gbps PAM4 SerDes integrated natively onto the substrate
- **Optics Implementation:** Co-Packaged Optics (CPO); optical engines co-located on multi-chip module substrate beside the switch silicon
- **Laser Source & Reliability:** 16 front-panel field-replaceable External Laser Small Form-Factor Pluggable (ELSFP) modules (Continuous-Wave lasers kept separate from hot switch ASIC)
- **Form Factor & Rack Standard:** 1.5OU form factor designed specifically for OCP Open Rack Version 3 (ORv3)
- **Power Delivery Network (PDN):** 46V to 56V DC direct busbar clip at 76A max, eliminating intermediate AC-DC conversion within the tray
- **Thermal Architecture:** 100% Direct Liquid Cooled (no air fans inside the switching envelope)
- **Deployment Status:** Engineering demonstration and sampling platform; commercial deployments slated for early 2027.

### [Layer Structure Data]
- Level | Component | Role & Technical Specification
- Switch Core | Broadcom Tomahawk 6 (BCM78919) | 3nm process, 102.4 Tbps packet processing, 512x 200G SerDes
- Optical Interconnect | Co-Packaged Photonic Engines | Direct electro-optical conversion on package substrate, eliminating DSP retimers
- Optical Power | 16x ELSFP Laser Modules | Continuous-wave laser power fed from front panel; field-swappable during operation
- Optical Interface | 16x MMC-16 Connectors | Very Small Form Factor (VSFF) connectors providing 64x 1.6 TbE channels
- Thermal Management | Monolithic Cold Plate Loop | Direct-to-chip liquid cooling absorbing >1,500W dissipated across ASIC and optics
- Power Architecture | 48V/54V Direct Busbar Clip | Direct rack busbar engagement at 76A, eliminating PSU heat inside chassis

### [Critical View]
While Co-Packaged Optics promises monumental efficiency gains, commercial mass adoption faces two stubborn engineering hurdles: field serviceability and substrate yield. While Celestica brilliantly mitigates laser burnout risks by isolating the lasers into hot-swappable front-panel ELSFP modules, any physical failure in an integrated optical engine still requires replacing the entire 102.4 Tbps switch assembly. Furthermore, moving from air to mandatory full-liquid cooling in a 1.5OU network chassis requires data center facilities to maintain strict dielectric/water coolant purity, zero-drip blind-mate quick disconnects, and specialized plumbing inside traditionally dry network distribution racks.

- context: Doubles the radix and bandwidth density of previous-generation 51.2 Tbps switches while eliminating multi-watt transceiver DSP overhead.

## [SUMMARY & FORWARD-LOOKING OUTLOOK]

### 1. What Changed Today?
The artificial intelligence landscape witnessed a simultaneous convergence across interactive generative media, hyper-efficient agent economics, and photonic physical infrastructure:
- **Interactive Video Real-Time Shift:** fal and Black Forest Labs transformed generative video from an asynchronous multi-minute waiting game into a synchronous 10-second interactive loop with FLUX 3 Max (1,139 Elo, 22x faster).
- **Agentic Cost-Per-Token Shock:** MiniMax commoditized long-horizon agent execution by releasing M3.1 Flash (Space Bunny) at $0.15/1M input and 100 tok/s, tearing down enterprise adoption barriers.
- **Commercial Creative Suite Disruption:** Krea 3 and ComfyUI v0.42.0 introduced deterministic, sub-8-second 2K image editing with product LoRA support, attacking Photoshop's commercial photography monopoly.
- **CPO Hardware Industrialization:** Celestica and Broadcom proved that 102.4 Tbps AI fabrics have physically outgrown copper traces, establishing liquid-cooled Co-Packaged Optics (DS6005) as the blueprint for 1.6T networking.

### 2. What Structural Trend Is Becoming Stronger?
The decoupling of AI value from generic, dense frontier foundation models toward **specialized inference optimization and sparse physical routing** is solidifying. Whether in foundation models using 23B active parameters out of 428B (MiniMax), video pipelines executing distilled flow-matching kernels (FLUX 3 Max), or network switches migrating electrons into photons directly on package substrates (Tomahawk 6-Davisson), the entire AI industry is ruthlessly optimizing energy, latency, and copper resistance per token generated.

### 3. What Bottleneck Is Emerging?
A severe **thermal and packaging reliability divergence** is emerging between semiconductor capabilities and field operations. While switch chips can now process 102.4 Tbps and video models can synthesize 24 fps dynamically, operating 100% liquid-cooled switch chassis with fiber-optic ribbon routing inside dirty, real-world data centers introduces complex mechanical, fluid, and connector maintenance overhead that legacy IT staff are unprepared to handle.

### 4. Which Layer of the AI Stack May Become Strategically More Important?
The **Advanced Optical Packaging and Co-Packaged Substrate Layer**. As copper SerDes reaches its physical physics limits at 224 Gbps and 448 Gbps, whoever controls the silicon photonics engines, optical glass substrates, external laser thermal management, and precision fiber-coupling automation will capture the infrastructure surplus previously held by standard server motherboard makers.

### 5. What Should Be Monitored Next?
1. **Commercial Uptake of CPO Fabrics:** Monitor whether hyperscalers (Meta, Microsoft, AWS) deploy Broadcom Tomahawk 6 Davisson / Celestica DS6005 switches at volume or continue relying on pluggable optics (USFP standard) through late 2026.
2. **Open-Weight Release of MiniMax M3.1 Flash (October 23, 2026):** Track whether the developer community successfully quantizes the 428B/23B MoE model to run on dual Mac Studios or consumer GPU rigs.
3. **Adobe's Defensive Roadmap:** Watch Adobe MAX and upcoming creative suite updates for potential price cuts or deep native integrations of low-latency generative DiTs to stave off market erosion from Krea, FLUX, and Kling.
