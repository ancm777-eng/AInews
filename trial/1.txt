### 4. Samsung and ASML Pioneer 12-Inch Photomask Platform for High NA EUV and Announce Industry-First DRAM Adoption by 2028 (Category 6)

**Source:** ASML Holding N.V. / Samsung Electronics Co., Ltd. / Business Wire / Taipei Times / Silicon Saxony  
**Event Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Source Date:** 2026-09-08, 08:30 CET / 15:30 KST  
**Status:** Strategic Industrial Consortium Announcement & Manufacturing Roadmap  
**Primary URL:** https://www.asml.com/en/news/press-releases/2026/samsung-electronics-and-asml-expand-strategic-collaboration

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? In leading-edge semiconductor manufacturing, optical lithography resolution is governed by the numerical aperture (NA) of the lens system. The transition from 0.33 NA EUV to 0.55 High NA EUV enables sub-2nm logic and sub-10nm DRAM scaling. However, High NA EUV optics introduce a fundamental physical hurdle: anamorphic magnification. Light is demagnified by 4x in the horizontal (X) axis but 8x in the vertical (Y) axis. Because the industry standard photomask format has been locked at 6 inches by 6 inches (152mm x 152mm) for over four decades, this 8x anamorphic reduction halves the maximum reticle exposure field on the wafer from 26mm x 33mm down to 26mm x 16.5mm (half-field). For massive AI accelerators and heterogeneous compute dies—which routinely push past 800 square millimeters—a single die cannot fit within a single High NA exposure field, forcing foundries to employ "stitching." Stitching requires two separate mask exposures per layer, which doubles defect exposure, introduces critical overlay alignment errors across the stitch seam, and cuts scanner wafer throughput in half.

On September 8, 2026, Samsung Electronics and ASML formally announced an expansion of their strategic partnership to pioneer next-generation semiconductor manufacturing for the AI era. Samsung officially joined the ASML-led industry consortium to develop and deploy a **12-inch photomask platform for High NA EUV**. By transitioning the physical reticle format from 6-inch substrates to 12-inch quartz plates, the usable mask area expands by 4x, fully restoring the reticle exposure field size to full-field dimensions (26mm x 33mm or larger) under 8x anamorphic magnification. This breakthrough eliminates reticle stitching boundaries, reduces wafer-level patterning defects, and restores scanner productivity. Concurrently, Samsung made an unprecedented technological commitment: it will introduce ASML’s High NA EUV tools into **high-volume manufacturing (HVM) for advanced DRAM by 2028**. This marks the first confirmed adoption of High NA EUV in dynamic RAM production globally (surpassing TSMC's 2030 logic target), paving the way for sub-10nm DRAM bitcells and 16-Hi/20-Hi High Bandwidth Memory (HBM4/HBM5) architectures required for next-generation AI accelerators.

System-level implications across lithography, mask blank materials, and memory-compute co-design:
1. **Anamorphic Optics & Stitching Elimination:** In conventional 6-inch High NA EUV reticles, exposing an 850mm² AI accelerator requires splitting the circuit layout across two distinct reticles. The resulting boundary stitch introduces overlay edge placement errors (EPE) of 1.5 to 2.5 nanometers, degrading yield on high-speed SerDes and power bus routing. The 12-inch mask platform restores single-exposure patterning across the entire die area, eliminating stitching-induced yield drops and simplifying physical RTL floorplanning.
2. **Mask Blank Mechanics, Pellicle & Thermal Distortion:** A 12-inch photomask plate (measuring ~300mm x 300mm with ~12mm to 15mm thickness) experiences substantial gravitational sag and thermal expansion when subjected to extreme EUV source power (exceeding 500W to 1,000W). Developing stable, ultra-low expansion (ULE) glass blanks, high-transmission carbon nanotube (CNT) pellicles, and specialized vacuum electrostatic chucks represents the primary mechanical engineering hurdle that Samsung and ASML will co-develop.
3. **DRAM Single-Exposure Scaling & HBM Packaging Integration:** Conventional 0.33 NA EUV requires quadruple patterning (SAQP) for sub-10nm DRAM capacitor contacts, inflating mask counts and process variance. Adopting 0.55 High NA EUV by 2028 allows Samsung to replace multi-patterning loops with a single exposure, cutting DRAM wafer cycle time by up to 25%. This lithographic precision enables ultra-dense Through-Silicon Via (TSV) pitches and planar micro-bump arrays, directly supporting Samsung's CUBE and zHBM packaging roadmaps where logic base dies and 20-high DRAM stacks are integrated vertically.

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem:** Overhauls the global photomask ecosystem—requiring new blank suppliers (Hoya, AGC), mask writing electron-beam tools, and metrology systems—to establish 12-inch reticles as the long-term High NA baseline.
- **Business & Market:** Positions Samsung ahead of SK hynix and Micron by locking in early High NA DRAM volume production for 2028, reinforcing its capability to deliver next-generation HBM stacks for hyperscaler AI clusters.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware:** Enables fabless accelerator developers (Nvidia, Broadcom) to design single-die logic modules exceeding 850mm² without the yield penalties of reticle stitching, expanding monolithic compute density per package.
- **Investment Implication:** Overweight semiconductor capital equipment leaders dominating High NA EUV (ASML), photomask blank leaders (Hoya), and specialized EUV inspection toolmakers (KLA, Lasertec).
