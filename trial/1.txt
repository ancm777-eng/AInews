### 3. Intel EMIB-T Packaging Captures Hyperscaler ASIC Momentum as MediaTek Adopts Dual-Track Strategy Alongside TSMC CoWoS

**Source:** Chosun Biz / Commercial Times / TrendForce / Taipei Times / BigGo Finance  
**Event Date:** 2026-09-02  
**Source Date:** 2026-09-03, 00:34 KST / 10:42 KST Updated  
**Status:** Commercial Packaging Supply Chain Disclosures & Executive Guidance  
**Primary URL:** https://english.chosun.com/site/data/html_dir/2026/09/03/2026090300034.html  

#### [Overview]
Why this semiconductor packaging shift matters: High-performance AI accelerators require advanced 2.5D packaging to interconnect compute logic dies with High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) stacks over dense, short-reach interfaces. Historically, TSMC’s Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate (CoWoS) has held a near-monopoly on advanced AI packaging. However, TSMC's CoWoS packaging lines are heavily booked through 2026 and 2027—with NVIDIA alone securing over 60% of total CoWoS-L capacity—leaving cloud hyperscalers and custom ASIC designers facing severe capacity allocation caps.

On September 2, 2026, disclosures from Taiwan's fabless semiconductor leader **MediaTek** and Korean industry reports confirmed that MediaTek is adopting a parallel dual-track packaging strategy, utilizing both **TSMC CoWoS and Intel EMIB-T (Embedded Multi-die Interconnect Bridge with Through-Silicon Vias)** for its next-generation hyperscale AI ASICs. The announcement follows MediaTek’s board approval of a $5 billion supply-chain capacity budget and marks the first time a Tier-1 Asian fabless giant has officially contracted Intel Foundry’s advanced packaging for commercial AI chips. Unlike TSMC's CoWoS, which places compute and memory dies across a large, expensive silicon interposer, Intel's EMIB architecture embeds miniature silicon bridges directly into the organic package substrate, significantly reducing interposer silicon costs and allowing package sizes to scale past nine reticles (up to 120 x 120 mm). The newly upgraded **EMIB-T variant incorporates Through-Silicon Vias (TSVs)** to enable vertical power delivery directly through the bridge, slashing transient voltage droop for high-current AI accelerators. MediaTek’s validation is accelerating broader industry interest: **Broadcom, Meta, and Google** are actively evaluating EMIB-T for upcoming TPU and ASIC generations to bypass TSMC capacity bottlenecks, while **SK hynix** confirmed at Hot Chips that it is evaluating mounting 2,048-bit HBM4 stacks directly on Intel EMIB substrates.

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem:** Validates embedded-bridge substrate packaging (EMIB-T) as a credible alternative to silicon-interposer packaging (CoWoS-S/L), accelerating the heterogeneous chiplet design ecosystem.
- **Business & Market:** Delivers a major commercial victory for Intel Foundry’s advanced packaging business, creating a viable second source for hyperscalers seeking to de-risk single-vendor packaging dependencies on TSMC.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware:** Relieves advanced packaging lead-time constraints across the AI server supply chain, allowing hyperscalers to expand custom ASIC deployments (TPUs, MTIA, custom XPUs) more rapidly.
- **Investment Implication:** Overweight semiconductor substrate fabricators equipped for embedded-bridge cavity milling; monitor Intel Foundry packaging revenue ramp beginning in H2 2027.

#### [Technical Deep Dive]

- **Core Technical Specifications:**
  - Packaging Platform: Intel EMIB-T (Embedded Multi-die Interconnect Bridge with Through-Silicon Vias)
  - Interconnect Architecture: Silicon bridge dies embedded within organic package substrate cavities; micro-bumps interconnecting logic and HBM dies across sub-micron routing traces
  - Reticle & Area Scaling: Supports packages exceeding 9x reticle size (>7,700 mm²) with substrate dimensions reaching up to 120 x 120 mm
  - Vertical Power Integration (TSVs): EMIB-T incorporates vertical TSVs directly through the bridge silicon, enabling vertical power routing to core logic and reducing power delivery network (PDN) loop inductance
  - Yield & Production Metrics: Technology verification yield reported above 90%; target mass-production yield benchmark of 98% required for volume hyperscaler ramp
  - Adopting & Evaluating Customers: MediaTek (Dual-track CoWoS + EMIB-T for AI ASICs), Google (Next-gen TPU evaluation), Meta (AI ASIC evaluation), Broadcom

### 4. SEMICON Taiwan 2026: TSMC Unveils Silicon-Direct Microchannel Cooling to Dissipate 4,100W in Next-Gen 14x Reticle CoWoS Packages (Category 6)

**Source:** TSMC Advanced Packaging R&D / SEMICON Taiwan Heterogeneous Integration Summit / TrendForce / Economic Daily News  
**Event Date:** 2026-09-01 (Symposium Keynote)  
**Source Date:** 2026-09-02 (Detailed Proceedings Finalized)  
**Status:** Conference Technical Presentation & Thermal Semiconductor Packaging Roadmap  
**Primary URL:** https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/09/02/news-tsmc-eyes-chip-level-microchannel-cooling-as-ai-system-power-could-rise-6x-in-five-years/  

#### [Overview]
Why this thermal packaging development matters: In advanced AI compute hardware, accelerator power dissipation is reaching physical thermodynamic boundaries. As multi-die packages integrate larger compute dies alongside multiple High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) stacks, package power consumption is scaling from 600W toward 4,000W+. In conventional server architectures, heat generated by silicon logic must conduct through a primary Thermal Interface Material (TIM-1: the thermal grease or solder layer between die and lid), an integrated copper heat spreader lid, a secondary TIM (TIM-2: the thermal pad between lid and heatsink), and finally into an external skived-fin liquid cold plate. Each interface layer introduces conductive thermal resistance that impedes heat flux. When package power exceeds 1,000W to 2,000W, the thermal gradient across these interface layers causes junction temperatures to exceed safe silicon operating limits (typically 85°C to 105°C), inducing thermal throttling and memory cell leakage regardless of how fast coolant flows through external server manifolds.

At the SEMICON Taiwan 2026 Heterogeneous Integration Summit in Taipei, TSMC Advanced Packaging R&D Director James Chen (Chen Yen-Ming) disclosed TSMC’s comprehensive physical roadmap for **silicon-direct microchannel cooling**, addressing the extreme thermodynamic requirements of next-generation CoWoS platforms. Chen revealed that between 2024 and 2029, TSMC projects that CoWoS package dimensions will expand from **3.3 times the reticle size to more than 14 times the reticle size**, packing roughly **10 large compute dies and 20 HBM stacks onto a single package** by 2028. This heterogeneous scaling will drive single-package transistor counts up by **48 times**, aggregate HBM bandwidth up by **34 times**, and total single-package power dissipation from **roughly 600W to an extreme 4,100W**. To prevent thermal failure and suppress power delivery network (PDN) resistive losses—which increase more than fivefold at 4,100W—TSMC has formally integrated **on-package microchannel cooling** into its advanced packaging research roadmap.

System-level architectural and thermal-electrical implications across the hardware stack:
1. **Direct-to-Silicon Microchannel Integration:** TSMC is eliminating external cold plates and copper lids by etching microscopic fluid microchannels directly into the silicon or integrating them into the package lid itself. This design cuts thermal interface layers from two down to one (or zero in direct silicon-contact designs), dramatically shortening the thermal conduction path and reducing junction-to-fluid thermal resistance.
2. **5kW Laboratory Heat Dissipation Demonstration:** TSMC announced that laboratory test vehicles utilizing CoWoS-R advanced structures have already successfully demonstrated heat dissipation exceeding **3.4 kW in 2025 and over 5,000 watts (5 kW) in 2026**, confirming that silicon-direct microchannels can thermally stabilize 4,100W-class packages. Research teams are actively evaluating **jet impingement** (direct fluid jetting onto logic hot spots) and **two-phase boiling heat transfer** within microchannels to handle localized heat fluxes exceeding 100 W/cm².
3. **Co-Design of Thermal, Mechanical Warpage, and PDN:** Dissipating 4,100W across a 14x-reticle package requires co-designing thermal expansion coefficients (CTE) between the silicon dies, the organic carrier substrate, and the microchannel manifold. Fluid pressure within internal channels must be managed to prevent structural warpage or micro-bump shearing, while vertical power delivery paths must be routed around fluid channels without compromising high-current voltage stability.

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem:** Shifts the boundary of datacenter liquid cooling directly into the semiconductor packaging foundry, establishing microchannel fluidics as a standard component of heterogeneous 3D IC design.
- **Business & Market:** Solidifies TSMC’s advanced packaging moat by offering integrated thermal solutions that hyperscaler merchant server ODMs cannot replicate with standard bolt-on cold plates.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware:** Enables datacenter server racks to support 4,100W multi-die compute packages, paving the way for megawatt-density AI server cabinets operating at supply water temperatures above 35°C (enabling efficient facility-level economizer cooling).
- **Investment Implication:** Overweight semiconductor etching equipment providers capable of deep silicon microchannel machining, precision fluid connector manufacturers, and two-phase dielectric fluid suppliers.

#### [Technical Deep Dive]

- **Core Technical Specifications:**
  - Organization: TSMC Advanced Packaging R&D (SEMICON Taiwan 2026 Keynote)
  - Packaging Vehicle: TSMC CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate) Heterogeneous Architecture
  - Package Area Expansion: Scaling from 3.3x reticle equivalent (current production) to >14x reticle equivalent by 2028–2029
  - Heterogeneous Silicon Density: Capable of integrating ~10 large compute logic dies and 20 HBM memory stacks on a single module
  - Silicon Scaling Projections (2024 to 2029):
    - Transistor Count per Package: 48x Increase
    - Aggregate HBM Bandwidth per Package: >34x Increase
    - Package Thermal Power Envelope: Scaling from ~600W to 4,100W per package
    - Power Delivery Network (PDN) Loss: Projected to increase >5x without architectural re-design
  - Thermal Engineering Mechanism: Silicon-direct microchannels etched into lid/silicon; elimination of TIM-2; jet impingement and 2-phase boiling heat transfer
  - Demonstrated Lab Heat Dissipation: 3.4 kW (2025 benchmark) and >5.0 kW (2026 CoWoS-R test vehicle)
  - Target Junction Operating Limits: Sub-85°C to 105°C under continuous 4,100W load.
  - Primary Source: TSMC Technical Presentation / TrendForce / Economic Daily News (2026-09-02)

#### [Critical View]
While TSMC has demonstrated 5 kW heat dissipation in laboratory environments, transitioning microchannel-integrated lids into commercial mass production involves extreme mechanical risks. Circulating high-pressure coolant directly within or atop a 14x-reticle silicon package creates potential points of fluid leakage that could destroy a $100,000+ accelerator module. Furthermore, non-uniform thermal contraction between fluid microchannels and silicon dies risks inducing severe package warpage and micro-bump solder fatigue during power cycling.

2. **Structural trend strengthening:** Foundation model developers are shifting competition from raw parameter counts to agentic execution efficiency and post-training alignment for computer use. Concurrently, hardware architects are disaggregating monolithic accelerators into massive 14x-reticle multi-die packages, forcing packaging and cooling into an integrated co-design discipline.
3. **Bottleneck emerging:** Package-level thermal density and interface thermal resistance. When single multi-die packages dissipate 4,100W, traditional external cold plates with dual TIM layers reach physical heat transfer limits, mandating on-package microchannel liquid fluidics.
4. **Layer becoming strategically more important:** Advanced heterogeneous packaging and embedded silicon bridge technologies (EMIB-T and CoWoS), which govern whether semiconductor designers can physically assemble next-generation accelerators as TSMC packaging capacity remains heavily allocated to merchant GPU providers.
5. **What should be monitored next:** Initial enterprise adoption metrics for GPT-6 Astra under OpenAI's Daybreak gating program, the progress of MediaTek and Google's tape-outs on Intel EMIB-T, and TSMC's manufacturing reliability data for microchannel-integrated package lids.
