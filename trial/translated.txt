### 4. TRUMPF Unveils Industrial HiPIMS Plasma Sputtering at SEMICON Taiwan to Overcome Through-Glass Via Bottlenecks in AI Substrates (Category 6)

Source: TRUMPF Official Announcement / SEMICON Taiwan / TechHQ / TechSoda  
Event Date: 2026-09-01 (Press Conference) / 2026-09-03 (SEMICON Taiwan Showcase)  
Source Date: 2026-09-03  
Status: Official Commercial Equipment Release & Packaging Manufacturing Unveiling  
Primary URL: https://www.trumpf.com/en_INT/newsroom/press-releases/press-release-detail-page/trumpf-makes-glass-substrates-ready-for-the-next-generation-of-ai-chips/  

#### [Overview]
Why this substrate manufacturing breakthrough matters: In high-performance AI accelerator design, package scaling is reaching physical limits with conventional organic materials. Modern AI accelerator modules integrate multiple compute chiplets alongside 8 to 16 High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) stacks, expanding substrate package areas beyond 100mm by 100mm. Conventional organic core packaging substrates utilizing Ajinomoto Build-up Film (ABF) dielectric layers suffer from thermomechanical warpage under high reflow temperatures, lack the mechanical rigidity to support ultra-dense micro-bump arrays, and exhibit dielectric signal dissipation (Df) that degrades 112G and 224G/448G PAM4 SerDes channels. While transitioning to glass core substrates offers ideal mechanical flatness, a tunable coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE) matching silicon, and ultra-low dielectric loss, manufacturing has encountered a fatal yield bottleneck: metallizing millions of high-aspect-ratio Through-Glass Vias (TGVs) without voids.

At SEMICON Taiwan 2026 in Taipei, industrial laser and plasma systems manufacturer TRUMPF announced the commercial availability of its specialized High-Power Impulse Magnetron Sputtering (HiPIMS) product line for semiconductor packaging. Piotr Lach, Head of Next Technology Demands at TRUMPF Elektronik, and CTO Dr. Berthold Schmidt presented technical data demonstrating that HiPIMS solves the fundamental failure mechanism of glass substrate manufacturing: incomplete via metallization. While ultrafast lasers can drill millions of microscopic through-holes in glass panels, conventional physical vapor deposition (PVD) struggles to deposit uniform conductive seed layers inside deep, narrow via holes with aspect ratios exceeding 10:1 to 15:1. Metal that thins out along the sidewalls produces latent open circuits that pass optical inspection but fail electrically under thermal stress, rendering entire glass panels scrap. TRUMPF's HiPIMS technology overcomes this by generating an ultra-dense, highly ionized metal plasma. When paired with directional radio-frequency (RF) substrate biasing, the charged metal ions are actively accelerated deep into narrow via cavities, creating dense, uniform, and void-free copper seed layers along vertical sidewalls. This process provides the reproducible metallization required to bring glass-core System-in-Package substrates into high-volume pilot production for 1,000W+ next-generation AI accelerators.

System-level electrical, thermal, and manufacturing co-design implications:
1. Power Distribution Network (PDN) & Transient Voltage Stability: High-aspect-ratio TGVs with uniform, void-free copper metallization reduce vertical via resistance and loop inductance. For 1,000W+ AI accelerators drawing transient current steps exceeding 1,500 amperes, low-inductance vertical TGVs suppress parasitic voltage droop between motherboard voltage regulators and core logic dies.
2. High-Speed SerDes & Signal Integrity (SI): The combination of smooth glass core surfaces (surface roughness Ra below 20 nanometers) and uniform TGV transitions minimizes impedance discontinuities for 224G and 448G PAM4 SerDes channels, significantly reducing channel insertion loss and mitigating reliance on costly retimer chips across accelerator boards.
3. Warpage Suppression vs. Glass Brittleness: Glass cores feature a tunable CTE (3.0 to 3.5 ppm/K) closely matched to silicon (2.6 ppm/K), suppressing package warpage across extreme thermal cycles. HiPIMS achieves uniform sidewall deposition without thermal micro-cracking, preserving the structural integrity of thin glass panels (100 to 500 micrometers) during subsequent chemical plating cycles.

#### [Strategic Impact]

- Technology & Ecosystem: Solves the primary manufacturing bottleneck holding back glass substrate commercialization, enabling OSATs and substrate manufacturers (including SEMCO, Absolics, and Unimicron) to transition glass packaging from experimental R&D into qualified pilot lines.
- Business & Market: Positions TRUMPF as an essential Tier-2 equipment supplier to leading packaging foundries, accelerating the timeline for fabless AI chip designers (NVIDIA, AMD, Broadcom) to adopt glass substrates by 2027–2028.
- Infrastructure & Hardware: Enables the physical scaling of AI accelerators beyond reticle limits, supporting modular compute-memory assemblies exceeding 100mm x 100mm with 16-Hi HBM4 stacks without substrate warping.
- Investment Implication: Overweight specialized semiconductor deposition and laser toolmakers (TRUMPF, LPKF); monitor pilot qualification yield rates across South Korean and Taiwanese substrate makers.

#### [Critical View]
While HiPIMS resolves the seed-layer deposition bottleneck within TGVs, glass substrates remain subject to acute handling and mechanical risks. Glass is inherently brittle: unlike compliant organic cores, any micro-crack induced during laser ablation or edge-handling can propagate catastrophically during high-temperature reflow, causing panel shattering. Furthermore, wet chemical electroplating chemistry must be precisely controlled to avoid generating internal tensile stress that could crack the glass matrix around densely pitched vias. Commercial volume adoption is not anticipated before the second half of 2027, meaning near-term AI hardware deployments will remain reliant on organic ABF packaging.
