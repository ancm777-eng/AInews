The next adoption signal is whether agent-runtime platforms convert the pricing change, effort controls, and EFS governance model into measurable production reliability and lower total workflow cost.

    Arm AGI Server CPU: Dual N3P SoCs, 136 Active Cores, and a 2.0 TB/s UCIe Fabric

    1. Abstract

    At Hot Chips 2026, Arm detailed an AGI data-center CPU for hyperscale AI orchestration that pairs two monolithic TSMC N3P SoC chiplets across a 16×16 UCIe macro array. The design delivers up to 136 active physical cores, 2.0 TB/s aggregate bidirectional die-to-die bandwidth, 12 channels of DDR5-8800 with 844.8 GB/s peak theoretical memory bandwidth, sub-100ns direct DRAM latency, and a 300W base TDP; silicon is validated and commercial servers are scheduled for late 2026.

    UCIe — An open die-to-die interconnect standard for chiplet-scale bandwidth and latency.

    OoO window — The pool of instructions a core can schedule out of order.

    Signal integrity — Preservation of valid high-speed electrical signaling across package and board paths.

    Power integrity — Stable delivery voltage during rapid current-transient events.

    Compute and package topology

    Two monolithic TSMC N3P Compute Subsystem chiplets contain approximately 100 billion transistors in total, or 50 billion per die. Each die integrates 70 Neoverse V3 cores, a six-channel memory controller, and native PCIe/CXL physical layers. Four of 140 physical cores—two per chiplet—are reserved for yield recovery, enabling 64-, 128-, or 136-active-core SKUs. The dies connect through 256 physical differential channels in a 16×16 UCIe macro array at 32 GT/s, providing approximately 1.0 TB/s per direction.

    The Arm Neoverse CSS V3 microarchitecture operates at 2.80 GHz base and 3.70 GHz boost frequencies, with 10-wide decode, 10-wide dispatch, 8-wide retire, a 384+-entry OoO window, and dual 128-bit vector execution pipelines. Each active core has 2 MB of private L2 cache, producing 272 MB total L2 across 136 cores, complemented by a shared System-Level Cache. The processor supports 12 DDR5 channels—six per chiplet—at DDR5-8800, up to 6 TB per socket, 96 lanes of PCIe Gen 6.0 with CXL 3.0 support, and four lanes of PCIe Gen 4.0.

    Board, package, and power-delivery consequences

    Routing the 16×16 UCIe array at 32 GT/s requires sub-micron die-placement alignment accuracy, tens-of-microns micro-bump pitch control, managed trace impedance, and preserved signal-eye margins on advanced organic substrate packaging. Twelve DDR5-8800 channels require high-layer-count motherboards, ultra-low-loss dielectric laminates such as Megtron-8-class materials, and strict serpentine trace-length matching to control insertion loss and crosstalk. Synchronized bursts across 136 wide-decode cores require multiphase VRMs and ultra-low-ESR MLCCs directly beneath the BGA socket to suppress high di/dt voltage droop.

    Architectural distinction. Unlike AMD EPYC Turin’s Core Complex Dies routed through a centralized I/O die, Arm places memory controllers on compute silicon. Against Intel Xeon 7 Diamond Rapids’ 3D Foveros Direct architecture, the Arm design uses direct multi-chiplet UCIe connectivity on an advanced substrate. The result is a direct memory-controller path targeting sub-100ns DRAM latency for memory-bound orchestration loops.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

    Arm’s design provides a production reference for high-bandwidth UCIe-based server silicon and targets host-CPU sockets where AI agents compile code, schedule tools, maintain large conversational state, and move data between memory and accelerators. Yet the latency benefit comes with a cost trade-off: integrating memory controllers and high-speed serializer/deserializer I/O on expensive N3P compute silicon consumes leading-edge wafer area for analog and I/O circuits that do not scale as efficiently as digital logic. Commercial success will depend on whether lower memory latency and 300W thermal density justify that silicon-cost premium relative to disaggregated I/O die designs.
