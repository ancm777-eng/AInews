    Applied Materials and Besi Expand Strategic Packaging Collaboration to EPIC Center for Sub-Micron Hybrid Bonding and Panel Architectures

    1. Executive Summary

    Conventional multi-die accelerators are constrained by optical lithography reticle boundaries (~858 mm²) and micro-bump thermal-compression bonding, which introduces severe parasitic capacitance, thermal resistance, and pitch limits. To overcome this interconnect bottleneck, Applied Materials and BE Semiconductor Industries (Besi) expanded their joint initiative into the $5 billion EPIC Center. The program scales direct copper-to-copper (Cu-Cu) dielectric hybrid bonding to sub-micron pad pitches (<1.0 µm) and pioneers rectangular 510mm × 515mm panel-level packaging. This transition drives a 10x increase in interconnect density while achieving an estimated >80% reduction in thermal boundary resistance compared to solder-based bonding.

    Cu-Cu Hybrid Bonding: Direct molecular bonding of dielectric materials combined with embedded copper pads without intervening solder balls, enabling sub-micron pitch density.

    Panel-Level Packaging (PLP): Advanced multi-die packaging executed on large rectangular substrates (e.g., 510mm × 515mm) rather than 300mm round wafers, maximizing usable surface area.

    Pad Pitch: Center-to-center physical distance between adjacent interconnect pads, directly dictating I/O contact density.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

    Transitioning sub-micron hybrid bonding from circular 300mm silicon wafers to 510mm × 515mm rectangular panels introduces severe mechanical warpage caused by mismatched coefficients of thermal expansion (CTE) across composite substrates, copper layers, and silicon. Furthermore, sub-micron bonding requires chemical-mechanical planarization (CMP) topography control with surface roughness held strictly below 0.5nm; nanoscale surface contaminants can cause catastrophic non-contact voids across thousands of connections. Until high-throughput panel inspection and defect repair systems mature, commercial panel-level hybrid bonding will remain restricted to specialized test runs rather than high-yield commercial foundry output.
