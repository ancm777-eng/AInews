    Tower Semiconductor and NewPhotonics Commercialize Laser-Integrated InP-on-Si PICs

    1. Abstract

    Tower Semiconductor and fabless innovator NewPhotonics have initiated high-volume commercial shipments of laser-integrated, serviceable optical engine Photonic Integrated Circuits (PICs) targeting 800G to 1.6T AI datacenter interconnects. Fabricated on Tower’s PH18DA silicon photonics foundry platform, the architecture achieves wafer-level heterogeneous integration of Indium Phosphide (InP) continuous-wave lasers, semiconductor optical amplifiers, and heaterless micro-ring modulators directly onto silicon waveguides. Supporting CPX-MSA socket-pluggable Near-Packaged Optics (NPO), the platform resolves the critical field-serviceability barrier of high-density optical fabrics while operating at 200 Gbps per lane optical signaling (PAM4) to match cutting-edge host SerDes architectures.

    Wafer-Level Heterogeneous Integration: Bonding native Indium Phosphide (InP) gain materials directly onto silicon photonics wafers prior to lithography, creating on-chip continuous-wave lasers without manual micro-optic alignment.
    Near-Packaged Optics (NPO): Locating optical engines on the host board immediately adjacent to the compute or switch ASIC to reduce high-frequency electrical trace lengths while preserving detachable socket serviceability.
    Heaterless Micro-Ring Modulator (MRM): Resonant electro-optic modulators that utilize electronic tuning rather than power-intensive thermal heaters to stabilize resonant optical wavelengths across fluctuating temperatures.
    CPX-MSA Standard: An industry multi-source agreement defining mechanical and electrical socket interfaces for pluggable optical engines, preventing full board discards during component failures.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    As per-lane signaling reaches 200 Gbps PAM4, channel attenuation in passive copper Direct Attach Copper (DAC) cables restricts copper reaches to under one meter, forcing scale-up clusters to adopt optical interconnects. Traditional silicon photonics solutions suffer from either high-loss external fiber harnesses or unserviceable co-packaged designs. The Tower-NewPhotonics PH18DA architecture resolves this dilemma by integrating the optical gain medium directly into the silicon substrate while adopting a socketable form factor.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

    Wafer-level heterogeneous bonding of InP onto silicon introduces complex material and operational challenges. Mismatched thermal expansion coefficients between InP and silicon substrates create lattice stress, making fabrication yields highly vulnerable to micro-defects that cannot be individually reworked across large wafer diameters. Furthermore, ensuring that detachable sub-micron optical connectors maintain alignment tolerances under severe datacenter vibration and recurring thermal cycling across years of deployment remains an unproven operational benchmark.
