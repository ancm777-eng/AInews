    d-Matrix Integrates Raptor Inference XPUs into Modular NVIDIA MGX Architecture via NVLink Fusion

    1. Abstract

    On September 10, 2026, inference semiconductor architect d-Matrix announced a strategic integration adopting NVIDIA NVLink Fusion to incorporate its Digital In-Memory Computing (DIMC) Raptor XPUs into modular NVIDIA MGX rack-scale server architectures. By utilizing high-speed SerDes physical layers delivering up to 3.6 Terabytes per second (TB/s) of bidirectional interconnect bandwidth per socket, d-Matrix circumvents the deployment barriers of bespoke server chassis while directly addressing external memory bandwidth bottlenecks. This architectural alignment couples specialized low-latency SRAM matrix math engines with standardized liquid-cooled datacenter trays, NVIDIA Vera host CPUs, and BlueField DPUs.

    Key Architectural Concepts
    Digital In-Memory Computing (DIMC): A circuit architecture executing matrix-vector arithmetic directly within or adjacent to internal SRAM arrays, bypassing the memory bus latency and thermal dissipation of external HBM.
    NVLink Fusion: A semi-custom point-to-point interconnect standard granting specialized third-party accelerator ASICs high-speed, coherent scale-up bandwidth into host CPU and DPU clusters.
    Modular MGX Reference Chassis: A standardized datacenter physical specification enabling interchangeable accelerator trays with unified 54V power distribution busbars and direct-to-chip liquid cooling manifolds.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    Inference serving for reasoning-dense large language models is predominantly memory-bandwidth bound during token autoregression. Heterogeneous accelerator implementations typically bottleneck at the host interface: standard PCIe Gen6 connections cap theoretical bidirectional bandwidth at 256 GB/s, creating severe serialization delays during inter-socket tensor exchange. Integrating NVLink Fusion elevates point-to-point throughput to 3.6 TB/s per socket—a 14-fold improvement over PCIe Gen6—facilitating line-rate tensor parallelism directly into the modular MGX compute plane.

This tight coupling may complicate long-term cross-compatibility with open interconnect standards such as Ultra Accelerator Link (UALink) and Compute Express Link (CXL), while leaving cache coherency and cross-accelerator memory virtualization heavily reliant on NVIDIA's proprietary host runtime stack.

    Amkor Scales Peoria Advanced Packaging Campus Investment to $12 Billion Anchored by NVIDIA

    1. Abstract

    On September 10, 2026, leading outsourced semiconductor assembly and test provider Amkor Technology announced a major Phase 2 expansion of its advanced packaging campus in Peoria, Arizona, scaling total capital expenditure from $2 billion to $12 billion. Underwritten by multi-year commercial capacity reservations and capital prepayments from NVIDIA Corporation, the expansion adds 60,000 square meters of cleanroom space to achieve an aggregate campus footprint of approximately 93,000 square meters (1 million square feet). Co-located to process wafer output from TSMC's Phoenix fabs, the facility establishes domestic 2.5D multi-die heterogeneous integration, micro-bump bonding, and high-power automated system-level testing within the United States.

    Key Packaging & Substrate Concepts
    2.5D Heterogeneous Packaging: The planar assembly of compute dies and HBM stacks side-by-side on an intermediate silicon or high-density redistribution layer (RDL) interposer.
    Thermocompression Bonding (TCB): A high-precision attachment process applying simultaneous heat and force to bond fine-pitch micro-bumps while mitigating substrate warpage on packages exceeding 100mm × 100mm.
    System-Level ATE (Automated Test Equipment): Specialized high-power test fixtures providing active direct-die thermal management during high-voltage structural vector sweeps on processors exceeding 1,000W.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    Modern artificial intelligence accelerators have surpassed the physical reticle limits of single monolithic silicon dies, necessitating heterogeneous multi-chiplet topologies interconnected with multiple High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) modules. Fabricating advanced front-end wafers domestically in Arizona without localized packaging has historically mandated trans-oceanic transit to East Asian OSAT lines, introducing physical mechanical stress, yield degradation, and cycle-time exposure. Expanding the Peoria site establishes an end-to-end regional manufacturing corridor supporting wafer bumping, precision die sort, 2.5D interposer integration, and full-thermal final burn-in.
