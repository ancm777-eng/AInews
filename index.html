    OCP Aligns NVIDIA, Microsoft, Google, and 80+ Vendors on 800V DC AI Factory Power

    1. Abstract

    NVIDIA, Microsoft, Google, and more than 80 hardware and infrastructure providers standardized an 800V Direct Current architecture through the Open Compute Project on August 13, 2026. The framework moves AI facilities beyond the conductor and conversion constraints of 480V AC distribution, targeting up to 2MW per rack row with 50%–80% lower copper mass, 8%–12% lower annual energy-related operating expense, and estimated $4M–$8M savings per 10MW deployment.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    The 800V DC / LVDC architecture consolidates facility conversion upstream: Solid-State Transformers or centralized rectifier skids convert medium-voltage AC directly to an 800V DC busway. Higher voltage lowers current, shrinking conductor cross-sections and reducing heat and resistance losses as clusters move from 100kW-per-rack designs toward multi-megawatt GPU rack rows for NVIDIA Blackwell and Vera Rubin systems.

    Five-layer 800V DC delivery path

    Layer 1 — Grid Interconnect: 10kV–34.5kV AC utility power enters through the medium-voltage grid substation. Layer 2 — Facility Rectification: a centralized DC Power Block or SST converts medium-voltage AC directly to 800V DC. Layer 3 — Row Distribution: overhead 800V DC busway and Row Power Center deliver up to 2MW per rack row. Layer 4 — Rack Step-Down: in-rack 800V-to-48V intermediate DC/DC converters feed accelerator trays. Layer 5 — Core Accelerator PDN: point-of-load multi-phase VRMs provide sub-1V power directly to GPU and ASIC silicon cores.

    Conversion topology shift. Legacy 480V AC systems typically pass through four to five stages—grid MV AC to 480V AC, 400V DC UPS, 48V DC, and sub-1V core logic—creating compounding thermal and resistance losses. Direct 800V DC consolidates upstream conversion into a single medium-voltage stage, enabling smaller conductors and lower distribution loss. Annual energy-related OpEx is projected to decline by 8%–12%.

    OCP technical alignment is now published. NVIDIA’s 800V DC-compatible MGX power racks are scheduled to ship in H2 2026, followed by 2MW row-level power centers and busbars in 2027; facility DC Power Blocks remain a longer-term deployment stage. The common interface, topology, and safety framework establishes an open hyperscale electrical standard across cloud operators and silicon suppliers, while creating demand for SiC/GaN power devices, high-voltage busways, switchgear, and solid-state transformers.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

    800V DC directly addresses the physical bottleneck in dense AI infrastructure, but it changes rather than eliminates operational risk. Unlike AC, 800V DC does not naturally cross zero voltage, requiring specialized solid-state circuit breakers, arc-flash mitigation, and retrained personnel. Retrofitting installed 480V AC campuses requires substantial capital expenditure, likely creating a dual-tier datacenter estate in which new AI factories adopt high-voltage DC while legacy facilities remain constrained by existing distribution systems.
