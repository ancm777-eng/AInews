    HBM4E: H2 2029 Indiana mass production; Advanced MR-MUF & US Testbed; optimized 2,048-bit bus; 16-Hi ultra-thin die; high-performance exascale AI.

    Future 3D Memory: post-2030 research; Advanced Hybrid Bonding Testbed; >2,048-bit 3D stacking; direct logic-memory 3D; heterogeneous generative AI systems.

    System-design implication: The shift from 1,024-bit to 2,048-bit HBM buses doubles routing density across 2.5D silicon interposers and organic substrates. Purdue-based co-design can tighten electrical-thermal-mechanical simulation loops for die warpage, liquid-cooled server-tray dissipation, signal-integrity eye margins, and high-frequency PDN impedance.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

    Site preparation is underway, with cleanroom commissioning targeted for October 2028. The facility geographically diversifies final accelerator integration and reduces trans-Pacific lead time for US packaging, validation, server assembly, and collaborative customer engineering; however, it does not create a fully self-contained domestic silicon supply chain because front-end DRAM wafers still require cross-border transport from South Korea. Higher US labor and cleanroom construction costs, advanced-package yield challenges, and the complexity of 16-Hi stacking remain material constraints. The strategic upside rests on whether localized packaging can convert the Purdue testbed into faster HBM4/HBM4E design cycles and viable post-2030 hybrid-bonded 3D memory.
