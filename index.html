By natively integrating DOS into the AWS Neuron SDK, Amazon aims to eliminate manual kernel engineering across Trainium2 and Trainium3, delivering immediate execution throughput gains and drastically lowering migration costs for enterprise foundation models.

    Microsoft Unveils Azure Maia 300: 288GB HBM4 Subsystem and TSMC 2nm Disaggregated Chiplet Design

    1. Executive Summary

    Frontier reasoning models and multi-turn agentic workloads are heavily constrained by memory bandwidth and DRAM capacity during autoregressive token generation, while sole reliance on merchant silicon compresses cloud margins. To break this memory wall, Microsoft unveiled the Azure Maia 300 AI accelerator, engineered for deployment across multi-gigawatt Azure infrastructure beginning in 2027. Fabricated on TSMC's 2nm N2P logic node alongside TSMC 3nm I/O chiplets, Maia 300 pairs 13.8 PFLOPS of FP4 compute with an eight-stack HBM4 subsystem delivering 288 GB of capacity at 18.0 TB/s peak bandwidth. In high-density 96-socket direct-liquid-cooled racks, Microsoft projects the architecture will yield up to a 40% efficiency improvement in token throughput per megawatt over contemporary merchant GPU clusters.

    Key Architecture Concepts:

    • HBM4 Subsystem: High-bandwidth memory adopting a 2,048-bit interface per stack, doubling the bus width of HBM3E to achieve 18.0 TB/s aggregate package throughput.
    • TSMC N2P Node: Advanced 2nm-class logic process utilizing gate-all-around nanosheet transistors and backside power delivery networks for high power density.
    • Disaggregated Chiplet: Modular packaging strategy that separates compute tiles from I/O scale-up dies onto independent, yield-optimized process nodes.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    Maia 300 is tailored specifically for inference-phase KV-cache expansion and long-context reasoning loops. By decoupling the compute engine onto TSMC N2P and the scale-up I/O onto TSMC N3, Microsoft maximizes wafer yield across large silicon footprints.

    Navitas and NVIDIA Demonstrate 24kW Single-Stage 800V-to-12V GaN Power Architecture for MGX AI Trays

    1. Executive Summary

    In AI server racks exceeding 100 kW, traditional power architectures that step down high voltage to an intermediate 48V distribution bus suffer severe resistive copper losses and board-level thermal congestion. Addressing this electrical roadblock, Navitas Semiconductor demonstrated an operational 24 kW power delivery board developed in collaboration with NVIDIA for the modular MGX server platform. Built with 16 NV6034 650V GaNFast gallium nitride transistors, the board converts 800V DC rack power directly down to a regulated 12V DC output in a compact 400 mm × 60 mm profile. By eliminating intermediate 48V conversion bricks, the single-stage architecture hits a 97.0% peak efficiency and exceeds 2,000 W/in³ active power density directly adjacent to accelerator sockets.

    Key Architecture Concepts:

    • Single-Stage Direct Conversion: Power topology that converts primary 800V DC rack bus voltages directly to 12V core-adjacent feeds without intermediate 48V/54V bus converters.
    • GaNFast Power Transistor: Wide-bandgap semiconductor device capable of megahertz-range switching speeds, slashing the footprint of passive inductive components.
    • I²R Resistive Loss: Conductor power dissipation that scales with current squared, significantly mitigated by maintaining high voltage across chassis power distribution paths.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    By placing the conversion stage directly adjacent to accelerator modules on the MGX tray, high-current runs are kept to absolute minimum lengths. Dual-cooled 8 mm × 8 mm surface-mount packaging dissipates heat simultaneously into server liquid cold plates and internal copper ground planes.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

    While eliminating the 48V stage frees chassis space, delivering 24 kW at 12V requires managing up to 2,000 A of local current. Any distance between the converter output and point-of-load voltage regulator modules (VRMs) can lead to secondary I²R copper losses that erode the 97% conversion efficiency. Furthermore, switching 800V at megahertz frequencies creates severe electromagnetic interference (EMI) that demands precision multi-layer PCB shielding to prevent signal corruption on adjacent PCIe Gen6 and 224G SerDes traces.
