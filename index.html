    Vertical Power Delivery Scaling: Vicor ChiP Fab Expansion for Sub-0.8V Accelerator Architectures

    1. Abstract

    On September 11, 2026, Vicor Corporation announced the acquisition of a 334,000-square-foot facility in Merrimack, New Hampshire, along with a 54-acre industrial parcel in Hooksett, New Hampshire, to construct ChiP Fab-2 and Fab-3. The expansion establishes a domestic packaging and automated manufacturing footprint of nearly one million square feet dedicated to high-density Vertical Power Delivery (VPD) modules. Designed to power next-generation artificial intelligence accelerators exceeding 1,000W to 1,500W TDP at sub-0.8V core supplies, Vicor's architecture mounts current multipliers directly beneath the processor package. This direct vertical transit eliminates lateral motherboard routing, reducing Power Distribution Network (PDN) resistive losses by up to 85% while clearing top-side board real estate for 224 Gbps SerDes signal distribution.

    Key Architectural Concepts:
    • Vertical Power Delivery (VPD): A power packaging topology where voltage transformation modules reside directly on the reverse side of the PCB or interposer substrate, routing power vertically via short pins to the silicon die.
    • PDN IR Drop: Ohmic voltage degradation ($V = I \times R$) occurring along trace conductors, which becomes catastrophic when dynamic transients scale beyond 2,000 amperes at sub-0.8V levels.
    • Factorized Power Architecture (FPA): A modular distribution scheme separating voltage regulation from current transformation, stepping down high-voltage buses (48V/54V) directly to sub-1V core potentials at the point-of-load.
    • Converter housed in Package (ChiP): High-density modular power integration combining magnetic planar structures, power silicon, and control ASICs into unified surface-mount components.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    Scaling accelerator core power beyond 1,000 watts creates an intractable bottleneck for conventional lateral multi-phase voltage regulator modules. Lateral distribution requires high current to traverse inches of multi-layer motherboard copper, inducing massive thermal conduction waste ($I^2R$) and consuming lateral trace layers urgently required for PCIe Gen6, CXL, and 224G SerDes differential pairs.

Vicor's multi-fab buildout must scale yields across these tight thermo-mechanical packaging envelopes to meet volume hyperscaler delivery schedules by 2027.
