    OmniOn Power Unveils Osprey QODN 2.6kW 48V-to-12V Bus Converters

    1. Executive Summary

    Modern accelerator boards drawing over 1,000 watts per socket generate massive copper resistive losses ($I^2R$) when distributing high currents across conventional server baseboards. To tackle this power density barrier, OmniOn Power has released the second-generation Osprey QODN non-isolated intermediate bus converter, delivering up to 2,600W continuous output (217A at 12VDC) within a standard DOSA quarter-brick form factor. Reaching an exceptional 98.5% peak conversion efficiency, the module provides a 30% power density increase, saving over 1.2 kW of localized heat dissipation per 120 kW AI rack.

    Key Architectural Concepts:

    Intermediate Bus Converter (IBC): A board-level DC-DC stage that converts high distribution voltages (48V/54V) down to an intermediate 12V distribution plane near processor clusters.

    DOSA Quarter-Brick: Standardized power module mechanical envelope measuring 58.4 x 36.8 mm, defining drop-in pinouts and mounting specifications for multi-vendor server designs.

    Power Delivery Network (PDN): The complete electrical distribution pathway from rack-level busways down through motherboard copper traces, intermediate converters, and point-of-load regulators into the processor silicon.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    The Osprey QODN operates across a 40V–60V input envelope compatible with Open Compute Project (OCP) 48V/54V rack busways. By stepping down intermediate current close to multi-phase voltage regulator modules, it minimizes resistive losses across board traces and eases chassis-level liquid cooling constraints.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

    Concentrating 217A of continuous current within a compact 58.4 x 36.8 mm footprint creates intense thermal density across PCB power planes.
