    Hamamatsu Photonics Commercializes iPHEMOS-DDX Inverted Emission Microscope for Dynamic Multi-Die and HBM Failure Analysis

    1. Executive Summary

    Heterogeneous 2.5D and 3D architectures integrating compute chiplets with dense High Bandwidth Memory stacks frequently develop microbump open/shorts, TSV timing degradation, and localized IR-drop hot spots that only appear dynamically under operational clock speeds. Standard automated pin testing and destructive cross-sectioning cannot isolate these active-state faults. To solve this packaging yield barrier, Hamamatsu Photonics commercialized the iPHEMOS-DDX (Model C18040-11), an inverted optical emission microscope priced at 160 million JPY (~$1.02 million USD). Featuring a 3-directional direct ATE docking interface, the system injects full-speed functional vectors while conducting non-destructive backside optical characterization, compressing failure analysis cycle times from weeks of physical slicing down to hours of live fault isolation across substrates exceeding 100 mm × 100 mm.

    Key Concepts (Category 6: HW-Circuit-Research)
    • PEM (Photon Emission Microscopy): Detection of faint infrared photon emissions generated by abnormal carrier recombination, dielectric leakage, and forward-biased semiconductor junctions.
    • EOFM / EOP: Electro-optical frequency mapping and probing utilizing reflected laser phase shifts to measure high-speed internal logic states and clock skew without physical micro-probing.
    • OBIRCH: Optical beam induced resistance change scanning that maps thermal resistance variations to identify localized voids, cracks, and necking in metal traces.
    • Direct ATE Docking: Rigid mechanical coupling of automated test heads directly to the analytical chamber, eliminating cable harness signal degradation during full-speed testing.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    The system mounts an automated test head from the left, right, or top orientations, driving the device under test (DUT) with full-frequency vectors while the optical stage inspects the thinned backside silicon substrate. This configuration captures transient timing degradation and voltage drops across active multi-die interconnects.
