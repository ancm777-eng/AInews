    A Call for Collective Action on Cyber Defense: Frontier AI and Critical Infrastructure Resilience

    1. Abstract

The declaration frames autonomous agentic cyber operations as a structural shift: multi-step systems can discover vulnerabilities, synthesize exploits, and conduct lateral movement at machine speed, creating an urgent need for defensive AI deployment, verified threat-telemetry sharing, and early defensive access to advanced models.

The coalition instead identifies a continuous contest between autonomous offensive and defensive systems. Agentic attackers can apply automated zero-shot binary disassembly, fuzzing, exploit construction, and persistent reconnaissance across hybrid cloud, edge, enterprise IT, and legacy municipal operational technology.     The declaration establishes broad normative alignment but does not create binding enforcement, guaranteed funding, or an implementation path for cash-constrained municipal utilities. The next bottleneck is operational trust: shared telemetry must be verified, remediation must remain safe in fragile OT environments, and early frontier-model access must avoid leakage of weights or execution harnesses. The coalition could accelerate a transition from human-managed SOCs to AI-driven defense fabrics, but its practical effect will depend on governance, local deployment capacity, secure hardware roots of trust, and accountable procurement.

    EnvHarness: Awakening Static Worlds for Adaptive Agent Learning

    1. Abstract

    Google Cloud AI Research, Washington University in St. Louis, and UNC Chapel Hill released EnvHarness: Awakening Static Worlds for Agent Learning on August 30, 2026 (arXiv:2608.19880). The open-source Python framework converts static agent benchmarks into policy-adaptive training worlds through non-invasive programmable wrappers, preserving human-verified ground-truth evaluation while altering starting states, tools, and observations in response to policy failures.

    EnvHarness operates only through the standard reset(), step(), observe(), evaluate(), and get_env_state() interfaces. Its automated designer, EnvRigger, analyzes rollout failures and emits isolated Python _Rules subclasses. These rules perturb initial conditions, action spaces, tool constraints, and observation spaces without editing the benchmark’s underlying dynamics or deterministic grading oracle.

    Five-layer execution path
    1. Agent Policy Model — tool invocation, code generation, and multi-turn action selection.
    2. EnvRigger Designer — policy-failure analysis and isolated wrapper-rule synthesis.
    3. EnvHarness Wrapper — interception of reset and step signals.
    4. Ground-Truth Oracle — deterministic human-authored validation.
    5. Base Simulation World — Linux CLI, browser container, or repository sandbox.

    The framework supports SWE-bench Verified, WebArena, ALFWorld, WebShop, OfficeQA, and SpreadsheetBench, with reference drivers compatible with PyTorch and standard LLM API endpoints. This design differs from end-to-end synthetic environment generation: rather than introducing untrusted synthetic verifiers, EnvHarness changes the trajectory through which an agent encounters a benchmark while retaining the original human-verified reward logic. The codebase is released under Apache-2.0 through the Google Research GitHub repository.

    Infrastructure implication. Adaptive rollout loops raise host CPU and microVM execution demand because sandbox states and wrapper rules must be mutated in parallel across training trajectories.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

    EnvHarness creates a reusable path beyond benchmark saturation, allowing expensive human-curated tasks to yield more targeted curriculum signal without replacing their grading foundations. Its boundary is equally important: the approach requires resettable, deterministic environments and cannot directly govern live production databases, third-party authenticated services, or physical robotics systems where state reset is not guaranteed. Future adoption will depend on robust sandboxing, reproducible wrapper generation, and evidence that adaptive curricula improve generalization rather than merely producing more sophisticated benchmark-specific behavior.

The proposed bill would require qualifying frontier AI organizations to report telemetry to NIST and the Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) when internal systems exceed defined compute thresholds or demonstrate autonomous coding, tool-assisted infrastructure modification, and recursive self-improvement capabilities.

Reporting would be triggered by AI training compute exceeding 10^26 FLOPs or by internal agents granted autonomous execution privileges over research infrastructure. Required telemetry would be directed to NIST and CAISI, while civil enforcement and investigative authority would be coordinated through the Federal Trade Commission and Department of Commerce.

    • Periodic telemetry filings go to NIST and CAISI.
    • Federal enforcement coordination is assigned to the FTC and Department of Commerce.

    Unlike voluntary corporate commitments, the bill proposes statutory reporting. Unlike California SB 53’s focus on critical cybersecurity incident logging, H.R. 8924 focuses on dynamic autonomous R&D behavior: recursive software development, multi-agent containment verification, and tool manipulation by unreleased models. It has been referred to the House Committee on Science, Space, and Technology for legislative review and committee hearings.

    Infrastructure implication. If enacted, the proposal could increase demand for tamper-evident telemetry logs, isolated execution enclaves, certified evaluations, and compliance architectures that demonstrate behavior without exposing proprietary model weights.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

    H.R. 8924 remains proposed legislation and may face substantial amendment, committee delay, or failure to reach a floor vote. Its core technical challenge is definitional: regulators must distinguish ordinary automated CI/CD and experiment orchestration from autonomous recursive self-improvement without producing a loophole-rich or innovation-inhibiting standard. Committee deliberations will determine the eventual scope, reporting cadence, confidentiality model, and whether the proposed compute threshold meaningfully captures the systems that create the highest control and security risk.

    Thor Ultra at Hot Chips 2026: An 800GbE UEC NIC for 100,000+ Accelerator Clusters

    1. Abstract

    At the Hot Chips 2026 Symposium at Stanford University on August 25, Broadcom detailed Thor Ultra, an 800 Gb/s-per-direction full-duplex AI Ethernet NIC SoC built for Ultra Ethernet Consortium clusters exceeding 100,000 accelerator endpoints. Eight 100G PAM4 SerDes lanes, native Ultra Ethernet Transport packet spraying, and on-chip out-of-order reassembly are intended to reduce ECMP hash-collision bottlenecks in large-scale All-Reduce and All-to-All workloads while supporting low-power DAC and LPO interconnects.

    PAM4 SerDes — Physical-layer signaling lanes that encode four amplitude levels for high-speed electrical links.

    UET — Ultra Ethernet Transport, the UEC transport approach used for Ethernet AI fabrics.

    Packet spraying — Distribution of packets from one message across available network paths rather than pinning a flow to one path.

    LPO — Linear Pluggable Optics, optical modules that bypass DSP retimers to reduce power and latency.

    Thor Ultra implements native UEC UET while remaining backward compatible with RoCEv2. Conventional Ethernet relies on flow-level ECMP, where elephant flows may hash onto the same link and create queueing and tail-latency failures. Thor Ultra decomposes RDMA messages into granular packets, dynamically sprays them across available links, and uses a dedicated on-chip reorder engine to place out-of-order packets into host and GPU memory at line rate. The objective is high bisection-bandwidth efficiency without host CPU reassembly overhead or proprietary subnet management.

    Hardware data path
    1. RDMA message enters the 800GbE NIC through eight 100G PAM4 SerDes lanes.
    2. Native UET hardware distributes individual packets across multiple paths.
    3. Receiver-credit flow control and sub-microsecond link monitoring adjust injection rates.
    4. On-chip reorder buffers reassemble packets into host and GPU memory at line rate.

    The NIC supports passive Direct Attach Copper (DAC), Active Electrical Cables (AEC), Linear Pluggable Optics (LPO), and standard retimed optical transceivers. Short-reach DAC can avoid the stated 15–20W optical-transceiver power penalty per port, while LPO removes power-hungry DSP retimers from optical modules for inter-rack links. Broadcom positions this physical-layer flexibility alongside hardware receiver-credit flow control to manage bursty collective-communication phases in high-density 800G racks.

    Deployment status. Silicon is sampling to major hyperscale cloud operators and AI server OEM partners; volume production and infrastructure deployment are targeted for 2027 buildouts.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

    Thor Ultra strengthens open Ultra Ethernet as a scale-out alternative to proprietary fabrics, but packet spraying transfers a critical burden into NIC buffer design and congestion behavior. Sufficient on-chip memory is required to sustain reassembly under heavy contention, and extreme switch drop rates can create Ethernet retransmission overhead and latency spikes relative to lossless credit-based fabrics. The next validation points are UEC interoperability results, sustained All-to-All behavior at 100,000+ endpoints, signal-integrity performance across DAC and LPO deployments, and the thermal consequences of dense 800G infrastructure.
