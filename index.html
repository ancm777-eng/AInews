Marvell Quantifies "Custom HBM as a System" Area Reclamation and Stack Thermal Resistance

1. Executive Summary

At 2026 memory prices, High-Bandwidth Memory accounts for over 50% of accelerator BOM cost and ~30% of 2.5D package footprint, while wide 2,048-bit HBM4 PHYs squeeze compute logic off leading-edge XPUs. Expanding on its 2026 OCP Global Summit disclosure on November 17, Marvell detailed how offloading HBM controllers onto a custom logic base die reclaims up to 25% of compute die area.

However, placing active Die-to-Die (D2D) PHY circuits beneath a DRAM stack exposes an acute thermal asymmetry: Samsung IEEE ECTC 2026 data cited by Marvell shows internal HBM stack resistance accounts for 57% of its vertical thermal path versus 13% for an adjacent ASIC, driving a system co-design roadmap around Cu-Cu hybrid bonding and silicon heat paths.

Key Hardware & Packaging Concepts

  • Custom HBM Base Die: A foundry-built logic layer beneath stacked DRAM dies that hosts offloaded memory controllers so the XPU only needs a compact D2D interface.
  • D2D PHY (Die-to-Die Physical Interface): A narrow, high-density electrical bridge replacing wide perimeter HBM PHYs on the compute die to reclaim up to 25% silicon area.
  • Cu-Cu Hybrid Bonding: Direct copper-to-copper dielectric bonding that eliminates ~30 μm microbump and underfill gaps, boosting vertical heat flow while shrinking stack height.
  • Vertical Thermal Path Resistance: The cumulative impedance to heat escaping upward into the cold plate; high internal DRAM stack impedance traps heat generated in the base die underneath.

2. Architecture & Performance

As per-stack bandwidth scales roughly 8x from 0.5 TB/s (HBM2E) toward 4.1 TB/s (HBM4E), replacing wide perimeter PHYs with a narrow D2D PHY relieves reticle congestion on ~800 mm² compute dies. Yet relocating switching logic beneath high-resistance DRAM layers creates localized hotspot risk unless the TIM, cold plate, and stack interfaces are co-engineered.

Synthesizing cross-study benchmarks, Marvell outlines four mitigations: Cu-Cu hybrid bonding removes ~30 μm microbump gaps to cut D2D thermal resistance by 23–47%, lift vertical thermal conductivity up to 3x, and reduce stack height by >15%; non-conductive silicon heat paths cut PHY resistance by >30%; adaptive D2D power gating targets up to 75% normal (42% peak) power savings; and research-stage etched silicon microfluidics target ~2–4 kW per ~800 mm² die.

AMD and UW-Madison Unveil PACER to Mitigate LLM Power Excursions on 750W GPUs

1. Executive Summary

As AI accelerators scale from 750W to 1,400W+ TDP, bursty LLM kernels generate rapid load steps that trigger Electrical Design Power peaks (EDPp) above rated thermal limits. On November 12, 2026, researchers from AMD and the University of Wisconsin–Madison published PACER, an empirical 1-millisecond telemetry study across 750W AMD Instinct MI300X GPUs running LLaMA3-70B, Qwen3-14B, DeepSeek-V3, and Stable Diffusion.

Roughly 80% of power peaks hit 1.0x–1.2x TDP, while a long tail (<1%) spikes to 1.7x TDP (>1,275W), tripping unsynchronized 1–2 ms DPM firmware throttling. By replacing coarse clamping with coordinated 1.2x–1.3x TDP excursion control, PACER delivers 3–12% TTFT and throughput gains (within 3% of an oracle) and up to 6% higher SLO attainment.

Key Hardware & Packaging Concepts

  • TDP vs. EDPp: Thermal Design Power (750W) is the sustained cooling ceiling, whereas Electrical Design Power peaks (EDPp) are brief board-power surges driven by dense compute kernels.
  • DPM Firmware Loop: Dynamic Power Management firmware polls power every 1–2 ms; because it runs unsynchronized across GPUs and slower than short LLM kernels, it causes asymmetric clock throttling.
  • Sub-Millisecond di/dt & PDN Stress: Microsecond current ramps deplete decoupling capacitors and induce VRM voltage droop before 1 ms board-power counters can observe them.
  • Computational Sprinting: Allowing brief, bounded excursions (1.2x–1.3x TDP) preserves peak frequency during short prefill bursts without triggering destructive tail overshoots.

2. Architecture & Performance

Existing DPM firmware polls Total Board Power on an unsynchronized 1–2 ms loop that misses sub-millisecond kernel bursts and clamps clocks asymmetrically across GPUs, introducing inter-GPU frequency skew. Conversely, aggressively clamping every excursion at 1.0x TDP (750W) eliminates overshoots at the expense of stalling productive bursts.

PACER resolves this trade-off with a coordinated hardware-firmware threshold set at 1.2x–1.3x TDP, permitting brief computational sprinting while clipping extreme 1.7x TDP tail spikes. Evaluated on production MI300X systems running vLLM and LongBench, PACER recovers 3–12% across TTFT and throughput metrics while improving SLO attainment by up to 6%.

3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

Firmware-hardware excursion tuning offers an immediate, zero-silicon-change lever to extract 3–12% more cluster performance, provided sub-millisecond electrical limits and rack-scale coordination are respected.

  • 1 ms Telemetry Blind Spot: Millisecond board-power counters average over microsecond-scale di/dt current spikes where silicon voltage droop, package-inductance ringing, and decoupling-capacitor depletion actually occur.
  • VRM Compliance Dependencies: Clamping average power to 1.2x–1.3x TDP does not independently guarantee VRM electrical compliance, which remains governed by spike duration, peak current (I_max), and droop margins.
  • 72-GPU Rack Scaling Challenge: Extending PACER to liquid-cooled racks (such as Vera Rubin NVL72 or Helios MI455X) will require rack-level synchronization so concurrent 1.2x–1.3x TDP sprints do not trip 48V/800V bus-bar protection.
