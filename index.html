    onsemi Acquires Ferric for $600 Million: Securing In-Package Integrated Voltage Regulators (IVR) for High-Current AI Accelerators

    1. Executive Summary

    Modern AI processors drawing over 1,000 Watts at sub-0.8V core rails face severe board-level resistive losses and high parasitic inductance during sharp compute load transitions. Addressing this physical packaging constraint, onsemi executed a definitive agreement to acquire Ferric for $600 million to integrate thin-film magnetic Integrated Voltage Regulators (IVRs) directly into 2.5D/3D chip packages. By compressing power loop inductance from 1.0 nH down to under 0.1 nH, this in-package approach eliminates transient voltage droop, avoids compute clock throttling, and reclaims valuable motherboard space for high-density interconnect routing.

    Key Concepts:

    • Integrated Voltage Regulator (IVR): A power converter incorporating micro-inductors directly onto a substrate or interposer, bypassing board-level routing losses.
    • Parasitic Loop Inductance: Unintended magnetic inductance along PCB power paths that causes severe supply voltage drops during steep di/dt current swings.
    • Transient Voltage Droop: An instantaneous supply rail sag during peak compute execution, which causes logic timing errors unless mitigated by fast point-of-load regulation.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    Ferric's micro-fabricated thin-film magnetic inductors integrate directly into semiconductor back-end-of-line (BEOL) and package interposer layers. This migration bypasses the high-impedance PCB copper distribution planes characteristic of conventional multiphase VRMs.
