    Samsung Unveils CUBE Roadmap at SEMICON Taiwan: HBM5 with 2nm Base Die and 3D Direct-Stacked zHBM

    1. Abstract

    At SEMICON Taiwan 2026, Samsung Electronics unveiled its comprehensive CUBE memory roadmap, establishing next-generation microarchitectural targets for HBM5 and revolutionary direct-stacked zHBM architectures. HBM5 incorporates an in-house 2nm Gate-All-Around foundry base die, up to 20 vertical DRAM stacking layers, and an integrated Heat Path Block (HPB) to achieve twice the aggregate stack bandwidth of HBM4E alongside a 20% reduction in package thermal resistance by 2028. For post-2029 exascale workloads, Samsung introduced zHBM, a 3D topology that eliminates 2.5D interposer trace losses by bonding DRAM dies vertically directly on top of host accelerator logic using direct copper-to-copper hybrid bonding.

    Key Technical Concepts

    • Heat Path Block (HPB): An integrated thermal dissipation architecture embedding specialized high-conductivity thermal dummy vias to conduct heat out through the upper surface of dense DRAM stacks into liquid cold plates.
    • Hybrid Cu-Cu Bonding: A solderless direct wafer-to-wafer or die-to-wafer interconnect technology eliminating micro-bumps to achieve sub-micron interconnect pitches and dramatically reduce parasitic capacitance.
    • zHBM Heterogeneous 3D Stacking: An architecture placing high-density DRAM stacks directly vertically atop the host logic compute die, circumventing 2.5D interposer reticle boundaries and trace attenuation.
    • Transient di/dt Voltage Droop: High-frequency voltage fluctuations induced along vertical power delivery networks during simultaneous multi-layer DRAM switching bursts, mitigated via on-chip deep trench decoupling capacitors.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    In conventional 2.5D accelerator configurations, placing 12 to 16 HBM stacks horizontally around a host logic die requires silicon interposers exceeding 3.3 times standard reticle dimensions, introducing severe warpage, fabrication yield penalties, and millimeter-long high-speed trace losses. HBM5 and zHBM solve these limits through foundry-node base die scaling and direct vertical interconnects.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

    Vertical assembly of 20-high DRAM stacks on a 2nm base die imposes extreme physical yield challenges. Thinning silicon dies below 30 micrometers drastically elevates wafer warp and micro-fracture susceptibility during high-speed thermo-compression or hybrid bonding. Furthermore, while zHBM circumvents lateral interposer trace penalties, dissipating up to 1,000 watts of thermal flux generated by the underlying host accelerator die directly through sensitive DRAM memory cells will necessitate unproven microfluidic or direct backside liquid cooling manifolds to prevent data retention failure.
