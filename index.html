    Hugging Face’s Summer 2026 Census Maps an Open-Model Economy of Extreme Concentration

    1. Abstract

Enterprise demand will therefore stay heterogeneous: 70B+ sovereign weights require cloud-scale serving, while sub-8B distilled models can remain local or edge-deployed.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

However, high switching speeds introduce difficult EMI and high-dV/dt control requirements, demanding precise gate-driver layout and magnetic shielding. The migration from 6-inch to 8-inch GaN-on-silicon wafers must also preserve defect-density discipline to avoid yield losses in multi-kilowatt modules.

    NVIDIA’s Prospective Feynman Platform Points to A16 Backside Power, 3D SoIC, and Co-Packaged Optics

    1. Abstract

    Supply-chain reports indicate that NVIDIA’s prospective second-half 2028 Feynman GPU platform is aligning with TSMC’s A16-class process, Super Power Rail backside power delivery, 3D SoIC stacking, HBM4E logic-base integration, and Co-Packaged Optics. The roadmap targets rack bisection bandwidth above 1,000 TB/s—1 PB/s—while TSMC expands SoIC capacity toward approximately 50,000 wafers per month by end-2027.

    BSPDN — Backside power delivery moves power rails below the active silicon layers, releasing frontside routing for signals.

    SoIC — Direct vertical die-to-die stacking without microbumps for 3D heterogeneous integration.

    CPO — Co-Packaged Optics places photonic engines beside compute or switching silicon to replace lossy electrical links.

    PDN — The on-chip power distribution network that governs voltage drop, impedance, and transient-current delivery.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    Conventional frontside power delivery forces power, ground, and high-speed signals to share microscopic metal layers, creating routing congestion and IR voltage drop. TSMC A16’s Super Power Rail BSPDN shifts power rails to the wafer backside, lowering PDN impedance and removing power vias from frontside routing. This supports higher transient current delivery to sub-1V compute logic while preserving signal-routing resources.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

    The roadmap would deepen NVIDIA’s advantage through early A16 and SoIC capacity alignment while shifting datacenter design toward liquid-cooled, multi-megawatt pods and optical switch fabrics. Manufacturing risk remains substantial: A16 BSPDN requires wafer thinning and backside lithography that may face early yield volatility, while embedded optical engines introduce optical-alignment, thermal, and repairability challenges. External laser sources or detachable optical connectors may be necessary so that a single optical failure does not invalidate an entire multi-die compute assembly.
