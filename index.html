    The central economic mechanism is a divergence between unit and aggregate consumption. Earlier enterprise conversational systems generally mapped one prompt to one direct response; their budgets followed relatively linear query-to-token assumptions. Agentic systems instead execute iterative planning, dynamic tool calling, environment-state verification, error-recovery retries, and multi-model reflection. Lower token prices can therefore subsidize deeper execution loops rather than reduce total operating expenditure.

    Runtime architecture shift
    • 2024–2025: single-turn prompt and direct response through a single monolithic LLM endpoint.
    • 2026–2028 forecast: multi-step autonomous planning and execution through a heterogeneous tiered model-routing pipeline.
    • Commercial consequence: flat per-user or per-seat subscriptions give way to metered outcome pricing or consumption tiers.
    • Infrastructure consequence: high-throughput, low-latency inference clusters must absorb bursty, high-concurrency agent execution.

    Gartner recommends tiered multimodel orchestration: lightweight models for task triage and frontier models for complex reasoning. Supporting components include automated routing middleware, state-caching fabrics, deterministic task filters, execution tracing, and intermediate-token pruning. These systems become core margin-control mechanisms, not optional optimizations, because aggregate workflow cost rises as autonomous loops compound volume across planning and execution stages.

    Strategic implication. Enterprise leaders should evaluate AI products by completed-outcome cost rather than headline token price. Routing proxies, semantic caching, constrained generation, and observability platforms become direct controls on software gross margin.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

    The fivefold projection is explicitly dependent on system design rather than an unavoidable physical law. Organizations using deterministic control flows, speculative decoding, constrained output generation, aggressive semantic caching, and disciplined escalation policies may avoid runaway consumption. The near-term challenge is architectural: enterprises must establish runtime governance before agent loops become embedded in pricing models and user expectations. The likely market shift is from generalized chatbot deployment toward cost-aware agent platforms that expose, budget, and optimize execution paths at the workflow level.

    OCP Establishes an Open Silicon Photonics Roadmap for 100,000-XPU AI Fabrics

    1. Abstract

    On August 17, 2026, following coalition expansion announced on August 13, the Open Compute Project formally launched its Open Silicon Photonics for AI Systems workstream. Led by Lightmatter and 19 technology leaders, the initiative released a 300-page foundational white paper for vendor-neutral Co-Packaged Optics reference architectures spanning OCP Modular Hardware System and Open Rack v3 environments, with a scale-out vision extending from 72-node pods to clusters of up to 100,000 XPUs.

    CPO — Co-Packaged Optics integrates optical engines near compute or switching silicon on a shared substrate.

    PIC — A Photonic Integrated Circuit routes and processes optical signals within an integrated optical device.

    SerDes — Serializer/deserializer circuitry converts parallel on-chip data into high-speed serial streams.

    ELS — External Laser Sources can separate heat-sensitive laser generation from high-power compute packages.

    The workstream addresses a physical constraint in ultra-scale AI clusters: copper interconnects accumulate insertion loss and thermal burden as per-lane SerDes rates reach 224 Gbps and approach 448 Gbps. Electrical transmission over boards and passive copper cables consumes excessive power and limits practical reach to tens of centimeters. CPO reduces electrical trace length by placing optical engines and PICs near compute or switching silicon, improving bandwidth density while reducing interconnect power.

    Open CPO system architecture
    1. Layer 1 — Accelerator Silicon: Host GPU / XPU Compute Die executes tensor math and attention operations.
    2. Layer 2 — Heterogeneous Substrate: CPO Optical Engine Interface integrates Electronic ICs and Photonic ICs.
    3. Layer 3 — Optical Connector Array: Precision Micro-Fiber Interface routes detachable optical connectors into waveguides.
    4. Layer 4 — Rack Interconnect Fabric: OCP ORv3 Optical Backplane replaces dense copper cabling with optical routing.
    5. Layer 5 — Scale-Out Switch Matrix: Optical Switch Fabric Domain scales interconnectivity across pods up to 100,000 XPUs.

    The architectural vision aligns future specifications with MHS and ORv3, while remaining compatible with silicon photonics, VCSELs, and micro-LED optical engines. Participating organizations include Celestica, Corning, Dell Technologies, Flex, Foxconn Interconnect Technology, Global Unichip Corporation, Hyve Solutions, Keysight, Qualcomm Technologies, and Quanta Cloud Technology. Compared with rack-faceplate pluggable transceivers, which require long PCB traces and power-intensive DSPs, CPO seeks to eliminate high-power retimers and mitigate signal insertion loss through package-adjacent optical conversion.

    Layout consequence. CPO eliminates lossy, high-layer-count PCB SerDes channels, but shifts complexity into mechanical tolerance control, optical connector arrays, and thermal-circuit co-design. Heat-sensitive lasers and modulators must be isolated from high-power GPU dies without compromising serviceability.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

    The workstream has released an architectural vision rather than a finalized interoperable product standard; multi-vendor reference specifications, electrical and optical interface guidelines, and interoperability test suites remain under development. Its largest limitation is field serviceability: permanently bonded optical engines can render an entire accelerator assembly inoperable if a laser or modulator fails, unlike hot-swappable pluggables. Progress therefore depends on robust detachable optical interfaces, standardized ELS designs, manufacturable micro-optical packaging, and validation that open CPO systems can meet hyperscaler reliability and repair requirements at scale.
