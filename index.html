    Directional Silicon Crystallization for Ultra-High-Stack 3D NAND Channels

    1. Abstract

    Samsung Electronics and POSTECH presented a nickel seed-layer and microwave-annealing process for directional silicon crystallization at the 2026 IEEE VLSI Technology and Circuits Symposium, later highlighted in Nature Electronics. Demonstrated on a 155-layer 3D NAND test vehicle, the technique promotes preferred crystal orientation along the vertical channel axis, reducing grain-boundary scattering and addressing channel-resistance constraints as NAND moves toward 400-plus layers.

    3D NAND — vertically stacked non-volatile memory cells connected through deep channel structures.

    Grain boundary — an interface between crystal domains that impedes electron mobility.

    Nickel seed layer — a thin metallic template used to initiate preferred silicon crystal orientation.

    Microwave annealing — electromagnetic thermal treatment enabling targeted, lower-temperature crystallization.

    A vertical wordline gate stack addresses memory cells above a nickel thin-film seed layer. During microwave annealing, the seed interface directs a crystallized silicon channel into a lower-resistance vertical conduction path, connecting to the underlying logic substrate for I/O signaling and control.

    Packaging relevance. Lower channel resistance can reduce heat dissipation inside ultra-dense flash packages, expanding electrical and thermal margin for high-throughput enterprise SSDs and near-storage AI acceleration boards without exceeding standard power-delivery budgets.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

    The research offers a materials-and-process route to one of the central physical bottlenecks in vertically scaled memory: randomized polycrystalline silicon growth increases resistance as channels become taller, whereas seed-assisted directional alignment can preserve conductivity. Commercialization remains early-stage. Integrating nickel into high-volume fabrication raises contamination and qualification challenges, because residual nickel ions could migrate into neighboring dielectric or gate-oxide regions, creating parasitic leakage or endurance degradation. Future work must therefore establish contamination controls, process repeatability, and reliability across production-scale ultra-high-stack NAND roadmaps.
