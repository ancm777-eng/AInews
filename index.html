    2. Architecture & Performance

Department of Defense et al., Case No. 3:26-cv-01482-RFL in the Northern District of California, arose after Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth designated Anthropic a national-security supply-chain risk and directed military contractors and federal agencies to cease use of Anthropic technology. The designation followed CEO Dario Amodei’s refusal to remove contractual restrictions on autonomous kinetic weapons and domestic surveillance.

    Hot Chips 2026: Cerebras CS-4 Nexus Reworks Wafer-Scale Power and Memory Architecture

    1. Abstract

    At Hot Chips 2026, Cerebras Systems detailed the CS-4 system on its Nexus modular rack platform: three TSMC 5nm WSE-3 Turbo wafer-scale engines per rack, each delivering 43.2 PB/s (43,200 TB/s) of raw on-chip SRAM bandwidth. The architecture couples wafer-scale compute with rear-adjacent point-of-load power conversion, directly targeting the memory-wall and power-delivery limits that constrain autoregressive token decoding in discrete GPU clusters.

    WSE-3T — A WSE-3 Turbo monolithic wafer-scale engine with 4 trillion transistors and 900,000 cores.

    PDN — The physical power-delivery network routing regulator output to silicon logic.

    PoL — Point-of-load conversion placed close to the compute package to reduce voltage droop and resistive loss.

    Prefill / Decode — Long-context ingestion and memory-bandwidth-bound autoregressive token generation, respectively.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    The CS-4 houses three WSE-3T engines in a Nexus modular rack. Each wafer is a 46,225 square millimeter monolithic TSMC 5nm device with 4 trillion transistors, 900,000 cores, 250 PFLOPS of tensor compute, 44GB of on-chip SRAM, and 43.2 PB/s (43,200 TB/s) raw SRAM bandwidth. At rack level, the configuration delivers approximately 750 PFLOPS of dense/sparse FP16/FP8 tensor performance and 132GB aggregate SRAM.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

    Commercial sampling and initial cluster deployments are underway, with production-volume deliveries ramping through H2 2026. A single WSE-3T’s 43,200 TB/s raw SRAM bandwidth is more than 670 times the 24–64 TB/s aggregate memory-bandwidth range cited for discrete GPU server nodes, but capacity remains the limiting resource: the CS-4 rack holds 132GB of SRAM. Frontier models exceeding hundreds of billions of parameters and ultra-long context workloads therefore require heterogeneous, disaggregated systems combining wafer engines with high-capacity HBM GPU clusters. The strategic bottleneck shifts toward 3D wafer-scale DRAM, advanced hybrid bonding, liquid cooling, and serviceable PoL power modules.
