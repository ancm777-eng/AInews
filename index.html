South Korea–U.S. AI Infrastructure Framework: Integrated Capital, Compute, and Semiconductor Capacity

1. Abstract

The San Francisco AI Summit formalized a non-binding $1 trillion (1,400 trillion KRW) South Korea–U.S. framework linking AI factories, HBM4 supply, 2nm GAA foundry capacity, advanced packaging, and heterogeneous compute. Its principal commitments combine SK Group’s $500B+ NVIDIA arrangement for a 2GW Vera Rubin DSX AI Cloud, Samsung’s $200B Broadcom MOU through 2030, NAVER’s $10B GAK Sejong expansion, and MSIT’s $1.2B AMD open-computing initiative.

2. Architecture & Performance

The framework shifts procurement from discrete component transactions toward co-investment across compute, logic fabrication, memory, packaging, power delivery, and cloud software. SK Group’s agreement covers Vera Rubin systems and SK hynix HBM4 within a broader $750B five-year semiconductor investment commitment; Samsung’s scope combines HBM4, 2nm Gate-All-Around logic, and 2.5D interposer integration. NAVER plans to expand GAK Sejong from 55MW to 200MW by 2028, targeting approximately 100,000 Vera Rubin GPUs and HyperCLOVA X optimization on Nemotron 3 Ultra. Hyundai Motor Group separately disclosed deployment of 50,000 NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs for autonomous-vehicle and robotics simulation.

Source: Ministry of Science and ICT / SK hynix Newsroom / Arirang News / Seoul Economic Daily (2026-07-27)

MSIT’s AMD AI Center of Excellence is designed as an open heterogeneous alternative: AMD Zen 6 CPUs and Instinct GPUs will interoperate with domestic Korean NPUs rather than relying solely on a single-vendor GPU cluster. Bilateral MOUs are signed, while site engineering, PDK sharing, and heterogeneous-cluster testbeds are active for initial 2027 deployment.

3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

The announced commitments establish Korea as a prospective international hub for frontier-AI hardware and packaging, but execution remains contingent on transmission capacity, transformer availability, liquid-cooling deployment, technology-transfer approvals, and local permitting. Because the umbrella arrangement and several constituent instruments are non-binding LOIs or MOUs, committed capital should not be treated as equivalent to deployed infrastructure until engineering milestones, power interconnection schedules, and supply contracts are converted into enforceable delivery programs.

Power Is the Binding Constraint

A 2GW AI factory and a 200MW data-center target require grid, transformer, and cooling programs to progress in parallel with GPU procurement.

NVIDIA Agent Toolkit for Engineering: Agentic Multiphysics Acceleration Across EDA Workflows

1. Abstract

NVIDIA expanded its Agent Toolkit for Engineering with re-architected PhysicsNeMo libraries and CUDA-X tools that connect LLM-based agent reasoning to GPU-accelerated physical solvers for semiconductor design, verification, packaging, and multiphysics engineering. Cadence and Samsung report up to 20x acceleration in multiphysics simulation and computational lithography, while Siemens EDA reports >10x faster library characterization and >10x lower token execution costs.

Key Concepts

EDA
Software used to design, simulate, verify, and prepare integrated circuits for fabrication.
PhysicsNeMo
GPU-accelerated libraries that apply physics-informed AI and numerical methods to engineering simulation.
RTL
Hardware-description code that specifies a digital design’s registers, logic, and data flow.
DRC / LVS
Signoff checks for layout-rule compliance and for matching physical layout against circuit schematics.

2. Architecture & Performance

At sub-2nm nodes, 3D chiplets, and multi-kilowatt power envelopes, conventional EDA flows face iterative bottlenecks in physical layout, signal integrity, power integrity, and thermal stress. NVIDIA’s architecture joins LLM agents to accelerated solvers: cuDSS for direct sparse solving, cuISS for iterative sparse solving, and cuEST for quantum-chemistry simulation. Nemotron 3 Ultra also delivered state-of-the-art results in agentic RTL coding on the Verilog design benchmark.

Source: NVIDIA Developer Blog / Cadence / Siemens EDA / Samsung (2026-07-26)

Cadence integrated PhysicsNeMo and CUDA-X acceleration into AuraStack for multiphysics and thermal-layout simulation. Siemens EDA combined NeMo Gym with Fuse EDA AI Agent loops for cell-library timing characterization. Samsung applied PhysicsNeMo to chip-scale thermal-stress analysis spanning up to 10 billion cells and deployed cuLitho for computational lithography. The libraries are live on the NVIDIA Developer portal, with commercial EDA deployments scheduled through H2 2026.

3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

Agentic physics workflows can shorten simulation and optimization cycles for custom AI ASICs, high-speed SerDes interconnects, and 3D chiplet packages, but accelerated prediction remains distinct from signoff correctness. Agent-generated layouts must continue through formal DRC/LVS, equivalence verification, and electrical validation because numerical approximations can conceal subtle interconnect, timing, thermal, or power-delivery defects before a multi-million-dollar tapeout.

Acceleration Is Not Signoff

Simulation speedups reduce iteration time, but they do not replace formal verification gates required before silicon fabrication.
