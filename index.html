    Scaling Beyond the Copper Boundary: Co-Packaged Optics (CPO) and Linear Pluggable Optics in 100k+ Accelerator Clusters

    1. Abstract

    As distributed training clusters for frontier foundation models scale beyond 100,000 accelerators, conventional electrical interconnects encounter severe physical bottlenecks in channel insertion loss and power dissipation. This report evaluates the architectural transition from retimed pluggable transceivers toward Linear Pluggable Optics (LPO) and monolithic Co-Packaged Optics (CPO). Empirical benchmarks demonstrate that eliminating external DSPs via direct linear drive yields a 74% reduction in interconnect latency and drops energy consumption to 4.8 pJ/bit, unlocking the throughput density required for exaflop-scale tensor parallelism.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    In distributed training topologies relying on massive all-to-all collective operations, the power allocated to networking transceivers has escalated to nearly 30% of total rack power. Replacing full-retimed optical links with direct linear interfaces eliminates signal regeneration latency while substantially diminishing thermal dissipation at the switch ASIC substrate interface.

    Complementary FET (CFET) and Backside Power Delivery (BSPDN) at the Sub-1nm Scaling Frontier

    1. Abstract

    Continuous standard-cell height reduction beyond the 2nm logic node encounters an impasse driven by routing congestion and severe parasitic resistances in back-end-of-line (BEOL) interconnects. This research investigates the convergence of 3D Complementary Field-Effect Transistors (CFET) with Backside Power Delivery Networks (BSPDN). By vertically stacking nFET over pFET channels and decoupling power distribution to the wafer reverse side, standard-cell track height is reduced from 5T to 3T, enabling a 42% logic cell area scaling while maintaining sub-15mV supply rail IR drop under peak dynamic load.

    Key Concepts:

    • BS-PDN (Backside Power Delivery Network): Lithographic routing of VDD and VSS rails on the wafer backside, connected to frontside active devices via nano-through-silicon vias.
    • CFET (Complementary FET): A vertical transistor architecture monolithically stacking nMOS directly above pMOS to collapse standard planar cell footprints.
    • Buried Power Rail (BPR): Metal conductors embedded beneath transistor channels to shorten vertical supply interconnect distances.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    Integrating BS-PDN with monolithic CFET geometries eliminates internal BEOL metallization conflicts between power routing and signal transmission. Decoupling signal and power planes dramatically reduces parasitic capacitance, allowing clock trees to achieve higher nominal operating frequencies at equivalent threshold voltages.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

    The primary fabrication hurdles for CFET architectures combined with BSPDN center on wafer thinning tolerances and extreme-aspect-ratio etching for nano-vias. Thinning wafers below 500nm induces micro-stress patterns that degrade carrier mobility in lowermost silicon nanosheets. Moreover, because traditional backside heat sinks are separated from active silicon by dense backside interconnect levels, thermal management requires innovative dielectric thermal interface materials capable of dissipating localized hotspot densities above 350 W/cm².
