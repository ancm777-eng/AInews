By processing live audio streams in continuous 80ms chunks, the system maintains conversational cadence, detects barge-ins, and synchronizes 24 kHz synthetic speech with Live2D kinematics—while offloading intensive web retrieval and planning tasks to background workers.     Consumer-Grade MoE Execution: llama-modchip v2.4 Compresses 284B Parameters to 64GB Desktop RAM

    1. Executive Summary

    Deploying large Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) foundation models typically requires multi-GPU server clusters, putting local execution beyond the reach of independent engineers. The release of open-source llama-modchip v2.4.0 removes this hardware barrier by applying heterogeneous structural compression to DeepSeek-V4-Flash (284.33B total parameters, 13.17B active). By combining second-order SwiGLU-H2 Hessian pruning on sparse expert layers with a 3.75-bit Fractal-64 representation on attention layers, the runtime reduces memory requirements from the 76.87 GiB IQ2_XXS baseline down to 56.56 GiB. This enables execution on commodity desktop systems with 64GB DDR5 RAM without discrete accelerators, delivering up to 14.7 tokens/second via AVX-512BW speculative decoding.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    Standard uniform 2-bit quantization formats (such as IQ2_XXS) cannot run within 64GB RAM constraints due to memory page limits and operating system overhead. The llama-modchip runtime implements heterogeneous tensor allocation: dense Multi-Head Latent Attention (MLA) layers receive higher precision preservation (3.75 bits/weight), whereas sparse Feed-Forward Network (FFN) experts undergo aggressive Hessian-weighted pruning. This achieves an aggregate density of approximately 1.71 bits per parameter, allowing an operating resident footprint (VmRSS) of 57.61 to 58.9 GiB.

    Next-Generation Power Topology: Envision Galaxy Campus Deploys 800V DC Solid-State Transformer Microgrid

    1. Executive Summary

    Multi-year utility interconnection delays and transmission bottlenecks are pushing gigawatt-scale AI infrastructure to re-evaluate power delivery systems. A field investigation by SemiAnalysis reveals that Envision Group's Galaxy Campus in Inner Mongolia has commissioned an initial 120MW compute phase in 12 months, achieving infrastructure costs under $3.0M per megawatt. The site deploys an innovative 800V DC power distribution bus enabled by Solid-State Transformers (SST), converting medium-voltage 10 kV AC directly to 800V DC. By bypassing multi-stage conversion steps, integrating on-site wind generation, and implementing direct-to-chip liquid cooling, the design reduces distribution losses while delivering electricity at approximately $0.055 per kWh.

    Solid-State Transformer (SST): A power semiconductor-driven conversion system utilizing silicon carbide (SiC) switching to step medium-voltage AC directly down to high-voltage DC without bulky low-frequency electromagnetic transformer cores.

    800V DC Distribution Bus: An efficient centralized data hall power architecture that replaces traditional 480V/415V AC distribution, eliminating intermediate AC-DC rectification cycles and lowering conductor resistance losses.

    Direct-to-Chip Liquid Cooling: Thermal dissipation architecture delivering dielectric coolant directly over processor heat spreaders, operating alongside exterior ambient dry coolers to remove central mechanical chillers.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    Traditional data centers route grid power through multiple transformation stages: medium-voltage AC steps down to 480V AC, rectifies to DC for battery backup, converts back to AC for distribution, and finally rectifies back to low-voltage DC at server power supplies. The Galaxy Campus collapses this chain. A 10 kV AC feed feeds directly into solid-state transformation hardware, providing an 800V DC bus that feeds rack-level power shelves. Co-located battery energy storage (BESS) provides millisecond-level transient smoothing across variable wind and solar supplies.
