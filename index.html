    AB-Attention Slashes Video DiT Compute by Up to 92%: Training-Free Kernel Accelerates Wan2.1 and HunyuanVideo Over 2x

    1. Executive Summary

    Generative video diffusion transformers (DiTs) such as Wan2.1 and HunyuanVideo face catastrophic compute bottlenecks because evaluating full self-attention across hundreds of thousands of spatio-temporal visual tokens scales quadratically, saturating GPU high-bandwidth memory (HBM). To eliminate this processing wall without compromising visual output, researchers introduced AB-Attention (Accurate Block Attention), a training-free sparse kernel driven by an Intra-Block Clustering Selector (IBCS) that dynamically clusters and routes only high-value visual tokens. By skipping between 89% and 92% of standard attention mathematics, the kernel delivers an immediate 1.84x to 2.23x end-to-end generation speedup on single datacenter accelerators while boosting visual fidelity by 1.6 dB to 4.4 dB PSNR over prior sparse baselines without requiring fine-tuning or offline weight restructuring.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    Standard video DiT pipelines enforce dense dot-product attention across every temporal frame and spatial patch, driving computational complexity to prohibitive levels during multi-step sampling. AB-Attention bypasses this redundant overhead by restricting query clustering within localized token blocks, thereby preventing high-dimensional memory thrashing and maintaining critical 3D temporal trajectories.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

    STMicroelectronics Details 800V DC Digital Controllers: Mitigating Transient Droop and Busbar Limits in 130 kW+ Racks

    1. Executive Summary

    Legacy 48V direct-current power architectures are breaking down inside high-density AI data centers, where 130 kW server cabinets require over 2,700 amperes of current and occupy up to 30% of rack space with massive copper busbars. STMicroelectronics addressed this physical delivery wall by introducing a family of 800V DC digital power controllers tailored for wide-bandgap Silicon Carbide (SiC) and Gallium Nitride (GaN) switching stages. By stepping rack busbars up to 800V, the design slashes full-load current by 94% down to 162.5 amperes, mitigating resistive distribution losses by 4 to 5 percentage points while pairing multi-megahertz algorithmic regulation with sub-microsecond hardware arc protection and integrated Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC).

    Key Architectural Concepts
    • 800V HVDC Distribution: Stepping intra-rack power distribution from 48V to 800V direct current to reduce conductor mass, minimize I²R thermal waste, and maximize compute sled volume.
    • Wide-Bandgap Switching: Power conversion topologies driven by SiC and GaN semiconductors capable of switching at multi-MHz frequencies to minimize transformer footprint.
    • Transient Droop (di/dt): Severe instantaneous voltage dips caused by sudden GPU compute spikes, requiring algorithmic digital compensation combined with on-board decoupling networks.
    • Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC): On-chip cryptographic roots-of-trust executing quantum-resistant algorithms to safeguard digital power stage firmware from network intrusion.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    Operating high-voltage busbars at 800V DC resolves thermal throttling in intra-rack distribution but introduces severe high voltage slew rates (dv/dt) that can cause catastrophic latch-up. STMicroelectronics solves this by decoupling high-speed algorithmic control loops from autonomous sub-microsecond hardware fault cutoffs.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

    Transitioning server infrastructure to 800V DC presents severe electromechanical obstacles during live maintenance. Unlike 48V systems, hot-swapping 800V compute sleds generates extreme electrical arc-flash hazards requiring expensive optical disconnect interlocks and solid-state circuit breakers. Furthermore, because transient load surges (high di/dt) from dynamic AI model execution can trigger voltage droop before digital feedback loops respond, power systems engineers must balance dense high-voltage power conversion against significant on-board decoupling capacitor volumes.
