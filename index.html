    Interconnect Silicon: MACOM Demonstrates 448 Gbps/Lane 3.2T Optical and 224 Gbps 1.6T ACC Silicon

    1. Executive Summary

    Severe high-frequency channel insertion loss and inter-symbol interference at multi-hundred-gigabit signaling rates represent the primary physical bottleneck constraining scale-up accelerator topologies. Ahead of ECOC 2026 in Málaga, MACOM Technology Solutions demonstrated working physical-layer silicon for AI fabrics, introducing 3.2 Tbps optical transceiver ICs operating at 448 Gbps per lane (224 GBaud PAM4) yielding a 3.584 Tbps raw line rate across 8 lanes. The portfolio incorporates 1.6 Tbps Active Copper Cable (ACC) equalizers operating at 224 Gbps per lane that extend in-rack copper reaches to 2.5–3.0 meters at <20% of DSP power consumption, alongside silicon supporting PCIe 6.0 optical and PCIe 7.0 copper fabrics.

    Fundamental Physical Layer Terminology:

    PAM4 (Pulse Amplitude Modulation 4-Level): Modulation encoding 2 bits per symbol across four discrete voltage levels, halving the required channel Nyquist bandwidth relative to NRZ at identical data rates.

    Nyquist Frequency: The minimum theoretical bandwidth equal to half the baud rate required to transmit baseband symbols without inter-symbol interference (ISI).

    Active Copper Cable (ACC): High-speed electrical cabling integrating analog linear equalizers into the plug backshells to compensate for copper dielectric loss without retiming DSPs.

    Transimpedance Amplifier (TIA): High-bandwidth, low-noise receiver analog front-end silicon converting photodetector current pulses into differential voltage swings.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    Scaling signaling rates to 224 GBaud pushes analog silicon to fundamental semiconductor boundaries. For 448 Gbps PAM4, the fundamental Nyquist frequency reaches 112 GHz, shrinking the symbol Unit Interval (UI) to a mere 4.46 picoseconds. At these extreme frequencies, conventional PCB trace loss exceeds acceptable power budgets, requiring active analog equalizers embedded directly into cable connectors.
