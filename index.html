    Samsung and SK Hynix Outline Diverging Paths for HBM Logic and Advanced 3D Packaging

    1. Abstract

    Hot Chips 2026 program disclosures published August 11, 2026 describe two post-HBM scaling paths: Samsung’s use of advanced foundry logic in the HBM Base Die, enabling customized on-die functions beneath stacked DRAM, and SK Hynix’s advanced 3D packaging roadmap centered on hybrid bonding for dense 12-layer and 16-layer stacks. Both approaches seek more bandwidth per accelerator socket while confronting thermal, power-delivery, and signal-integrity constraints.

    HBM Base Die — The foundational silicon layer beneath vertically stacked DRAM dies that routes signals to the host processor.

    Hybrid Bonding — Direct copper-to-copper dielectric bonding that avoids microbumps and enables tighter vertical interconnect pitch.

    SI — Signal integrity: preservation of electrical signal quality across high-speed interconnects.

    PDN — Power distribution network: power and ground routing designed to control voltage drops.

    Samsung: advanced logic-process integration in the HBM base die for customized logic, built-in test circuits, and dynamic power management. SK Hynix: advanced 3D packaging and direct Cu-to-Cu hybrid bonding to reduce thermal resistance, shrink vertical pitch, and increase bit density through high-layer-count stacking.

    The disclosed roadmap spans HBM3e’s 2024–2025 baseline of standard logic base dies, microbumps, and advanced MR-MUF packaging; HBM4 and next-generation designs for 2026–2027 using advanced foundry logic base dies or hybrid direct Cu-to-Cu bonding; and a 2027+ logic-in-memory direction involving integrated near-memory compute logic and 3D heterogeneous chiplet stacks. The associated bottlenecks evolve from interposer SI/PI and microbump pitch limits to thermal dissipation, 16-high die stacking, power density, and substrate warping.

    Board and package implications. Logic-enabled base dies and hybrid bonding can improve timing margins and lower parasitic capacitance around high-speed SerDes interfaces, but they tighten local thermal co-design and transient IR drop management across dense micro-bump arrays. The work is scheduled for Hot Chips 2026 at Stanford University, August 23–25, within 2026–2027 manufacturing pathfinding.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

    HBM is moving from a commodity memory component toward a semi-custom logic-and-packaging substrate coupled to accelerator design, strengthening demand for hybrid-bonding equipment and 3D-IC signal-integrity and thermal EDA tools. Samsung’s advanced-node base die raises wafer cost and defect sensitivity; SK Hynix’s hybrid bonding requires stringent particle control to prevent bonding voids. Most critically, localized hotspots in 16-high stacks could trigger thermal throttling and erode the bandwidth gains these roadmaps are intended to unlock.
