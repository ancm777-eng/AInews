    Broadcom Structures $50B+ Private Debt Facility to Underwrite OpenAI Nexus Custom Silicon

This industrial leasing structure directly finances the deployment of up to 10 GW of dedicated compute capacity across 2026–2029, encompassing the first-generation Jalapeño and second-generation Serrano ASICs under the Nexus program.     GlobalFoundries and TSMC Ink $2B CoWoS Interposer Supply Agreement in New York

    1. Executive Summary

    Advanced silicon interposers remain a critical hardware bottleneck in frontier accelerator manufacturing, with fabrication historically concentrated in Taiwanese cleanrooms. To resolve this geographical concentration, GlobalFoundries and TSMC have signed a 5-year, $2 billion manufacturing pact to produce silicon interposers for TSMC's CoWoS platform at GlobalFoundries Fab 8 in Malta, New York. The partnership integrates passive Deep Trench Capacitors (DTC) directly into interposer silicon, suppressing transient voltage droop caused by rapid compute spikes (high dI/dt) in accelerators operating above 1,000 W. Slated for volume production in H1 2028, the initiative creates high-density passive decoupling infrastructure within the United States.

    Key Concepts:

    • CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate): 2.5D packaging integration where logic chiplets and HBM are interconnected via high-density through-silicon via (TSV) interposers.
    • Deep Trench Capacitor (DTC): Microscopic, high-aspect-ratio capacitive wells etched inside silicon substrates to maximize passive charge density.
    • dI/dt Transient Voltage Droop: Rapid supply voltage drops resulting from sudden, multi-hundred-ampere switching surges in high-power accelerator execution blocks.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    Integrating DTC structures directly below active compute silicon minimizes parasitic loop inductance in the power delivery network, stabilizing core voltages during high-frequency execution spikes.

    3. Strategic Outlook & Limitations

    Producing interposer wafers in New York improves wafer supply chain resilience, but it does not establish a complete domestic packaging pipeline. Finished interposer substrates must still ship to offshore facilities for micro-bump bonding, wafer thinning, testing, and organic carrier placement until complementary domestic packaging factories become operational. Furthermore, deep trench capacitors address only high-frequency voltage fluctuations within the package; low-frequency voltage droop across server motherboards and power delivery buses must still be managed by board-level voltage regulator modules.
