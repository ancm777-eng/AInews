    Advanced Semiconductor Packaging Crunch: TSMC Evaluates 20% CoWoS Price Surge as Reticle Sizes Reach 5.5x

    1. Executive Summary

    While industry attention has focused primarily on front-end lithography nodes, backend advanced packaging and test capacity have emerged as the binding physical bottleneck limiting AI accelerator shipments. Without Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate (CoWoS) packaging, massive multi-die compute processors cannot communicate with High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM). Supply chain disclosures confirm that TSMC is evaluating a 10% to 20% price adjustment on advanced CoWoS packaging for 2027 allocations, driven by next-generation platforms scaling to 5.5x the lithographic reticle limit and automated testing durations multiplying by up to 7.0x across assembly lines.

    CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate): TSMC's 2.5D wafer-level integration architecture that bonds logic chiplets and HBM onto a silicon interposer with micro-bumps.

    Reticle Limit: The maximum optical field exposure area (~858 mm²) achievable by a standard lithography scanner, requiring stitched interposers for larger packages.

    ATE (Automated Test Equipment): Specialized high-throughput hardware verifying pin continuity, high-speed interfaces, and thermal-stress tolerances.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    The transition from single-die processors to modular multi-chiplet topologies causes an exponential increase in automated testing duration. While a single-die Hopper-class module serves as the 1.0x testing baseline, testing dual-die Blackwell modules requires 4.0x the time, and Rubin-class architectures require up to 7.0x due to high-pin-count base die verification and extended thermal cycles.
