    Sub-0.8V Core Power Integrity: Vertical Power Delivery and SIVR via Infineon-C2i Integration

    1. Abstract

    Infineon Technologies AG expanded its advanced datacenter power roadmap on August 28, 2026, announcing the acquisition of Bengaluru-based C2i Semiconductors to industrialize grid-to-core Vertical Power Delivery (VPD) and Substrate Integrated Voltage Regulators (SIVR). As next-generation AI accelerators eclipse 1,000 watts per socket and demand sub-0.8V rails carrying transient bursts upwards of 1,000 amperes, conventional lateral power distribution networks suffer acute I²R losses, substrate copper congestion, and severe dynamic voltage droop. Integrating C2i’s software-defined multiphase controllers with Infineon's power switches collapses the regulator-to-die electrical path from over 30 millimeters to less than 1 millimeter, eliminating lateral motherboard transmission bottlenecks and freeing perimeter board estate for high-speed UCIe and HBM4 interfaces.

    Core Architectural Concepts:

    • Vertical Power Delivery (VPD): A physical packaging topology mounting power stages directly beneath compute dies to slash transmission path distance.
    • Substrate Integrated Voltage Regulators (SIVR): Micro-miniaturized power conversion circuits embedded directly into package substrate layers.
    • Dynamic Voltage Droop (di/dt): Sudden transient voltage drops triggered by massive accelerator current spikes, risking logic timing faults.

    2. Architecture & Performance

    Lateral voltage regulator module (VRM) architectures require server motherboards to allocate up to 10 thick copper plane layers exclusively to manage high current delivery. Vertical integration routes 12V or 48V distribution rails directly through backside substrate micro-vias, executing step-down conversion millimeters away from the active execution units.
