### 1. NVIDIA Unveils DSX MaxLPS Site Power Framework for Vera Rubin NVL72 AI Factories at AI Infra Summit 2026 (Category 6)

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? Physical utility power interconnects and electrical substation lead times have replaced silicon wafer supply as the primary physical bottleneck governing artificial intelligence datacenter expansion. Next-generation AI accelerators operate at thermal design power thresholds exceeding 1,000W to 1,500W per socket, yet production clusters executing complex AI pipelines rarely consume peak nameplate power across every compute node at the same instant. Conventional datacenter engineering provisions electrical distribution networks according to worst-case nameplate peak ratings, leaving substantial margins of expensive substation capacity stranded and unutilized during dynamic workload phase shifts. If datacenter power distribution and system software cannot dynamically reclaim and reallocate stranded electrical headroom across compute trays in real time, cluster operators are physically prohibited from deploying additional accelerator density within existing utility power contracts.

On September 15, 2026, at the AI Infra Summit in Santa Clara, California, NVIDIA Vice President of Hyperscale and High-Performance Computing Ian Buck delivered an infrastructure keynote detailing **DSX MaxLPS (Maximum Land Power Shell)**, a site-level power and operating framework designed for next-generation **Vera Rubin NVL72** AI factories. MaxLPS optimizes the three fundamental site constraints—land, utility power, and the physical datacenter shell—by coupling facility-level power telemetry with NVIDIA Dynamic Power Software (DPS) to continuously monitor power draw and dynamically shift headroom from idle or communication-bound nodes to compute-bound accelerators. Buck presented commercial production results from GPU cloud provider Lambda, where DSX MaxLPS deployed on a five-rack, 19-node HGX B200 cluster enabled 19 nodes to operate within the electrical budget originally allocated for 16 nodes, achieving a 24% cluster-wide token throughput increase and a 23% improvement in performance per watt. Under specific workload concurrency conditions, NVIDIA projects that DSX MaxLPS can enable up to 40% more GPU capacity for Vera Rubin NVL72 systems within the same megawatt power envelope. NVIDIA also disclosed key co-development initiatives, including collaboration with Amazon Annapurna Labs on NVHBM custom memory interfaces, d-Matrix integrating Vera CPUs with Raptor XPUs via NVLink Fusion, and flexible-load grid response integration with Silicon Valley Power and Emerald AI.

System-level electrical, thermal, and packaging co-design implications:
1. Dynamic Power Shifting and Transient Suppression: The DSX MaxLPS control loop tracks instantaneous electrical draw across server power distribution units and GPU voltage regulator modules, redistributing stranded capacity and damping transient step-load surges (high di/dt) before they stress facility power distribution networks.
2. High-Density Power Delivery: The Vera Rubin NVL72 integrates 72 Rubin GPUs and 36 Vera CPUs in a monolithic liquid-cooled rack drawing over 130 kW. Distributing this current requires transitioning from legacy 48V to 54V vertical busbars and high-voltage DC distribution (stepping toward 800V DC facility distribution) to minimize motherboard resistive losses (I squared R losses) and free up board routing real estate for high-speed differential pairs.
3. Warm-Water Thermal Efficiency: The reference platform utilizes 45 degrees Celsius warm-water direct liquid cooling, minimizing chiller overhead, improving facility Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE), and converting conserved cooling energy directly into available compute headroom within the fixed site power budget.

### 2. NVIDIA Surges Mixture-of-Experts Training 10.4x on GB200 via JAX and Transformer Engine Ragged-Tensor Kernels

**Source:** NVIDIA Developer Blog / Quantum Zeitgeist / Daily.dev  
**Event Date:** 2026-09-15  
**Source Date:** 2026-09-15, 08:00 PDT (2026-09-16, 00:00 KST)  
**Status:** Official Technical Engineering Publication & Open-Source Kernel Release  
**Primary URL:** https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/accelerating-dropless-moe-training-jax-transformer-engine/

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures—utilized by leading frontier models such as DeepSeek-V3, Qwen, and Mixtral—have emerged as the prevailing design for scaling model capacity while controlling inference and training computational costs. Instead of routing every token through a uniform dense feed-forward network, MoE dynamically routes tokens to a subset of specialized expert networks via a learned gating router. However, scaling MoE pre-training across distributed GPU clusters introduces an acute systems bottleneck: dynamic router decisions distribute tokens unevenly across experts, creating non-uniform "ragged tensors". Conventional GEMM routines require rectangular, uniform matrices, forcing training pipelines to either pad tensors with dummy zeros (wasting memory and compute) or drop excess tokens (degrading model convergence and reasoning quality). Furthermore, all-to-all communication required to route tokens across networked GPUs creates heavy interconnect traffic, causing expensive tensor cores to sit idle while awaiting communication barriers.

On September 15, 2026, NVIDIA published technical benchmark results detailing how integrating the JAX Python library with targeted NVIDIA Transformer Engine kernel optimizations achieves a **10.4x compute throughput speedup** during dropless MoE training on **NVIDIA GB200 NVL72** systems. Benchmarked on the 671-billion-parameter **DeepSeek-V3** architecture, NVIDIA documented that an unoptimized baseline achieved only **103 TFLOPS/GPU**, with inter-GPU communication consuming **84% of accumulated kernel execution time**. By applying custom low-level kernels tailored to ragged data structures, compute throughput reached **1,068 TFLOPS/GPU**, while maintaining a **97% scaling efficiency across 1,024 GPUs**. The dropless optimization stack achieves this performance without token dropping or padding by implementing: (1) single-call grouped GEMM kernels via cuBLAS and cuBLASLt that process variable expert token counts within unified GPU kernel executions; (2) MXFP8 group-aware quantization that compresses activations without reported degradation in reasoning fidelity; (3) fused NCCL Expert Parallelism (NCCL EP) primitives that merge dispatch and combine phases while deduplicating tokens sent to identical destination nodes; and (4) JAX host offloading paired with XLA multistreaming collectives to overlap inter-node all-to-all data transfers behind matrix multiplication.

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem:** Validates JAX and Transformer Engine as an enterprise-grade framework for dropless MoE training, demonstrating that low-level kernel co-design can eliminate severe communication bottlenecks without altering model mathematics.
- **Business & Market:** Drastically improves the computational return on invested capital for organizations training massive MoE models on GB200 infrastructure, accelerating training completion schedules.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware:** Maximizes arithmetic utilization across NVLink scale-up domains and InfiniBand fabrics by overlapping collective communications directly behind tensor core execution.
- **Investment Implication:** Overweight specialized compiler runtimes, communication-hiding middleware, and micro-precision quantization libraries; highlights the enduring structural advantage of NVIDIA's full-stack software optimization ecosystem.

Key specifications:

- Target Model Architecture: DeepSeek-V3 (671-Billion-Parameter Mixture-of-Experts)
- Target Accelerator Platform: NVIDIA GB200 NVL72 (Blackwell Architecture)
- Software Framework Stack: JAX coupled with NVIDIA Transformer Engine and NGC MaxText container
- Baseline Compute Throughput: 103 TFLOPS/GPU (84% of accumulated kernel execution time consumed by inter-GPU collectives)
- Optimized Compute Throughput: 1,068 TFLOPS/GPU (10.4x speedup in per-GPU arithmetic throughput)
- Multi-GPU Scaling Efficiency: 97% scaling efficiency sustained across 1,024 networked GPUs
- Kernel Primitives: Grouped GEMM (cuBLAS/cuBLASLt), MXFP8 Group-Aware Quantization, Fused NCCL Expert Parallelism (fused dispatch/combine with token deduplication), XLA multistream collectives

While achieving 1,068 TFLOPS/GPU is an exceptional systems milestone, these benchmark results are vendor-reported and rely on specialized low-level kernels tailored specifically to Blackwell tensor core instructions and NVLink scale-up fabric. Replicating these speedups on heterogeneous accelerators or non-NVIDIA networking fabrics remains technically demanding. Furthermore, running dropless MoE with MXFP8 quantization requires careful monitoring of router numerical stability, as dynamic routing shifts during early training epochs can produce activation spikes that risk numerical underflow or overflow in 8-bit registers.
