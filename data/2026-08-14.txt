### 3. NVIDIA, Microsoft, and Google Unify Behind OCP 800V DC Power Standard for Megawatt AI Racks

**Source:** Network World / Wccftech / Open Compute Project (OCP) / Converge Digest  
**Event Date:** 2026-08-13 | **Publication Date:** 2026-08-13  
**Status:** Multi-Enterprise Standard Adoption & Specifications Release  
**Primary URL:** https://www.networkworld.com/article/google-microsoft-nvidia-800v-dc-standard-ai-data-centers/  

#### [Overview]
On August 13, 2026, NVIDIA, Microsoft, and Google—in formal collaboration with the Open Compute Project (OCP) and over 80 participating equipment and infrastructure vendors—announced the standardization of an **800-Volt Direct Current (800V DC / LVDC)** power architecture for next-generation AI datacenter facilities. As accelerated compute clusters scale from 100 kW per rack toward multi-megawatt rows to support dense GPU architectures (such as NVIDIA Blackwell and Vera Rubin systems), conventional 480V alternating current (AC) distribution reaches physical and thermal constraints. Under the standardized OCP 800V DC framework, facility-level Solid-State Transformers (SST) or centralized rectifier skids convert medium-voltage grid AC directly to an 800V DC distribution busway, eliminating multiple intermediary conversion stages. By delivering power at higher voltage and correspondingly lower current, the architecture achieves a 50% to 80% reduction in copper wiring mass, reduces annual facility energy-related operating expenses by 8% to 12% via lowered distribution and conversion losses, and yields an estimated $4 million to $8 million in CapEx savings per 10 MW deployment. NVIDIA confirmed that 800V DC-compatible MGX power racks will begin shipping in the second half of 2026, followed by row-level power centers supporting up to 2 Megawatts (2MW) per rack row in 2027.

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem Perspective:** Establishes an open hyperscale electrical standard through OCP, aligning competing cloud operators (Google, Microsoft) and chipmakers (NVIDIA) on unified power interfaces, conversion topologies, and safety protocols.
- **Business & Market Dynamics:** Accelerates commercial demand for wide-bandgap power semiconductor fabricators (Silicon Carbide / Gallium Nitride) and high-voltage busway manufacturers while marginalizing non-compliant legacy power distribution vendors.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware Implications:** Solves the physical conductor and heat dissipation bottleneck at the rack row, allowing compute densities to scale to 2MW per row to support ultra-dense AI superclusters.
- **Investment Strategy:** Overweight power semiconductor manufacturers (SiC/GaN), high-voltage DC busbar and switchgear providers, and solid-state transformer manufacturers.

#### [Technical Deep Dive]

- **Core Technical Specifications (SW/HW):** [Distribution Voltage]: 800 Volts Direct Current (800V DC / LVDC) | [Consortium Partners]: NVIDIA, Microsoft, Google, and 80+ OCP Hardware Vendors | [Per-Row Power Capacity]: Up to 2 Megawatts (2MW) per rack row | [Copper Conductor Reduction]: 50% to 80% decrease in copper weight | [Energy OpEx Reduction]: 8% to 12% lower annual energy-related OpEx via reduced conversion/distribution loss | [Facility CapEx Benefit]: $4M to $8M savings per 10 MW build | [Rollout Schedule]: MGX 800V DC power racks shipping H2 2026; 2MW Row Power Centers and busbars in 2027; Facility DC Power Blocks long-term | **Source:** OCP / Network World / Wccftech (2026-08-13)
- **Comparative Analysis:** Legacy 480V AC architectures route power through four to five distinct conversion steps (Grid MV AC to 480V AC, to 400V DC UPS, to 48V DC, to sub-1V core logic), generating compounding thermal and resistance losses. Direct 800V DC conversion consolidates upstream conversion into a single medium-voltage stage, reducing line losses and enabling smaller conductor cross-sections.
- **Release / Deployment Stage:** OCP technical alignment published; first MGX-compatible 800V DC power racks shipping in H2 2026; 2MW row deployments slated for 2027.

[Layer Structure Data]
- Layer Order (Top to Bottom) | Component / Layer Name | Architectural Role & Technical Specification
- Layer 1 (Grid Interconnect) | Medium-Voltage Grid Substation | Utility interconnect delivering 10kV–34.5kV AC directly to the AI facility perimeter
- Layer 2 (Facility Rectification) | Centralized DC Power Block / SST | High-density Solid-State Transformers converting medium-voltage AC directly to 800V DC
- Layer 3 (Row Distribution) | Overhead 800V DC Busway & Row Power Center | High-voltage overhead busbars delivering up to 2MW per rack row with reduced copper mass
- Layer 4 (Rack Step-Down) | In-Rack 800V-to-48V Intermediate DC/DC Converters | High-efficiency step-down conversion stages feeding accelerator compute trays
- Layer 5 (Core Accelerator PDN) | Point-of-Load (PoL) Voltage Regulator Modules | Low-voltage multi-phase VRMs delivering sub-1V power directly to GPU/ASIC silicon cores

#### [Critical View]
While 800V DC mitigates conductor density and energy losses, it introduces distinct engineering challenges around high-voltage DC safety. Direct current at 800V does not cross zero voltage naturally, requiring specialized solid-state circuit breakers, advanced arc-flash mitigation, and re-trained operational personnel. Furthermore, existing 480V AC datacenters face substantial capital expenditure to retrofit to 800V DC, creating a dual-tier datacenter landscape.

### 4. Anthropic Pursues $6 Billion Acquisition of AI Efficiency Startup Decart

**Source:** Bloomberg / Reuters / Ctech / PYMNTS / Globes / Investing.com  
**Event Date:** 2026-08-13 | **Publication Date:** 2026-08-13  
**Status:** Corporate M&A Negotiations Disclosed  
**Primary URL:** https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/anthropic-in-talks-to-acquire-ai-startup-decart-for-6-billion  

#### [Overview]
On August 12–13, 2026, Bloomberg and Reuters reported that AI safety and frontier foundation model developer Anthropic is in active negotiations to acquire Israeli AI startup Decart for approximately $6 billion. Founded in 2023 by Dr. Dean Leitersdorf, his brother Orian Leitersdorf, and Moshe Shalev, Decart has gained recognition for developing software and kernel-level compilation tools that significantly enhance chip compute efficiency during model training and inference. Decart's core technology portfolio includes the "Decart Optimization Stack" (DOS)—which optimizes GPU and accelerator memory scheduling and attention paging—alongside real-time interactive world models such as "Oasis" and "Lucy". Decart previously raised $300 million in Series B funding in May 2026 at a valuation of nearly $4 billion, backed by Radical Ventures, NVIDIA, Adobe Ventures, Sequoia Capital, and Benchmark. If finalized, the transaction would represent Anthropic's largest acquisition to date, with Decart's ~100-person engineering team slated to integrate into Anthropic's inference and performance organization, establishing Anthropic's first dedicated R&D hub in Tel Aviv. The discussions occur as Anthropic expands its compute infrastructure across Google TPU clusters, AWS Trainium custom ASICs, and NVIDIA GPU clusters to improve Claude's gross margins ahead of a prospective initial public offering (IPO).

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem Perspective:** Accelerates the vertical integration of custom compilation and kernel optimization into frontier model stacks, allowing foundation model providers to squeeze maximum inference throughput out of underlying compute clusters.
- **Business & Market Dynamics:** Positions Anthropic to improve unit economics and gross margins for Claude API traffic, mitigating margin erosion as token prices face downward pressure from competing open and commercial models.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware Implications:** Custom memory scheduling and kernel optimization reduce hardware occupancy per inference request across multi-cloud GPU and custom ASIC deployments.
- **Investment Strategy:** Overweight AI compiler toolchains, low-level inference runtime developers, and silicon efficiency startups.
