### 4. Texas A&M, KIMM, and UST Model Kerr Self-Focusing Control for Precise 4H-SiC Laser Slicing in Advanced Power Packaging

**Source:** Semiconductor Engineering / arXiv (2608.03814) / Texas A&M / KIMM / UST  
**Event Date:** 2026-08-04 | **Publication Date:** 2026-08-10  
**Status:** Technical Preprint / Research Disclosure (arXiv:2608.03814)  
**Primary URL:** https://semiengineering.com/femtosecond-laser-process-targets-depth-controlled-4h-sic-wafer-slicing/  

#### [Overview]
*Why this research is important:* As power electronics and high-voltage power delivery networks (PDN) move toward 3D vertical stacking, wafer thinning is critical to lower substrate thermal resistance and parasitic interconnect inductance. However, 4H-Silicon Carbide (4H-SiC—a third-generation wide-bandgap semiconductor prized for its high breakdown electric field and thermal conductivity) is extremely hard and brittle. Conventional mechanical diamond sawing causes high kerf loss, micro-cracks, and high wafer breakage rates. While non-contact stealth laser slicing forms internal modified layers to facilitate cleavage, non-linear optical effects like *Kerr self-focusing* (where high-intensity laser light alters the material's refractive index, causing uncontrolled beam focusing inside the crystal) distort the precise depth of modification.

In an arXiv research preprint (**2608.03814**, posted August 2026), researchers from Texas A&M University, the Korea Institute of Machinery and Materials (KIMM), and the University of Science and Technology (UST) developed an extended *Marburger formula* model that incorporates focused ray optics to predict Kerr self-focusing thresholds in 4H-SiC. The team established a processability map linking laser pulse energy and focal depth to internal modification zones. In laboratory-scale experimental trials, this physics framework achieved depth-controlled internal modification at sub-100 μm targets, offering a predictive methodology to reduce separation stress during non-contact wafer slicing.

*Impact on board and package layout designers:* For advanced packaging and power delivery network (PDN) designers, mastering non-contact sub-100 μm 4H-SiC wafer slicing provides a potential fabrication pathway for ultra-compact vertical PMICs and high-density voltage regulators. Substrate thinning lowers thermal resistance and parasitic inductance, potentially enabling future AI server power stages to operate at elevated switching frequencies with lower heat dissipation.

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem Perspective:** Provides a theoretical and experimental optical foundation for non-contact wide-bandgap substrate slicing, addressing material processing bottlenecks in 3D power semiconductor packaging.
- **Business & Market Dynamics:** Potential long-term reduction in material loss and yield loss for 4H-SiC power chips, benefiting suppliers targeting high-density power modules.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware Implications:** Supports higher power density per rack in AI data centers by providing a processing pathway toward vertically stacked SiC power conversion dies.
- **Investment Strategy:** Overweight advanced femtosecond laser equipment makers, wide-bandgap SiC power semiconductor vendors, and 3D heterogeneous packaging specialists.

#### [Technical Deep Dive]

- **Core Technical Specifications (SW/HW):** [Research Institutions]: Texas A&M University, KIMM, UST | [Material]: 4H-Silicon Carbide (4H-SiC) Wafers | [Laser Type]: Femtosecond Laser (Infrared Wavelength) | [Physics Framework]: Extended Marburger formula incorporating normalized irradiance & focused ray optics | [Validation Level]: Laboratory-scale experimental verification of internal modification depth control | **Source:** Semiconductor Engineering / arXiv (2608.03814)
- **Comparative Analysis:** Mechanical diamond sawing causes severe kerf loss and micro-cracks on 4H-SiC wafers. Stealth femtosecond laser slicing guided by Kerr self-focusing modeling achieves depth-precise internal modification with lower kerf loss and minimal mechanical separation stress.
- **Release / Deployment Stage:** [Research preprint published; laboratory-scale processability map validated]

#### [Critical View]
While the Kerr self-focusing model accurately predicts modification depth under controlled laboratory conditions, commercial 4H-SiC wafers exhibit subtle variations in dopant concentration, crystal defects, and surface roughness. In high-throughput industrial manufacturing, these material variations could alter optical non-linearity, requiring real-time adaptive pulse calibration to maintain depth precision.
