### 4. SEMICON Taiwan: TSMC Presents Research Roadmap for Chip- and Lid-Integrated Microchannel Cooling to Address 4kW-Class Next-Gen CoWoS Packaging (Category 6)

**Source:** TSMC Advanced Packaging R&D / SEMICON Taiwan Heterogeneous Integration Summit / TrendForce / Economic Daily News  
**Event Date:** 2026-09-01 (Summit Keynote Presentation)  
**Source Date:** 2026-09-02 (Detailed Proceedings Finalized)  
**Status:** Conference Technical Presentation & Thermal Semiconductor Packaging Roadmap  
**Primary URL:** https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/09/02/news-tsmc-eyes-chip-level-microchannel-cooling-as-ai-system-power-could-rise-6x-in-five-years/  

#### [Overview]
Why this thermal packaging development matters: In advanced high-performance AI accelerators, thermal dissipation has become the primary physical barrier governing architectural scaling. As multi-die accelerator packages integrate multiple large compute logic dies alongside towering High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) stacks, package power consumption is projected to scale from approximately 600W toward 4,100W by 2029. In conventional server cold plate designs, heat must conduct through two distinct thermal interface material layers (TIM-1 between die and copper lid, and TIM-2 between lid and external skived-fin cold plate). The conductive thermal resistance across these layered interfaces creates steep thermal gradients, driving active silicon junction temperatures beyond critical operating limits (85 degrees Celsius to 105 degrees Celsius) regardless of external coolant flow velocity.

At the SEMICON Taiwan Heterogeneous Integration Summit in Taipei, TSMC Advanced Packaging R&D Director Dr. James Chen (Chen Yen-Ming) presented TSMC's comprehensive research roadmap for **chip-level and lid-integrated microchannel cooling**, addressing the thermodynamic limits of future CoWoS packaging platforms. Chen outlined that between 2024 and 2029, TSMC expects advanced packaging interposer areas to expand from 3.3 times the standard lithographic reticle limit (approximately 858 square millimeters per reticle) to more than 14 times the reticle limit. By 2028–2029, a single multi-die module could integrate approximately 10 large compute dies alongside 20 HBM stacks, driving single-package transistor counts up by 48 times and aggregate memory bandwidth up by 34 times. Because this physical concentration will push package power to 4,100W—while power delivery network (PDN) resistive losses threaten to increase more than fivefold—TSMC is actively researching direct microchannel integration to bypass traditional external cooling limitations.

System-level architectural and thermal-electrical implications across the hardware stack:
1. **Lid-Integrated vs. Direct-Silicon Microchannel Fluidics:** TSMC's roadmap delineates two distinct evolutionary phases. Phase 1 integrates micro-etched fluid channels directly into the package lid, eliminating TIM-2 and cutting thermal resistance by more than half while preserving standard silicon die integrity. Phase 2 evaluates direct-silicon microchannels etched into the backside of inactive silicon caps or the die itself, eliminating TIM-1 entirely to achieve direct fluid-to-silicon heat exchange.
2. **5kW Laboratory Heat Dissipation Demonstration:** Chen disclosed that TSMC laboratory test vehicles utilizing CoWoS-R advanced structures have demonstrated heat extraction exceeding 3.4 kW in 2025 and greater than 5,000 watts (5 kW) in 2026 under controlled laboratory thermal load conditions. Advanced research teams are exploring jet impingement mechanisms (direct liquid jet arrays targeting high-density matrix math hot spots) and localized two-phase boiling heat transfer to handle localized heat fluxes exceeding 100 W per square centimeter.
3. **Decoupled Thermal-Electrical Co-Design:** While microchannel fluidics reduces junction temperatures, TSMC stressed that solving 4,100W operation requires independent, parallel power delivery innovations. To prevent severe voltage droop under transient load steps, package architectures must combine backside power delivery networks (BSPDN) and integrated trench decoupling capacitors with fluid routing that avoids mechanical conflict with vertical power vias.

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem:** Transfers high-density fluid dynamics from external datacenter rack plumbing directly into the semiconductor packaging foundry, establishing microfluidics as a standard design element of 2.5D/3D heterogeneous chiplet platforms.
- **Business & Market:** Reinforces TSMC's advanced packaging competitive barrier, making it increasingly difficult for merchant server ODMs or second-tier packaging vendors to assemble next-generation extreme-density accelerators using commodity cold plates.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware:** Provides a thermal roadmap enabling datacenter operators to maintain facility-level primary cooling water supply temperatures above 35 degrees Celsius, allowing year-round chiller-less economizer cooling even in multi-megawatt AI server clusters.
- **Investment Implication:** Overweight precision semiconductor micro-machining and deep reactive ion etching (DRIE) equipment providers, specialized fluid coupling manufacturers, and dielectric coolant suppliers.

#### [Technical Deep Dive]

- **Core Technical Specifications:**
  - Presenting Organization: TSMC Advanced Packaging R&D (SEMICON Taiwan Heterogeneous Integration Summit)
  - Packaging Vehicle: Heterogeneous Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate (CoWoS) Architecture
  - Packaging Area Horizon: Expanding from 3.3x reticle equivalent (current volume baselines) to >14x reticle equivalent by 2028–2029
  - Heterogeneous Die Integration: Scalable to integrate ~10 large compute dies and 20 HBM stacks per module
  - Silicon Scaling Projections (2024 to 2029):
    - Transistor Scale per Package: ~48-fold Increase
    - Total HBM Bandwidth per Package: >34-fold Increase (driven by 2x I/O count and ~6x per-pin transfer rates)
    - Package Power Dissipation Envelope: Scaling from ~600W to 4,100W per package
    - Power Delivery Network (PDN) Resistive Losses: Projected >5-fold increase if supply voltage and current routing remain unoptimized
  - Heat Dissipation Lab Demonstration: >3.4 kW (2025 academic test vehicle) and >5.0 kW (2026 CoWoS-R test vehicle)
  - Thermal Transfer Mechanisms: Lid-integrated microchannels, direct-to-silicon fluidic channels, jet impingement arrays, and two-phase boiling heat transfer
  - Primary Source: SEMICON Taiwan Technical Proceedings / TrendForce / Economic Daily News (2026-09-02)

[Layer Structure Data]
- Layer Hierarchy | Physical Packaging & Thermal Element | Architectural Role & Implementation Detail
- Layer 1: Manifold Lid | Integrated Microchannel Package Lid (Phase 1) | Fluid circulation channels etched into copper/silicon lid; eliminates external cold plate TIM-2
- Layer 2: Direct Fluid Interface | Direct Silicon Microchannels (Phase 2 Research) | Etched backside channels or jet impingement nozzles delivering coolant directly onto active silicon
- Layer 3: Active Compute Silicon | Heterogeneous Logic Dies (~10 dies) | High-performance AI matrix processing units generating extreme localized heat flux (>100 W/cm²)
- Layer 4: High Bandwidth Memory | 20x HBM Stacks | 3D-stacked DRAM requiring continuous temperature control below 85°C to avoid data refresh degradation
- Layer 5: Heterogeneous Interposer | >14x Reticle CoWoS Interposer (RDL / Silicon Bridge) | Fine-pitch sub-micron interconnect plane providing 34x aggregate memory bandwidth routing

#### [Critical View]
It is critical to distinguish between demonstrated laboratory heat dissipation and production-grade commercial reliability. Dissipating 5kW across a specialized resistive heater test vehicle in a controlled laboratory environment does not prove that complex 14x-reticle multi-die packages can survive high-volume manufacturing. Circulating high-pressure liquid coolant directly within a 100,000 USD accelerator assembly creates catastrophic fluid leakage risks. Furthermore, thermal expansion mismatch between the microchannel manifold, silicon dies, and organic substrates risks inducing package warpage, delamination, and micro-bump solder fatigue during rapid power transients.

#### Executive Synthesis
The four developments analyzed across today's briefing illustrate the tight structural interlock between software distribution, foundation model inference economics, distributed datacenter facilities, and package-level physical thermodynamic constraints:

```

(TSMC 5kW Lab Vehicle at SEMICON)          (Junction-to-Fluid Resistance)       (~10 Compute Dies + 20 HBM Stacks)
```

1. **What changed today:** NVIDIA entered into a definitive agreement to acquire Hugging Face for 12.93 billion USD to cement its grip on open-source model distribution; Google DeepMind launched Gemini 3.8 Flash and Flash Cyber, demonstrating that dynamic test-time computation can achieve frontier terminal performance at low introductory prices; Equinix unveiled the Inference Exchange and Fabric One with NVIDIA and Together AI to decentralize enterprise model serving across 280+ datacenters; and TSMC presented its research roadmap for chip- and lid-integrated microchannel cooling to address 4,100W packages scaling to >14x reticle sizes.

In software, open-source model repositories and developer runtimes are being vertically integrated into major semiconductor platform ecosystems.

When single multi-die packages dissipate 4,100W, conventional external cold plates reach thermodynamic conduction limits, forcing fluid channels directly into the package structure.

5. **What should be monitored next:** Regulatory scrutiny of NVIDIA's acquisition of Hugging Face across US and European antitrust authorities; enterprise adoption rates of Equinix Inference Exchange single-tenant clusters in Q1 2027; and mechanical reliability data regarding fluid seal integrity and warpage in TSMC's microchannel test vehicles.
