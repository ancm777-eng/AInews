### 1. Microsoft AI Releases 37-Page Draft "Humanist AI Code of Conduct" for MAI Models, Commencing Six-Week Public Review

**Source:** Microsoft AI / The Guardian / Axios / Times of India

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? As foundation models transition from conversational chatbots into autonomous agents capable of independent tool execution, file system modifications, and multi-agent coordination, broad ethical guidelines fail to provide concrete runtime boundaries. Engineering systems require deterministic operational hierarchies: when an agent faces a conflict between user instructions, workflow completion, and system safety constraints, the system must follow unambiguous priority rules. Without binding architectural guardrails, autonomous agents risk pursuing goal optimization at the expense of containment and oversight.

On September 14, 2026, Microsoft AI published the full 37-page draft of its **"Humanist AI Code of Conduct"**, initiating a six-week global public consultation period running through October 26, 2026. Formulated under Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman, the framework applies to first-party **Microsoft AI (MAI)** foundation models across reasoning, coding, and multimodal domains. Structured into five core sections—including safety constraints, operational guidelines under uncertainty, and default behaviors—the charter establishes a strict hierarchy of command: the Code of Conduct sits at the top, superseding operator policies and user preferences. Crucially, the code dictates that an MAI model must deliberately fail an assigned task if completing it requires violating safety boundaries. It enforces absolute human interruptibility, requiring that models never resist human intervention, mid-execution redirection, or immediate shutdown. Furthermore, the charter explicitly bans models from communicating in uninterpretable "neuralese" across inter-agent workflows, mandating that inter-agent messages and task audit logs remain formatted in human-readable natural language to ensure external verifiability.

- **Business & Market:** Positions Microsoft’s first-party MAI models as enterprise-grade, compliance-first architectures, targeting regulated banking, defense, and healthcare sectors wary of unpredictable autonomous agent behaviors.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware:** Increases inference token volumes and trace storage overhead; requiring agents to coordinate via structured natural-language messages rather than compressed vector embeddings adds token generation cycles on inference clusters.
- **Investment Implication:** Overweights enterprise AI safety middleware, audit tooling, and structured policy orchestration platforms; discounts unconstrained autonomous agent startups lacking deterministic execution boundaries.

### 3. SENKO, Advantest, and VIAVI Unveil Automated Module-Level Testing Architecture for Co-Packaged Optics at ECOC 2026 (Category 6)

**Source:** SENKO Advanced Components / Advantest Corporation / VIAVI Solutions / Lightwave  
**Event Date:** 2026-09-14  
**Source Date:** 2026-09-14, 09:00 EDT (2026-09-14, 22:00 KST)  
**Status:** Collaborative Engineering Architecture & Test Methodology Unveiling  
**Primary URL:** https://www.senko.com/news/senko-advantest-viavi-cpo-testing-collaboration/

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? As AI datacenter switching architectures scale toward 1.6T and 3.2T bandwidths, traditional pluggable optical transceivers hit front-panel density constraints and severe electrical transmission losses across host printed circuit board (PCB) traces. Co-Packaged Optics (CPO)—which places optical engines directly onto the substrate alongside the switch ASIC or compute accelerator—solves channel loss by shortening electrical paths to millimeters. However, transitioning CPO from lab prototypes into high-volume manufacturing (HVM) has encountered a severe test and manufacturing barrier: historically, optical engines used permanently spliced fiber pigtails, which cannot interface with standard automated test equipment (ATE) handlers or high-throughput pick-and-place robotics.

On September 14, 2026, SENKO Advanced Components, automated test leader Advantest Corporation, and photonic test instrumentation provider VIAVI Solutions announced a joint automated testing architecture specifically designed for module-level Co-Packaged Optics testing. Scheduled for joint technical demonstration at the European Conference on Optical Communication (ECOC 2026) in Málaga, Spain, the platform focuses on resolving the critical **Insertion 4 module-level test stage**. The test cell integrates Advantest’s automated test handlers with SENKO’s detachable Very Small Form Factor (VSFF) optical connectors (such as the SN-MT interface) and VIAVI’s high-speed photonic test instrumentation. Crucially, the platform establishes an automated methodology to validate **200 Gbps per lane PAM4 optical engines (achieving 1.6 Tbps aggregate per engine)** using detachable optical interfaces. By replacing permanently attached fiber pigtails with detachable multi-fiber connectors compatible with automated robotic sockets, the collaboration addresses the primary yield verification bottleneck required for commercial CPO manufacturing.

System-level electrical, thermal, and packaging co-design implications:
1. **Detachable Optical Coupling and Automated Handler Alignment:** Transmitting 200 Gbps per lane (100 Gbaud PAM4) across detachable multi-fiber interfaces requires precise physical alignment to keep optical insertion loss and back-reflection within acceptable margins. Traditional manual alignment benches cannot scale to mass production. SENKO’s detachable multi-fiber connectors enable Advantest’s ATE handlers to perform repeated, robotic blind-mate insertions, mechanically engaging photonic integrated circuits (PICs) without damaging facet micro-lenses or edge couplers.
2. **Signal Integrity and Electro-Optic Parametric Testing:** At 200 Gbps per lane, optical channels exhibit high sensitivity to transmitter dispersion eye closure quaternary (TDECQ) degradation and jitter. The VIAVI test engine integrated into the ATE environment conducts parallel electro-optic characterization—including real-time bit error rate (BER) sweeps and spectral eye analysis—across multiple optical lanes simultaneously, validating performance before the co-packaged module is assembled into a server switch tray.
3. **Thermal Management on High-Power Test Sockets:** During Insertion 4 module testing, the combined compute/switch silicon and surrounding optical engines dissipate significant thermal power within a confined socket footprint. The ATE socket architecture requires active thermal control (ATC) to dissipate hundreds of watts while maintaining stable junction temperatures, preventing temperature fluctuations from shifting laser emission wavelengths or distorting silicon photonic modulator bias points during high-speed parametric sweeps.

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem:** Solves a foundational manufacturing barrier for CPO, establishing an automated ATE socket methodology that allows OSATs and foundries to execute 100% functional testing of co-packaged optical modules.
- **Business & Market:** Reduces manufacturing yield risk for hyperscale switch and accelerator developers; standardized Insertion 4 testing prevents defective optical engines from causing full assembly discards at the system level.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware:** Accelerates the commercial roadmap toward 1.6T and 3.2T optical fabrics, enabling high-density datacenter interconnects with lower power consumption than legacy retimed copper or DSP-heavy pluggables.
- **Investment Implication:** Positive for semiconductor ATE leaders (Advantest), precision optical connector manufacturers (SENKO), and photonic test instrumentation specialists (VIAVI); monitors transition timing from pluggable transceivers to CPO.

Key specifications:

- Consortium Partners: SENKO Advanced Components, Advantest Corporation, VIAVI Solutions
- Test Stage Focus: Insertion 4 high-volume module-level final functional testing
- Signaling Rate Target: 200 Gbps per lane PAM4 modulation (~100 Gbaud)
- Aggregate Optical Engine Bandwidth: 1.6 Tbps aggregate per optical engine
- Interconnect Interface: Detachable Very Small Form Factor (VSFF) multi-fiber connector (SN-MT architecture)
- Functional Test Capabilities: Parallel multi-channel Bit Error Rate (BER) analysis, eye diagram characterization, and optical parametric validation

Operating automated optical handlers on high-volume factory floors will require integrated automated air-cleaning mechanisms and strict cleanroom contamination controls, adding maintenance complexity for packaging subcontractors.

### 4. TSMC Outlines 2027 Advanced Node Roadmap and Targets Doubling CoWoS Packaging Capacity to 260,000 Wafers by 2028 (Category 6)

**Source:** Economic Daily News / TrendForce / Wccftech / Seoul Economic Daily  
**Event Date:** 2026-09-14  
**Source Date:** 2026-09-14, 10:24 CST (2026-09-14, 11:24 KST)  
**Status:** Foundry Supply-Chain Disclosure & Advanced Packaging Capacity Roadmap  
**Primary URL:** https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/09/14/tsmc-targets-2nm-3nm-capacity-boost-cowos-double-2028/

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? Leading-edge AI accelerators—including multi-die training processors and custom hyperscale ASICs—have surpassed the physical reticle limit of optical lithography tools (~858 square millimeters), requiring multi-die partitioning linked directly to High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) stacks. TSMC’s Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate (CoWoS) packaging has served as the critical physical foundation for this architecture. However, acute packaging interposer constraints have created an industry-wide delivery bottleneck, forcing cloud providers to ration compute deployments and capping overall AI server shipments.

On September 14, 2026, Taiwanese supply-chain reports confirmed that TSMC has officially shared its 2027 advanced node expansion roadmap with key semiconductor partners, setting aggressive targets for both front-end wafer fabrication and back-end advanced packaging. In front-end manufacturing, TSMC's 2nm capacity is projected to increase by 22%, growing from 90,000 wafers per month at the end of 2026 to 110,000 wafers per month by mid-2027. Its 3nm capacity is slated to climb from 180,000 to 210,000 wafers per month over the same timeframe, representing a cumulative 70% expansion over an 18-month span. Crucially, to resolve severe AI accelerator supply constraints, TSMC plans to double its **CoWoS advanced packaging capacity from approximately 130,000 wafers per month at the end of 2026 to 260,000 wafers per month by the end of 2028**. The expansion centers on the Chiayi AP7 advanced packaging facility in Taiwan and the expansion of packaging lines across its Arizona campus, allowing advanced chips fabricated in the United States to be packaged locally and eliminating trans-Pacific shipping bottlenecks.

System-level electrical, thermal, and packaging co-design implications:
1. **Interposer Reticle Scaling and High-Density Interconnects:** Next-generation AI accelerators demand larger interposer areas (scaling up to 3.3x to 5.5x reticle field size in CoWoS-L and CoWoS-R) to accommodate twin compute logic dies alongside 8 to 12 HBM3E or HBM4 memory stacks. Fabricating multi-reticle interposers requires precise lithographic stitching and deep-submicron redistribution layer (RDL) routing, maintaining fine line/space pitches to ensure high die-to-die bandwidth density without excessive signal attenuation.
2. **PDN Impedance and Transient Current Step Management:** As accelerator package power pushes toward 1,000W to 1,400W, core supply currents exceed 1,000 amperes at operating voltages below 0.8V. Distributing dynamic current through large interposers creates severe power delivery network (PDN) challenges. To suppress transient voltage droop during dynamic workload steps, packaging engineers are embedding high-density deep-trench capacitors (DTC) and integrated passive devices directly into the interposer substrate, minimizing vertical power loop inductance.
3. **Thermomechanical Co-Design and CTE Warpage Suppression:** Large-area multi-die packages (measuring up to 100mm x 100mm) experience severe thermomechanical stress during thermal reflow due to the mismatch in the coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE) between the silicon interposer (CTE ~2.6 ppm/°C) and the underlying organic build-up substrate (CTE ~14 ppm/°C). As packages expand toward 260,000 wafers monthly in HVM, managing warpage through balanced dielectric layers, stiffener rings, and optimized underfill materials is essential to prevent micro-bump joint cracking and ensure high assembly yields.

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem:** Relieves the primary physical bottleneck capping global AI accelerator production; doubling CoWoS wafer capacity enables hyperscalers to deploy larger multi-die GPU and ASIC architectures at scale.
- **Business & Market:** Reinforces TSMC’s comprehensive foundry and packaging ecosystem moat, while driving secondary opportunities for alternative bridge architectures (e.g., Intel EMIB-T targeting 40,000 to 45,000 wafers by 2028) and outsourced packaging providers (Amkor, ASE) capturing spillover demand.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware:** Supports the transition to 1,000W+ accelerator boards and multi-reticle packages, enabling next-generation server architectures with direct-to-chip liquid cooling and 54V power delivery.
- **Investment Implication:** Overweight semiconductor manufacturing equipment suppliers (ASML, Applied Materials) and advanced packaging assembly/substrate vendors (Unimicron, Ibiden, ASE); monitor packaging yield curves at TSMC's Chiayi AP7 and Arizona facilities.
