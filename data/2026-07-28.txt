### 1. South Korea and U.S. Tech Giants Formalize $1 Trillion Bilateral AI Infrastructure and Semiconductor Framework at San Francisco Summit

**Source:** Arirang News / Ministry of Science and ICT (MSIT) / SK hynix Newsroom / Seoul Economic Daily / Reuters

#### [Overview]
Following high-level bilateral negotiations at the San Francisco AI Summit led by South Korean President Lee Jae Myung and Silicon Valley executives, South Korean and U.S. technology leaders finalized non-binding Letters of Intent (LOIs) and Memoranda of Understanding (MOUs) outlining a historic **$1 trillion** (approx. 1,400 trillion KRW) umbrella framework for bilateral AI infrastructure and semiconductor cooperation. The strategic alliance positions South Korea to evolve from a memory export hub into an indispensable global co-developer of end-to-end AI factories, data centers, and physical robotics platforms. Under the umbrella framework, SK Group executed a **$500 billion+** strategic agreement with NVIDIA to build a 2-gigawatt NVIDIA DSX AI Cloud in Korea powered by Vera Rubin GPUs and SK hynix HBM4 memory, serving as the core anchor of SK's broader $750 billion 5-year semiconductor investment commitments. Concurrently, Samsung Electronics executed a **$200 billion** MOU through 2030 with Broadcom, encompassing High Bandwidth Memory (HBM4) supply, 2nm Gate-All-Around (GAA) logic foundry fabrication, and 2.5D advanced interposer packaging integration.

Expanding the infrastructure stack, NAVER, NVIDIA, and Brookfield Investments finalized an agreement to expand NAVER's GAK Sejong data center from 55 megawatts to 200 megawatts (deploying approximately 100,000 Vera Rubin GPUs) by 2028, supported by NAVER's HyperCLOVA X model optimization on NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra. Hyundai Motor Group unveiled its physical AI strategy, deploying 50,000 NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs for autonomous vehicle and robotics simulations. Crucially, South Korea's Ministry of Science and ICT signed a **$1.2 billion** MOU with AMD CEO Lisa Su at the "AMD Advancing AI 2026" event to establish an "AMD AI Center of Excellence" in South Korea. This center will construct an open, heterogeneous computing platform integrating AMD EPYC CPUs and Instinct GPUs with domestic South Korean NPUs, establishing a cost-effective, open-architecture alternative to single-vendor GPU clusters.

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem Perspective:** Establishes South Korea as the principal international hardware and packaging hub for Western frontier AI labs, integrating 2nm GAA logic, HBM4 memory, and gigawatt-scale AI factories.
- **Business & Market Dynamics:** Diversifies cloud supply chains by pairing proprietary NVIDIA GPU clusters with open AMD-NPU heterogeneous architectures, mitigating single-vendor capacity bottlenecks.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware Implications:** Accelerates power-grid expansion and liquid-cooling deployment to support multi-gigawatt regional AI data center capacity across East Asian compute corridors.
- **Investment Strategy:** Overweight Korean semiconductor packaging equipment manufacturers, high-voltage power-grid vendors, and heterogeneous computing compiler platforms.

### 2. NVIDIA Launches Dual Joint AI Research Labs in South Korea with KAIST ($300M Joint Program) and Seoul National University

**Source:** NVIDIA Newsroom / The Korea Herald / Chosunbiz / Seeking Alpha / StreetInsider  
**Event Date:** 2026-07-26 | **Publication Date:** 2026-07-27  
**Status:** Official Corporate R&D Execution & Academic Partnership  
**Primary URL:** https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-kaist-joint-ai-research-lab  

#### [Overview]
In a major expansion of its global research footprint, NVIDIA announced the establishment of two premier joint AI research centers in South Korea: the **NVIDIA-KAIST Joint AI Research Lab** located at the KAIST Kim Jaechul Graduate School of AI in Seoul (KAIST's dedicated AI hub in Hongneung/Yangjae, headquartered in Daejeon) and the **NVIDIA AI Technology Center (NVAITC)** at Seoul National University (SNU). The initiative is led by a **$300 million**, five-year joint program with KAIST. The valuation comprises **$250 million** in dedicated compute access ($50 million annually over 5 years provided through local NVIDIA Cloud Partners), together with **$50 million** in research grants, student fellowships, and laboratory facility commitments. The NVIDIA-KAIST lab is NVIDIA's first joint university research lab in Korea and focuses on developing agentic AI models, multi-step tool execution, and Korean language-optimized foundation models built on the NVIDIA Nemotron open-model architecture.

Led by incoming KAIST faculty member Hyunwoo Kim, the joint lab establishes an academic-to-industry pipeline by funding at least 10 KAIST researchers annually with NVIDIA internship opportunities and hiring pathways for top graduates. Simultaneously, NVIDIA's partnership with Seoul National University establishes the NVAITC to advance joint research across physical AI (utilizing NVIDIA's Cosmos world foundation model), scientific AI, foundation models, and accelerated computing. NVIDIA chief scientist Bill Dally noted that embedding NVIDIA's full-stack software expertise within Korea's leading research universities will accelerate sovereign AI model development tailored specifically to South Korea's industrial requirements.

### 4. NVIDIA Expands Agent Toolkit with PhysicsNeMo and CUDA-X Libraries for Autonomous Multiphysics Chip Design and Verification

**Source:** NVIDIA Developer Blog / StreetInsider / GlobeNewswire / Electronic Design Automation Review  
**Event Date:** 2026-07-26 | **Publication Date:** 2026-07-27  
**Status:** Official Software Release & EDA Partner Integration  
**Primary URL:** https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-agent-toolkit-physicsnemo-cuda-x/  

#### [Overview]
As semiconductor fabrication advances toward sub-2nm nodes, 3D chiplets, and multi-kilowatt power envelopes, classical Electronic Design Automation (EDA: software toolchains used to design, simulate, and verify integrated circuits) faces severe computational bottlenecks. Physical layout constraints, Signal Integrity (SI: high-speed signal propagation without distortion), Power Integrity (PI: stable voltage delivery across complex circuit blocks), and thermal stress require multi-week iterative simulation loops. Connecting autonomous AI agent reasoning directly with GPU-accelerated physics solvers is essential to reduce semiconductor tapeout timelines and improve first-pass silicon success rates.

NVIDIA announced a major expansion of its **NVIDIA Agent Toolkit for Engineering**, introducing re-architected **PhysicsNeMo** libraries and updated **CUDA-X** tools specifically designed to power agentic AI workflows across semiconductor design, chip verification, advanced packaging, and multiphysics engineering. The toolkit connects LLM-based agent reasoning with accelerated physics solvers, including `cuDSS` (direct sparse solvers), `cuISS` (iterative sparse solvers), and `cuEST` (quantum chemistry simulations). Furthermore, NVIDIA released its **Nemotron 3 Ultra** open model, which achieved state-of-the-art results in agentic register-transfer level (RTL: hardware description code defining digital circuit logic) coding on the Verilog design benchmark.

Industry software partners and semiconductor manufacturers demonstrated disaggregated production performance gains across specialized workloads. **Cadence** integrated the toolkit into its AuraStack AI platform, reporting up to **20x faster multiphysics simulation performance**. **Siemens EDA** combined NeMo Gym with its Fuse EDA AI Agent, achieving **>10x faster library characterization** and a **>10x reduction in token execution costs**. **Samsung Electronics** deployed PhysicsNeMo for chip-scale thermal-stress analysis across domains containing up to 10 billion cells, while using `cuLitho` libraries for **up to 20x faster computational lithography** (the algorithmic modification of photomasks to correct optical diffraction).

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem Perspective:** Transforms EDA toolchains from passive simulation solvers into active multi-agent environments in which AI agents autonomously parse logic, run physics simulations, and apply layout optimizations.
- **Business & Market Dynamics:** Reinforces NVIDIA's dominance in chip-design software infrastructure by embedding CUDA-X and PhysicsNeMo into major commercial EDA toolmakers (Cadence, Siemens EDA) and foundries.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware Implications:** Substantially reduces token execution costs (over 10x reduction) while accelerating time-to-tapeout for custom AI ASICs, high-speed SerDes interconnects, and 3D chiplet packages.
- **Investment Strategy:** Overweight AI-accelerated EDA toolmakers, advanced packaging inspection vendors, and custom ASIC design services utilizing agentic workflows.

#### [Technical Deep Dive]

- **Core Technical Specifications (SW/HW):** [Multiphysics Speedup]: [Up to **20x faster** via Cadence AuraStack + PhysicsNeMo] | [Lithography Acceleration]: [Up to **20x speedup** via Samsung + cuLitho] | [Library Characterization]: [**>10x faster** turnaround & **>10x token cost reduction** via Siemens Fuse] | [CUDA-X Solvers]: [cuDSS (Direct Sparse), cuISS (Iterative Sparse), cuEST (Quantum Chemistry)] | **Source:** NVIDIA Developer / Cadence / Siemens EDA / Samsung

NVIDIA's Agent Toolkit enables autonomous agent loops to query physics libraries, execute sparse solvers, and optimize layout geometries in minutes.
