Why does this matter? Under federal antitrust jurisprudence, competing commercial enterprises are strictly prohibited from entering into horizontal agreements that restrict production output, suppress technological advancement, or coordinate release schedules. Under Section 1 of the Sherman Act, concerted actions among competitors that restrain trade or artificially dampen competitive rivalry face rigorous legal scrutiny. When frontier artificial intelligence executives publicly endorse mutual restraint or call for "pacing the frontier," actions intended by research scientists as responsible ethical self-regulation can be challenged by commercial subscribers as an unlawful conspiracy to limit product innovation and preserve incumbent market structures.

Represented by trial attorney Nick Rowley, the plaintiffs—active paying subscribers to Claude Pro/Team, ChatGPT Plus/Pro, Grok Premium, and Gemini Advanced—allege that the leading artificial intelligence developers engaged in an unlawful horizontal conspiracy to restrict technological development velocity. ### 4. MACOM Demonstrates 448 Gbps/Lane 3.2T Optical Silicon, 224 Gbps 1.6T Active Copper, and PCIe 7.0 Physical Layer ICs Ahead of ECOC 2026 (Category 6)

**Source:** MACOM Technology Solutions / GlobeNewswire / Lightwave / Converge Digest  
**Event Date:** 2026-09-15  
**Source Date:** 2026-09-15, 08:15 EDT (21:15 KST)  
**Status:** Commercial Semiconductor Architecture Demonstration & Product Line Unveiling  
**Primary URL:** https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/15/3150821/0/en/MACOM-Demonstrates-Technologies-Enabling-the-Future-of-High-Speed-Connectivity-at-ECOC-2026.html

#### [Overview]
Why does this matter? In massive distributed artificial intelligence training clusters, physical networking interconnects represent the binding constraint governing scale-up compute efficiency. As next-generation accelerator architectures transition from 100 Gbps to 200 Gbps SerDes per lane—and pathfind toward 400 Gbps SerDes—the high-frequency electrical loss of copper traces across conventional printed circuit boards (PCBs) becomes prohibitive. At high baud rates, dielectric loss and conductor surface roughness cause electrical channel attenuation to scale non-linearly. Passive Direct Attach Copper (DAC) cables are physically constrained to reaches below one meter at 224 Gbps per lane, leaving system architects unable to link accelerator trays across adjacent racks without incurring severe power penalties from digital signal processors (DSPs).

On September 15, 2026, analog and optoelectronic semiconductor manufacturer MACOM Technology Solutions Inc. announced working demonstration silicon for next-generation AI datacenter connectivity prepared for the European Conference on Optical Communication (ECOC 2026) in Málaga, Spain (Stand 1055). Directly addressing electrical channel insertion loss, MACOM introduced silicon solutions across three primary high-speed architectural domains: (1) **3.2 Tbps aggregate Optical Transceiver physical-layer ICs utilizing 448 Gbps per lane signaling** (operating at 224 GBaud PAM4, generating a raw line rate of 3.584 Tbps across 8 lanes to support a 3.2 Tbps payload after FEC and line encoding), comprising monolithic modulator drivers on the transmit side, wideband Transimpedance Amplifiers (TIAs) and high-responsivity photodetectors on the receive side; (2) **1.6 Tbps Active Copper Cable (ACC) equalizers operating at 224 Gbps per lane** (112 GBaud PAM4, achieving a 1.792 Tbps raw line rate across 8 lanes), utilizing analog linear equalizers embedded within cable backshells to compensate for channel loss and inter-symbol interference, thereby extending copper reach up to 2.5 to 3.0 meters while consuming less than 20% of the electrical power required by retimed DSP-based cables; and (3) high-speed fabric physical-layer ICs supporting **Optical PCIe 6.0 (64 GT/s PAM4)** for disaggregated accelerator memory pooling and **Copper PCIe 7.0 (128 GT/s PAM4)** for board-level low-latency interconnects.

System-level electrical, thermal, and packaging co-design implications:
1. **Signal Integrity (SI) and Nyquist Frequency Scaling:** In PAM4 modulation (2 bits per symbol), the fundamental Nyquist frequency equals half the baud rate:
   - For 224 Gbps PAM4 (112 GBaud), the baseband Nyquist frequency is exactly **56 GHz**, with a symbol duration (Unit Interval, UI) of approximately **8.93 picoseconds**.
   - For 448 Gbps PAM4 (224 GBaud), the baseband Nyquist frequency reaches **112 GHz**, shrinking the symbol duration to approximately **4.46 picoseconds**.  
   At 56 GHz and 112 GHz, conventional PCB materials (such as standard FR-4) exhibit intolerable attenuation exceeding -3 dB to -5 dB per inch. System designs must transition to ultra-low-loss dielectric laminates (e.g., Megtron 8 or PTFE-based materials) paired with ultra-smooth HVLP copper foil. By placing MACOM's 224G analog equalizers directly inside the cable backshells (ACC), system architects can compensate for high-frequency attenuation and inter-symbol interference (ISI) over extended reach without requiring power-intensive digital retimers across the motherboard.
2. **Power Integrity (PI) and Linear Drive Architectures:** Retimed optical DSPs typically consume 8 to 12 watts in an 800G module and 20 to 25 watts in a 1.6T module, causing multi-terabit switch chassis to dissipate several kilowatts purely on optical interfaces. MACOM’s linear direct-drive modulator drivers and TIAs interface directly with host SerDes without an internal transceiver DSP. This linear approach reduces optical module power dissipation by up to 60%, drastically alleviating thermal loads and reducing transient voltage droop across the rack Power Delivery Network (PDN).
3. **Optoelectronic Thermal Constraints and Packaging:** Operating high-speed lasers and photodetectors alongside compute packages drawing 800W to 1,200W requires strict thermal decoupling. Optical performance parameters—such as Relative Intensity Noise (RIN), Transmitter and Dispersion Eye Closure Quaternary (TDECQ), and photodetector dark current—degrade sharply at elevated temperatures. Packaging engineers must isolate optoelectronic engines onto separate thermal domains, utilizing dedicated vapor chambers and direct-to-die liquid cold plates to maintain optical junction temperatures below 70 degrees Celsius while silicon ASIC junctions operate up to 95 degrees Celsius.

```
High-Speed AI Interconnect Physical Layer Architecture:
In-Rack Scale-Up Fabric (<2.5m Reach):
[Host 112 GBaud SerDes] ──> [MACOM 224G Linear Equalizer IC in Cable Plug] ──> [1.6T Active Copper Cable (ACC)]
  - Baseband Nyquist: 56 GHz | UI: ~8.93 ps
  - Compensates for high-frequency channel loss & ISI; consumes <20% of DSP power.

Inter-Rack Scale-Out Fabric (>2.5m Reach):
[Host 224 GBaud SerDes] ──> [MACOM 448G/Lane Modulator Drivers (TX)] ──> [Optical Modulator Engine]
                                                                                │ (Single-Mode Fiber)
[Host SerDes / ASIC]   <── [MACOM 448G/Lane High-Bandwidth TIAs (RX)] <── [Photodetector Array]
  - Baseband Nyquist: 112 GHz | UI: ~4.46 ps | 8-Lane Line Rate: 3.584 Tbps (3.2T Payload)
```

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem:** Validates the physical feasibility of 224 GBaud (448 Gbps/lane) signaling for 3.2T optical transceivers, bridging the architectural gap between current 100G/200G SerDes deployments and future multi-terabit scale-out topologies.

#### [Critical View]
Operating physical optical channels at 224 GBaud (448 Gbps per lane) pushes analog semiconductor design to fundamental physical boundaries. At a symbol duration of approximately 4.46 picoseconds, timing jitter, chromatic dispersion penalty, and high-frequency noise closure significantly erode signal-to-noise ratio margins. In intensity-modulation direct-detection (IM-DD) architectures, laser Relative Intensity Noise (RIN) and thermal dissipation directly constrain receiver sensitivity. Furthermore, while Active Copper Cables (ACC) extend reach at 224 Gbps per lane, analog linear equalization cannot fully cancel deep reflection notches caused by imperfect PCB connector-to-board via transitions. Datacenter operators adopting 224G copper backplanes must enforce strict fabrication tolerances on back-drilling, via stub reduction, and trace geometry, potentially limiting initial yields across high-density AI server carrier boards.
