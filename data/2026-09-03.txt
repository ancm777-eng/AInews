### 3. U.S. Department of Justice Files Statement of Interest in OpenAI Copyright Litigation, Urging Court to Treat AI Model Training as Transformative Fair Use

**Source:** U.S. Department of Justice / U.S. District Court (SDNY) / The Washington Post / Reuters

#### [Overview]
Why this federal legal filing matters: The central legal uncertainty confronting foundation model developers has been mass copyright infringement litigation brought by news publishers, authors, and media conglomerates. If federal courts determine that ingesting publicly accessible internet data to train neural networks constitutes copyright infringement requiring prior licenses, domestic AI developers would face massive statutory damages and mandatory licensing tolls that could deter pre-training compute investment and entrench an anti-competitive oligopoly accessible only to hyperscalers with deep licensing balance sheets.

On September 2, 2026, the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) entered a formal **Statement of Interest** in Manhattan federal court (*In re OpenAI, Inc. Copyright Infringement Litigation*, Case No. 25-md-3143, U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York). Filed pursuant to **28 U.S.C. Section 517**—which authorizes the DOJ to attend to the interests of the United States in pending private litigation without formally intervening as a co-party under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 24—the government urged presiding U.S. District Judge Sidney Stein to reject the publishers' claims and find that copying copyrighted written works for the purpose of training large language models qualifies as **"extraordinarily transformative fair use"** under Section 107 of the Copyright Act. Signed by senior Justice Department officials including Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward and Assistant Attorney General Brett Shumate, the filing asserted that neural networks analyze statistical semantic relationships across text corpora rather than reproducing protected creative expression for expressive consumption. Crucially, the DOJ established a national security and antitrust rationale: the government argued that imposing universal copyright licensing fees would stifle domestic AI progress, hand foreign adversaries an asymmetric advantage, and erect insurmountable barriers to entry for startups.

- **Investment Implication:** Positive for foundation model developers and hyperscale compute operators; negative for digital publishing equities reliant on legal licensing mandates.

### 4. Infineon Acquires C2i Semiconductors to Commercialize Vertical Power Delivery and SIVR for Next-Gen AI Clusters (Category 6)

**Source:** Infineon Technologies AG / The Data Center Engineer / Reuters  
**Event Date:** 2026-08-28  
**Source Date:** 2026-08-28  
**Status:** Official Corporate Acquisition & Technical Architecture Roadmap Disclosure  
**Primary URL:** https://www.infineon.com/cms/en/about-infineon/press/press-releases/2026/INFXX202608-142.html  

#### [Overview]
Why this power delivery development matters: Next-generation AI accelerators, GPUs, and custom XPUs are pushing core power consumption beyond 1,000 watts per socket, operating at ultra-low core logic supply voltages (sub-0.8V) and requiring transient currents approaching or exceeding 1,000 to 1,500 amperes. In conventional lateral power delivery architectures, voltage regulator modules (VRMs) are placed on the motherboard adjacent to the processor socket. Routing thousands of amperes laterally through centimeters of PCB copper traces creates severe resistive transmission losses (governed by I squared times R dissipation), massive thermal hot spots near the socket perimeter, and high power distribution network (PDN) loop inductance that induces unacceptable transient voltage droop during dynamic computational bursts.

On August 28, 2026, Infineon Technologies AG announced the acquisition of Bengaluru-based startup **C2i Semiconductors**, a specialist in software-defined multiphase digital controllers, smart power stages, and **Substrate Integrated Voltage Regulators (SIVR)**. The transaction is engineered to accelerate Infineon's roadmap for **grid-to-core Vertical Power Delivery (VPD)** across high-performance AI datacenters. Vertical power delivery reconfigures the physical architecture of the accelerator board: instead of mounting VRMs laterally around the chip, power stages and voltage regulators are placed directly underneath the compute die on the backside of the substrate or carrier board. This reduces the power delivery path length from tens of millimeters to less than one millimeter, slashing PDN impedance, eliminating lateral voltage drop, and freeing up 100% of the die perimeter for high-speed SerDes, UCIe, and HBM memory interfaces. By incorporating C2i's software-defined digital multiphase algorithms, Infineon enables real-time dynamic phase shedding and predictive non-linear transient response tuning, stabilizing the core supply rail within tight sub-5% voltage margins during steep di/dt current steps.

System-level implications across package, board, and rack engineering:
1. **Package Substrate & SIVR Integration:** Placing integrated voltage regulators within or directly beneath the package substrate requires advanced embedding techniques and ultra-dense micro-via arrays to route high-current supply rails directly to core logic power pins, bypassing horizontal substrate traces.
2. **PCB Stack-Up & Board Signal Integrity (SI):** Lateral power delivery traditionally demands 6 to 10 thick copper plane layers (2 oz or 3 oz copper) within the server motherboard solely to carry high currents without overheating. Migrating to vertical power delivery eliminates horizontal current crowding, allowing board designers to reduce PCB layer counts from 28+ layers down to 18–20 layers and dedicate outer layers to high-speed differential pairs (PCIe Gen 6 / 112G PAM4) with minimized via stubs and lower insertion loss.
3. **Thermal-Electrical Co-Design:** While vertical power delivery eliminates lateral motherboard resistive losses, it concentrates thermal dissipation directly beneath the accelerator socket. Dissipating heat from both the compute logic (top side) and the vertical power stages (bottom side) mandates dual-sided direct-to-chip liquid cooling cold plates, driving data center mechanical racks toward closed-loop liquid distribution manifolds.

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem:** Accelerates the industry-wide migration from lateral VRMs to integrated vertical power delivery (VPD), establishing software-defined digital control as an indispensable requirement for sub-0.8V AI accelerator power integrity.
- **Business & Market:** Solidifies Infineon’s competitive positioning against Monolithic Power Systems (MPS), Texas Instruments, and Vicor in hyperscale AI server power delivery by pairing C2i's digital controller IP with Infineon's silicon, SiC, and GaN power switches.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware:** Unlocks higher compute density per server tray by recovering board real estate around the processor socket, allowing denser accelerator packing and reducing motherboard I squared times R power losses by up to 15%.
- **Investment Implication:** Overweight advanced power semiconductor manufacturers and specialized server thermal/liquid cooling suppliers; monitor board redesign cycles across server ODMs transitioning to vertical power topologies.

#### [Technical Deep Dive]

- **Core Technical Specifications:**
  - Acquired Entity: C2i Semiconductors (Bengaluru, India; founded specializing in digital power IP)
  - Target Architectural Domain: Grid-to-core Vertical Power Delivery (VPD) & Substrate Integrated Voltage Regulators (SIVR)
  - Power Density Target: Core logic delivery exceeding 1,000A to 1,500A at sub-0.8V operating rails
  - Power Delivery Network (PDN) Optimization: Reduces physical interconnect distance between regulator and core logic from >30mm to <1mm, slashing PDN parasitic loop inductance and I squared times R losses
  - Control Methodology: Software-defined multiphase digital control with adaptive phase shedding and predictive non-linear transient response
  - Semiconductor Material Synergy: Integration of C2i digital controllers with Infineon power stages across Silicon MOSFET, Silicon Carbide (SiC), and Gallium Nitride (GaN) technologies
  - Deployment Status: Commercial IP acquisition completed; technical integration into Infineon AI power roadmap ongoing; reference board sampling targeted for 2027.
  - Primary Source: Infineon Technologies Corporate Announcement / The Data Center Engineer (2026-08-28)

### [Summary & Forward-Looking Outlook]

#### Executive Synthesis
The structural developments analyzed across this reporting cycle illustrate how foundation model software scaling, custom accelerator economics, federal legal policy, and power delivery hardware engineering are converging into an integrated architectural feedback loop:

```
[Agentic Token Scaling]           -->   [Hyperscaler Custom XPUs]       -->   [Federal Fair Use Advocacy]
(Gemini 3.8 Flash & $0.24-$0.58 Pareto) (Broadcom Q3 AI Semi: $16.7B)         (DOJ Statement in NYT v. OpenAI)
                                                        |
                                                        v
[Rack Density & Board Layout]     <--   [Vertical Power Delivery (VPD)] <--   [Core Power Integrity Limits]
(Double-Sided Liquid Cooling)           (Infineon Acquires C2i / SIVR)        (1,000A+ at Sub-0.8V Rails)
```

1. **What changed today:** Google DeepMind launched Gemini 3.8 Flash to establish a cost-to-intelligence Pareto benchmark ($0.24 to $0.58 per task) for high-frequency agentic loops; Broadcom reported a 221% surge in AI chip revenue to $16.7 billion, confirming explosive hyperscaler demand for custom ASICs; the U.S. DOJ formally urged a federal court to designate LLM pre-training as transformative fair use on national security grounds; and Infineon detailed its roadmap for vertical power delivery and substrate-integrated regulators following its acquisition of C2i Semiconductors.
2. **Structural trend strengthening:** The bifurcation of AI silicon between merchant GPUs and hyperscaler-customized ASICs (XPUs). As agentic token volume scales exponentially, hyperscalers are using custom silicon co-developed with Broadcom to optimize cost and escape commercial markups.
3. **Bottleneck emerging:** Power delivery network (PDN) impedance and transient di/dt voltage droop at the chip socket. Delivering 1,000A+ at sub-0.8V rails laterally is reaching physical limits, forcing the adoption of vertical power delivery and direct-to-chip dual-sided liquid cooling.
4. **Layer becoming strategically more important:** Substrate-integrated power management (PMIC/SIVR) and high-density packaging interconnects (UCIe and Tomahawk Ethernet switching), which dictate the physical scaling limits of both silicon and rack-level systems.
5. **What should be monitored next:** The Southern District of New York's response to the DOJ Statement of Interest, customer tape-out schedules for Broadcom's next-generation 3nm/2nm custom XPUs, and the initial commercial deployment of vertical power delivery boards in OCP reference designs.
