### 3. Xiaomi Open-Sources Embodied AI Foundation Model Xiaomi-Robotics-1 with 100K-Hour UMI Trajectory Stack

**Source:** TechNode / RuntimeWire / GitHub / Open Source For You  
**Event Date:** 2026-08-06 | **Publication Date:** 2026-08-06  
**Status:** Open-Source Code, Weights, & Benchmark Release  
**Primary URL:** https://github.com/Xiaomi/Xiaomi-Robotics-1  

#### [Overview]
On August 6, 2026, Xiaomi officially open-sourced the complete codebase, model weights, and deployment pipeline for **Xiaomi-Robotics-1**, a 5-billion-parameter Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation model engineered for general-purpose robotic manipulation. Built to lower the engineering barrier for physical AI, the model addresses data scarcity by utilizing a two-stage training architecture. In the pretraining phase, Xiaomi-Robotics-1 was trained on over 100,000 hours of Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) handheld demonstration trajectories spanning 1,700+ scenarios across household, commercial, and industrial environments. In the post-training alignment phase, the model was fine-tuned on over 10,000 hours of cross-embodiment real-robot data (including 7,200 hours of household robot execution). Architecturally, the model couples a Qwen3-VL backbone with a Diffusion-Transformer (DiT) via a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) setup, enabling real-time action generation optimized for consumer GPUs. By releasing the full pipeline under an Apache license, Xiaomi directly challenges proprietary embodied AI platforms by offering an accessible, reproducible blueprint for cross-hardware robot deployment.

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Business & Market Dynamics:** Pressures proprietary embodied AI developers (Figure AI, Tesla Optimus) by democratizing a 5B parameter manipulation policy and evaluation benchmark.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware Implications:** Drives demand for edge inference compute modules and low-latency motor controllers capable of running asynchronous Diffusion-Transformer action loops.
- **Investment Strategy:** Overweight edge AI inference silicon, handheld data collection hardware frameworks, and open-source robotics tooling platforms.

### 4. Intel Foundry Details 240x240 mm Hyper-Large Form Factor (HLFF) Package Architecture with EMIB-T Silicon Bridges at IEEE ECTC

**Source:** Semiconductor Engineering / Intel Foundry Technical Disclosure / IEEE ECTC  
**Event Date:** 2026-08-04 | **Publication Date:** 2026-08-04  
**Status:** Peer-Reviewed Engineering Roadmap & IEEE ECTC Technical Disclosure  
**Primary URL:** https://semiengineering.com/from-blueprint-to-build-engineering-the-worlds-largest-ai-chips/  

#### [Overview]
*Why this research is important:* As multi-billion parameter foundation models scale context windows and execution heads, single-reticle (~858 mm²) silicon logic dies reach physical manufacturing limits. Integrating compute die arrays, High Bandwidth Memory (HBM4/4e), and high-speed I/O tiles requires expanding package substrates far beyond standard reticle sizes. However, scaling substrate dimensions introduces severe encapsulation mold constraints, thermal warpage (bending under operational heat), and signal degradation across inter-chiplet connections. Overcoming these physical bottlenecks requires transitioning to **Hyper-Large Form Factor (HLFF) packages** connected via localized embedded silicon bridges.

At the IEEE Electronic Components and Technology Conference (ECTC), Intel Foundry published an architectural blueprint and manufacturing process for **Hyper-Large Form Factor (HLFF) organic packages measuring up to 240 mm x 240 mm**. Calculating total package footprint area:

240 mm x 240 mm = 57,600 mm²

57,600 mm² / 858 mm² (single lithography reticle) = **~67.1-fold expansion in package substrate area**

Rather than using a full, monolithic 2.5D silicon interposer—which suffers from severe reticle sizing caps and high manufacturing costs—Intel embeds localized **EMIB-T (Embedded Multi-die Interconnect Bridge with Through-Silicon Vias)** dies directly within organic substrates. This enables fine-pitch micro-bump interconnects (down to 25 µm pitch) between compute dies and memory stacks. To handle thermal expansion stresses across a 57,600 mm² package, Intel developed low-warpage encapsulation mold compounds, enabling multi-kilowatt power delivery for next-generation data center AI accelerators.

*Impact on board and package layout designers:* For semiconductor package layout and high-speed PCB engineers, 240x240 mm HLFF organic substrates eliminate traditional reticle-size area caps while requiring multi-tier Power Delivery Networks (PDN) capable of managing thousands of amperes. Designers must handle micro-bump bridge routing at sub-25 µm pitches, account for localized Coefficient of Thermal Expansion (CTE) mismatch gradients across 57,600 mm², and integrate liquid-cooling cold plates directly onto the hyper-large package lid.

#### [Strategic Impact]

- **Technology & Ecosystem Perspective:** Establishes organic substrates with embedded EMIB-T silicon bridges as a scalable alternative to monolithic 2.5D silicon interposers, bypassing CoWoS reticle boundaries.
- **Business & Market Dynamics:** Positions Intel Foundry as an advanced packaging supplier for cloud service providers building multi-die custom AI accelerators.
- **Infrastructure & Hardware Implications:** Enables single-package AI compute platforms with terabytes-per-second die-to-die bandwidth and multi-kilowatt power delivery interfaces.
- **Investment Strategy:** Overweight high-density organic substrate suppliers, advanced encapsulation mold compound makers, and EMIB-T packaging equipment providers.

#### [Technical Deep Dive]

- **Core Technical Specifications (SW/HW):** [Package Footprint]: Up to 240 mm x 240 mm (57,600 mm²) | [Substrate Footprint Expansion]: ~67.1x expansion over single reticle area (~858 mm²) | [Interconnect Bridge]: EMIB-T (Embedded Multi-die Interconnect Bridge with TSVs) | [First-Layer Bump Pitch]: Scaled down to 25 µm micro-bump pitch | **Source:** Intel Foundry / IEEE ECTC / Semiconductor Engineering
- **Comparative Analysis:** Standard 2.5D packaging relies on full-area silicon interposers that are physically constrained by wafer reticle limits (~858 mm² to ~2,700 mm²). Intel's HLFF platform uses a 57,600 mm² organic substrate with localized EMIB-T silicon bridges, expanding total package area by ~67.1x compared to a single reticle while avoiding full silicon interposer costs.
- **Release / Deployment Stage:** [Process validation and encapsulation research published at IEEE ECTC; qualification ongoing for custom cloud accelerators]

- Layer Order (Top to Bottom) | Component / Layer Name | Architectural Role & Technical Specification
- Layer 1 (Top Silicon Dies) | Heterogeneous AI Logic & HBM4/4e Stacks | High-density compute chiplets connected via sub-25 µm micro-bumps
- Layer 2 (Encapsulation Compound) | Low-Warpage Encapsulation Material | Advanced compound engineered to prevent thermal warping across 57,600 mm²

#### [Critical View]
While 240x240 mm organic substrates significantly expand package integration area, managing thermal dissipation across a multi-kilowatt package presents severe mechanical challenges. Thermal expansion gradients across 57,600 mm² can induce mechanical stress, micro-bump cracking, or substrate warping if liquid-cooling cold plates fail to maintain uniform surface clamping pressure.
